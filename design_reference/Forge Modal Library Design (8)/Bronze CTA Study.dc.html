Turn 2 · Dialing the brightness down Less lit. More cast. Three sources of the brightness, turned down independently: the top rim (0.42 cream is the loudest single value in the button), the label (bronze-bright #CDA063 is the pressed-state hue being used at rest), and the fill's own value. Cheesy comes from a hot rim on a light body — luxury comes from a dark body with one restrained edge.
2a Restrained forged Recommended
1b's exact fill and sides. Rim cut 0.42 → 0.20, forge glow removed entirely, border eased, label dropped from bronze-bright to bronze-primary. Still unmistakably curved metal — just no longer gleaming.
2b Oxidized
The fill itself walked down about 12% in value and slightly desaturated — aged bronze that has been handled, not cast yesterday. Rim at 0.16. Reads expensive and quiet; the risk is it starts to lose its read as the primary action.
2c Anthracite bronze
Almost black, warm only in the shadows — bronze survives as the rim and the type. The most restrained thing that is still recognisably metal. Very close to 1d in feel, but warm rather than cold, so it stays in the family.
Isolating the culprit — top rim only Same fill, same border, same label in all four. Only the 1px cream highlight along the top edge changes. This is the dial I'd actually turn — pick a number and I'll set the token.

Turn 1 · Primary CTA material Is the bronze luxury, or is it dull? Four treatments of the same button, at the two scales it actually ships at — the fixed commit bar (15px / 15px padding) and the in-card action (13.5px / 11px padding). Same hue in all four. The variable is the material model: how light falls, where the rims are, and whether the label sits on the metal or is cut into it.
1a Current — shipping today
The sanctioned dark forged fill, but with the generic bronze border and a soft outer glow and none of the metal rims. No top highlight, no side falloff — so the face reads as a flat dark-brown slab that has sunk into the card rather than metal catching light.
In-card scale
1b Full forged — the DS spec, unused Recommended
Identical fill to 1a plus the four treatments the foundation already defines and nobody applies: polished top rim, reflected bottom rim, dark side falloff, brushed grain, metal border, tight forge glow. The face now curves. Same hex values, radically more material.
In-card scale
1c Polished plaque — engraved label
The bright brushed sweep as a full fill with near-black ink cut into it. Maximum presence and unmistakably metal — but the foundation explicitly forbids this sweep as a large fill, and at 404px wide it starts to read trophy rather than tool.
In-card scale
1d Iron plate — bronze reserved for type
The austere answer: cold iron face, one bronze hairline catching light on the top edge, bronze only in the label and icon. Bronze stays scarce and therefore precious — but the primary action loses its weight advantage over every secondary button in the app.
In-card scale
