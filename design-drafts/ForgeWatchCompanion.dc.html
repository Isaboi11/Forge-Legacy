Forge Legacy on the Wrist

Four states and two beats, one enum, two themes. The watch is a remote for the phone, not a second logger — it shows the set you are on, takes one tap to finish it, and runs the rest countdown where you can feel it. Every number on these artboards is a projection of state the phone already holds.

The four states

One WatchState enum drives all four. The phone pushes state; the watch renders it and sends back three commands. Nothing on the wrist has its own copy of the truth.

The prose beside each artboard is annotation and never reaches the device. What ships is only what is inside the case: a label, a name, a number, a bar row, a button. If a sentence here cannot be traced to a pixel on the left, it is a note to the person building it — not copy.

02 — Active

Active Phase 3

One target, one button. The set counter leads because it is the thing you look down to check mid-set; the exercise name confirms it. The bar row under the target is the set you are on within the exercise — earned bronze behind you, track ahead.

Exercise
exercises[i].name — two lines, then truncate. Never abbreviate a lift name
Target
The phone sends the display string from displayWeight() + unitLabel. The watch never converts units
Set done
Sends setDone{exerciseIndex, setIndex}. The phone logs the planned weight and reps, starts rest, and replies with the resulting state
Double tap
The phone ignores a setDone for a set already done and replies with state. Exactly one set is logged
Unreachable
Button reads Phone not reachable and does nothing. It never queues a log it cannot verify — a set that lands twice is worse than a tap you repeat
The unit stays
Review proposed 185 × 8. Held at 185 lb × 8 — the unit is set-and-forget on the phone, so the wrist is the one place a kg/lb mix-up would go unnoticed until it is under a bar. It is already the quietest thing on the screen: grey, and two-thirds the size of the number
The name stays whole
Review proposed Bench Press. Held at the catalogue name — "Barbell" and "Dumbbell" are not padding, they are which implement to pick up, and this app has 700+ exercises where that prefix is the only difference between two of them

03 — Rest

Rest Phase 3

The one screen that earns the watch on its own. The ring is the whole reason not to pull the phone out of your pocket between sets, and the haptic is the reason not to watch the ring.

Countdown
Computed from restEndsAt (epoch ms) against the watch's own clock — no ticking messages, so it keeps counting out of Bluetooth range
Ring
Bronze on the charcoal track, sweeping down. Reduce Motion gets a static arc, per the existing rest-timer accessibility rule
At zero
WKInterfaceDevice.play(.notification) — the wrist tap fires independently of the phone's ding, so neither one waits on the other
Controls
restAdjust(−15) · restSkip() · restAdjust(+15). The Digital Crown also adjusts in 15 s steps
NEXT
Revised in review. Was a superset-only "next: Row" line. It now carries the upcoming exercise and its target on every rest — the thing you are about to walk back and do. Scope addition beyond plan §1, and worth it: it turns rest from a countdown into a briefing
Supersets
Handled by the same block. On a superset the NEXT exercise simply differs from the one you just finished — no special case, no second layout

04 — Finished

Finished Phase 3

A receipt, not a ceremony. Sealing a session stays on the phone — that moment has weight and a 45 mm screen is the wrong place to spend it. The wrist confirms the work and hands you back.

Shown when
Every set is done, or the athlete ended the session on the phone
Numbers
Elapsed from startedAt; set count from the session projection. Both already exist
The button
Deliberately inert — a label, not an action. Styled as a ghost so it never invites a tap that cannot work
Falls back
To Idle within seconds if the phone app is killed

The moments between

The review asked for Set Complete and Exercise Complete in the flow. Neither should be a state. A state is something you land on and have to leave — and mid-workout, a screen you must dismiss costs a tap you did not agree to. Both are beats: one plays and passes on its own, the other is a header change on a screen you were already looking at.

Beat A — Set logged

Set logged Phase 3

Roughly 600 ms between the tap and the rest ring. It exists for one reason: to close the loop on a tap you made without looking, so you know it landed before you walk away from the bar.

Trigger
The phone's reply to setDone — never the tap itself. What you see confirms what was written, not what was sent
Unreachable
The beat does not play. You get the Active screen with "Phone not reachable" instead. Silence is the honest answer
Haptic
A single light tap on entry, distinct from the rest-zero notification
Reduce Motion
The mark appears without the stamp scale. Duration unchanged
Dismissable
No. It passes on its own — and a tap during it falls through to Rest, never blocks

Beat B — Exercise complete

Exercise complete Phase 3

Not a screen — the Rest screen wearing a different header. When the set you just finished was the last of its exercise, "Rest" becomes the name of what you finished, and NEXT is promoted to bronze because it now means move, not repeat.

Why not a state
Because the athlete's job is identical either way: wait, then do the next thing. A separate screen would add a dismissal to a moment that has none
Header
The finished exercise's name, sentence case at 15 pt so a long lift name still fits on one line
NEXT label
Bronze and reads Next exercise. On an ordinary rest it is grey and reads Next — the colour is the whole signal
Last exercise
There is no next, so this beat does not fire. The final Set done goes straight to Finished

How the four connect

The watch never moves itself between states. Every transition below is the phone pushing a new projection — which is what makes an unreachable phone a display problem instead of a data problem.

What it does when things go wrong

This is the part that decides whether the watch gets worn twice. Each row is a Phase 4 vetting item — the PO ticks it on a real session, on a dated binary.

SituationWhat the wrist does
Phone locked in a pocket Nothing changes. WatchConnectivity does not need the phone awake or the app foregrounded — the tap still logs.
Out of range mid-rest The ring keeps counting, because it counts against restEndsAt and the local clock. Buttons read Phone not reachable. On return, one state push resyncs and nothing double-logs.
Phone app killed Falls to Idle within seconds. Resuming on the phone re-pushes and the wrist catches up.
Set done tapped twice Exactly one set logged. The command carries its setIndex and the phone's handler is idempotent on it.
Watch rebooted mid-session Reopens to the correct set — the phone's application context is latest-wins and survives the restart.
Units switched to kg mid-session The next state push carries kg. The watch is never the thing that converts.
Clocks disagree The ring tolerates ±2 s. The phone's ding stays authoritative for sound; the wrist haptic is independent.

Tokens on the wrist

Corrected in revision 3. The first two revisions drew from src/constants/tokens.ts, which is stale — the app has not rendered that palette in months. The live values are in foundation.forge.ts and foundation.paper.ts, transcribed from the Claude Design project, and they differ on the bronze, the entire charcoal ramp, and the tertiary grey.

And the watch gets both themes. The build plan said Forge-only on the grounds that a watch is always OLED black. That was wrong on this project's own rule — layout is both themes, colour is one — and an Alabaster athlete glancing at a black wrist is the seam showing. Use the switch at the top of the page.

RoleForgeAlabasterUsed for
Ground #0C1013 #F6F2E8 Every screen. charcoal900 in both — the name is a role, not a colour.
Primary text #F0EDE8 #28231D Lift name, target, timer. cream100 — the darkest value in the light theme, deliberately.
Bronze — ornament #BA8654 #A47A3D Ring fill, set bars, the stamp mark. Not for words — 3.40:1 on Alabaster.
Bronze — text #BA8654 #88683A bronzeInk. Every small caps label — SET 3 OF 5, REST, NEXT, the clock. 4.51:1 on paper.

Track / border #24242A #CDBD9F Ring track, unfilled set bars, control outlines.
Secondary text #9E9890 #6E6860 The unit beside the weight; the Idle footnote.
Tertiary text #888282 #8B8377 The NEXT label on an ordinary rest. ⚠ Alabaster’s value is a known unfinished problem — 3.15:1, and not fixable on one line.

What this means for the shipped target. expo-target.config.js currently declares three colour sets against the stale palette — $accent #C8A97E, ForgeGround #0E0E12, ForgeMuted #765B44. Two of the three are wrong, and there is no track, no cream and no per-theme pair. That is a Phase 3 change, and it is larger than a find-and-replace: watchOS resolves light and dark from a single colour set with two appearances, so each of the eight rows above becomes one set with two values.

⚠ Separately, and outside this spec: 13 places in src/ still hardcode #C8A97E — card shadows, the toggle track, a focus glow, two difficulty scales. Those are drawing the old bronze in the shipped app today.

Both themes, side by side

The two screens you actually stare at, pinned to a theme each so they can be judged against one another rather than from memory. The switch at the top of the page moves every other artboard; these four stay put.

One thing to decide with your eyes, not the tokens. Alabaster is correct by the system and it is the right default for an athlete who chose the light app — but a cream field at arm’s length under gym lighting is a different proposition from a cream card held at reading distance, and on an always-on display it is the difference between a dim glow and a lit panel on your wrist between sets. If it reads worse to you here, the honest answer may be that the watch follows the app’s theme except during a live session. Say which and I will write it into the spec.

The line around V1

On the wrist

  • Current set and target, in the athlete's own units
  • Set done — the phone logs it, the watch asks
  • Rest countdown with a haptic at zero
  • ±15 s and Skip, by button or Digital Crown
  • What is next, with its target, on every rest — supersets included
  • A logged-set beat that confirms the tap landed
  • Idle and Finished screens that look finished

Deliberately not

  • Starting a workout from the watch — needs the whole session builder
  • Editing weight or reps on the wrist — the phone is a foot away
  • Heart rate or HealthKit — a separate route entirely
  • GPS runs from the watch — the phone stays the runner
  • A watch-face complication — cheap to add in V2, once the app exists
  • Android and Wear OS

Everything on the left is a projection of state the phone already holds, plus three commands it already handles. Everything on the right needs new domain logic on a device we cannot compile locally. That is the whole reason the line sits where it does.

The review named the principle better than the plan did, so it belongs here: the phone is the workspace, the watch is the instrument. Browsing, configuring, editing, analysing and everything social stay on the phone. The wrist answers five questions — what am I doing, what weight and reps, what set am I on, how long have I rested, what is next — and takes one answer back: done. Any future feature that does not fit one of those six lines is a phone feature that happens to be near your wrist.
