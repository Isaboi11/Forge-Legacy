Motion — can we, and should we

You already can. The question is which parts deserve it.

A site that promises “nothing posts on its own” and “we won't guilt you into training” cannot move like a slot machine without contradicting itself. Motion here should read as weight and inevitability — slow, few, one-directional. That is a brand position, not a budget limit.

How to add it — no JS

Scroll-linked motion is now pure CSS. animation-timeline: view() ties an animation to an element entering the viewport with no observer and no library, and silently does nothing where unsupported — your existing engine stays the floor.

/* progressive enhancement */
.rank-fill {
  animation: rankgrow 1 linear;
  animation-timeline: view();
  animation-range: entry 20% cover 55%;
}

Verdict on the car-rental reference: worth it for one thing only — the confidence to let a single element carry a long, slow, unhurried move. Take that. Leave the scroll-jacking, the cursor tricks and the loader. Your page already animates; adding three deliberate moments is an afternoon, and adding thirty would cost you the restraint the copy is selling.
