Band Assisted Pull-Up

Latissimus DorsiBicepsUpper Back
Resistance BandBeginnerVertical Pull

Why it matters

The Assisted Pull-Up builds strength and size in your latissimus dorsi, biceps and upper back. As a compound movement, it trains several muscles together and carries over to real-world strength and power.

How to do it

  1. Anchor the band securely, check there is no slack at the start, and expect the tension to rise through the range.
  2. Set your grip and start from a full hang or fully stretched position.
  3. Set your shoulders down away from your ears before the first rep.
  4. Drive your elbows down toward your hips.
  5. Pull until your chest reaches your hands.
  6. Lower under control back to a full stretch.

Coaching cues

  • Drive your elbows down toward your hips to lead the pull.
  • Pull your chest up to your hands rather than leading with your chin.
  • Start every rep from a full stretch at the top.
  • Drive your elbows down and back to pull from your lats.
  • Match your effort to the band — its resistance climbs toward the end of the range.

Watch out for

  • Kipping or swinging to complete the rep.

    Swinging uses momentum instead of your back and arms, so the pull does less.

    Instead Pull from a dead hang and keep your body quiet.

  • Stopping the pull the moment your chin clears your hands.

    Stopping at the chin trains a shorter range and less of your back.

    Instead Pull until your collarbone or chest reaches your hands.

  • Failing to reach a full stretch between reps.

    Without the full stretch you train a shorter range and build less.

    Instead Let your arms straighten fully and your shoulder blades rise at the top.

  • Letting the band snap you back through the last part of the range.

    Giving in to the band skips the controlled lowering where much of the work happens.

    Instead Control the return as the band’s tension pulls you back.

The detail

Tempo
Pull with intent, then lower for about two to three counts.
Breathing
Exhale as you pull up, breathe in as you lower under control.
Range of motion
Travel from a full hang to your hands at your chest every rep. Take the latissimus dorsi through its full working range each rep.
Setting up
Anchor the band securely, check there is no slack at the start, and expect the tension to rise through the range.
If you are new to it
Use assistance or a resistance band to complete full-range reps.
Once it is easy
Add a pause at the top with your chest to your hands before lowering.
