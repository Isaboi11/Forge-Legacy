Barbell Deficit Deadlift

GlutesErector SpinaeRectus AbdominisAdductors
BarbellIntermediateHinge / Hip Dominant

Why it matters

The Deficit Deadlift builds strength and size in your glutes, adductors, erector spinae and rectus abdominis. As a compound movement, it trains several muscles together and carries over to real-world strength and power.

How to do it

  1. Load the bar evenly on both sides and secure it with collars before you lift.
  2. Stand on a plate or low platform so you start below the usual position.
  3. Set your feet about hip-width with the load close to your shins.
  4. Set your back flat and take a breath into your belly before you pull.
  5. Push your hips back and let your knees bend slightly as the load lowers.
  6. Keep the load brushing your legs the whole way.
  7. Stand up by driving your hips forward until you are tall.

Coaching cues

  • The deficit adds range — only use one you can reach without your back rounding.
  • Push your hips back until you feel a stretch in your hamstrings.
  • Keep the load in contact with your legs from start to finish.
  • Finish by squeezing your glutes, not by leaning back past standing.
  • Squeeze your glutes hard at the top of each rep.

Watch out for

  • Taking a deficit so large the start position collapses.

    This takes work off the target muscle and reduces what you get from the set.

    Instead Lower the platform until you can set your back flat at the bottom.

  • Rounding the lower back as the load leaves the floor.

    A rounded back is the weakest position to lift from.

    Instead Set your back flat and lift your chest before the load moves.

  • Turning the hinge into a squat by bending the knees too early.

    Early knee bend takes the stretch and work off your hamstrings and glutes.

    Instead Send your hips backward first and let the knees follow.

  • Hyperextending and leaning back at the top.

    Leaning back past standing loads your lower back and adds nothing to the lift.

    Instead Stop when you are standing tall with your glutes squeezed.

The detail

Tempo
Lower for about two to three counts, then drive up with intent.
Breathing
Breathe in at the top, brace, hold through the lift, then exhale once you are standing.
Range of motion
Lower until you feel a hamstring stretch with a flat back — depth is limited by your hamstrings, not the floor. Take the glutes through its full working range each rep.
Setting up
Load the bar evenly on both sides and secure it with collars before you lift.
If you are new to it
Practice the hip hinge with a light load or dowel to groove the pattern first.
Once it is easy
Pause just below the knee to build control in the stretched position.

Safety

  • Reduce the load if you lose control of the movement.
  • Stop the set if you feel sharp pain.
