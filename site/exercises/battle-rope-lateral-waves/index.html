Battle Rope Lateral Waves

ForearmsFront Deltoids
Battle RopeIntermediatePower / Plyometric

Why it matters

The Battle Rope Lateral Waves builds strength and size in your forearms and front deltoids. As a compound movement, it trains several muscles together and carries over to real-world strength and power.

How to do it

  1. Anchor the rope securely and take a stable, athletic stance with tension in the line.
  2. Set a stable, athletic base with your feet under your hips.
  3. Load your hips and take a breath before the explosive rep.
  4. Be explosive on the way up, moving the load or your body as fast as possible.
  5. Absorb the landing or return softly through your hips and knees.
  6. Reset to a stable, balanced position before the next rep.

Coaching cues

  • Lead with your elbow and stop around shoulder height.
  • Be explosive on the way up and controlled on the way down.
  • Land softly on your mid-foot, absorbing through your hips and knees.
  • Reset fully between reps rather than rushing the next one.
  • Keep a firm grip and let your forearms do the work.

Watch out for

  • Swinging the weight up with a hip drive.

    This takes work off the target muscle and reduces what you get from the set.

    Instead Slow it down and let the shoulder raise the weight on its own.

  • Landing stiff-legged with a hard, loud impact.

    A stiff landing wastes the force your legs should be absorbing.

    Instead Land quietly and let your hips and knees bend to absorb the force.

  • Rushing reps and losing power output.

    Rushed reps are less explosive, and power is the whole point of the movement.

    Instead Take a full reset between reps so each one is maximally explosive.

  • Letting the knees collapse inward on landing.

    A collapsed-knee landing is a weak position to absorb force.

    Instead Land with your knees tracking over your toes and your hips back.

The detail

Tempo
Explode on the effort; land and reset under full control.
Breathing
Take a breath and brace before each rep, then exhale on the effort.
Range of motion
Complete the full jump, throw or drive, then reset before the next rep. Take the forearms through its full working range each rep.
Setting up
Anchor the rope securely and take a stable, athletic stance with tension in the line.
If you are new to it
Start with lower height or lighter load and step down between reps.
Once it is easy
Increase height, distance or load only while landings stay quiet and controlled.

Safety

  • Reduce the load if you lose control of the movement.
  • Stop the set if you feel sharp pain.
  • When you fatigue, step down rather than jumping down between reps.
