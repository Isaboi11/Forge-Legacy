Step-Up Box Jump

QuadricepsGlutesHamstringsRectus Abdominis
Plyometric BoxBeginnerSquat / Knee Dominant

Why it matters

The Step-Up Box Jump builds strength and size in your quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings and rectus abdominis. As a compound movement, it trains several muscles together and carries over to real-world strength and power.

How to do it

  1. Set a box height you can land on comfortably and make sure it is stable and won’t slide.
  2. Set the box at a height where your working thigh is about parallel with your foot on it.
  3. Stand close enough that you step up rather than forward.
  4. Place your whole foot on the box with your weight through the middle of it.
  5. Drive through the top foot and stand tall without pushing off the trailing leg.
  6. Lower under control until the trailing foot touches, then repeat.

Coaching cues

  • The trailing leg is not a helper — keep its toe light so the top leg does the work.
  • Keep the load stacked over the middle of your foot.
  • Let your knees track in line with your toes.
  • Push your knees out as you descend into the bottom.
  • Drive through your knees and feel the front of your thighs work.

Watch out for

  • Pushing off the bottom foot to get up.

    This takes work off the target muscle and reduces what you get from the set.

    Instead Lower the box until you can rise without the trailing leg driving.

  • Letting the knees cave inward under load.

    Caved knees are a weaker position and rob power from the lift.

    Instead Push your knees out toward your little toes as you descend and stand.

  • Rising hips-first so the load shifts forward onto your toes.

    Hips shooting up first tips you forward and turns the squat into a good morning.

    Instead Lead with your chest and drive your hips and shoulders up at the same rate.

  • Cutting the depth short above parallel.

    A shallow squat trains a fraction of the range and much less of your legs.

    Instead Lower until your hip crease drops below the top of your knee.

The detail

Tempo
Lower under control for about two counts, then stand with intent.
Breathing
Take a big breath at the top, hold it through the descent and drive, then exhale at the top.
Range of motion
Aim for thighs at or below parallel while keeping your heels flat on the floor. Take the quadriceps through its full working range each rep.
Setting up
Set a box height you can land on comfortably and make sure it is stable and won’t slide.
If you are new to it
Start with a box or bench behind you to learn consistent depth.
Once it is easy
Add a pause in the bottom position to build strength out of the hole.
