Bodyweight Good Morning

GlutesErector SpinaeRectus AbdominisAdductors
BodyweightIntermediateHinge / Hip Dominant

Why it matters

The Good Morning builds strength and size in your glutes, adductors, erector spinae and rectus abdominis. As a compound movement, it trains several muscles together and carries over to real-world strength and power.

How to do it

  1. Set your feet about hip-width and set a flat back before you begin.
  2. Brace your midsection before the first rep.
  3. Push your hips back and hinge your torso forward with a flat back.
  4. Feel your hamstrings stretch as your hips travel back.
  5. Drive your hips forward to stand tall and squeeze your glutes.

Coaching cues

  • Push your hips back until you feel a stretch in your hamstrings.
  • Keep your back flat from your head to your hips the whole way.
  • Finish by squeezing your glutes, not by leaning back past standing.
  • Squeeze your glutes hard at the top of each rep.
  • Own the full range of every rep before you add reps or make it harder.

Watch out for

  • Rounding the lower back as you hinge forward.

    Rounding takes the stretch off your hamstrings and loads your spine instead.

    Instead Set a flat back and lead with your chest as you hinge.

  • Bending mostly at the knees instead of the hips.

    Turning the hinge into a squat shifts the work off your hamstrings and glutes.

    Instead Send your hips backward first and keep your shins near vertical.

  • Leaning back and over-arching at the top.

    Over-arching shifts load to your spine and away from your glutes.

    Instead Stop when you are standing tall with your glutes squeezed.

The detail

Tempo
Lower for about two counts, then drive up with intent.
Breathing
Breathe in as you hinge, exhale as you stand tall.
Range of motion
Hinge until you feel a hamstring stretch with a flat back, then stand tall. Take the glutes through its full working range each rep.
If you are new to it
Practice the hinge with your hands on your hips to groove a flat back.
Once it is easy
Slow the lowering phase or add a pause at the bottom of the hinge.
