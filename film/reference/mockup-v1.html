What the film has to do

Solve the four reasons people quit, in the order they feel them.

Visitors already have a workout app, or they quit one. The film doesn't list features. It walks through the moments where other apps lose people and shows Forge Legacy handling each one. Every claim on screen is something the app does today.

Problem 1 · Logging is a chore
44.5%of people who dropped a health app said entering data took too long

Shot 2: three sets logged in three taps, with a new best caught automatically and the rest timer already running.

Problem 2 · Stuck with no plan
3 wksstuck at 225 on bench. Every lifter hits a wall like this.

Shot 3: Holt changes the actual program in seconds. The answer comes as an updated Thursday workout, not a tips article.

Problem 3 · One missed week ends it
70%median share of users gone within 100 days

Shot 4: the week is missed and nothing resets. The rank holds and the app says "Welcome back." This is the shot no competitor running streaks can copy.

Problem 4 · Numbers that mean nothing
1 yrof workouts usually ends up as a spreadsheet nobody opens

Shot 5: the year becomes chapters, honors, photos and a squad. This answers "who am I becoming", not "what did I lift".

The storyboard.

The film opens on the gap and spends the rest of its time closing it. The first line, "Most people quit their workout app by spring", sets a deadline, and a notch on the date counter marks it: day 100, "Most people quit by here." Each shot after that names one reason people quit in small grey type, strikes it through in bronze, and shows the fix. When the counter passes day 100 it reads "Still here", so the viewer sees Jordan get past the point where most people give up. Nobody has to claim it.

On timing, each line stays up long enough to read comfortably: about a third of a second per word plus a second to notice it. Each shot follows a read-then-watch rhythm. The problem line appears while the phone sits still and dimmed. It's struck through, the fix arrives, and only then does the phone light up and act it out, with a bronze outline on the part of the screen that matters. Nobody has to choose between reading and watching. That puts the full film at 34 seconds. To keep the homepage version under 30 seconds, the squad shot moved out of the film and into the section directly under the video. Squads still appear in the film as one of the Legacy cards. For ads that need 15 seconds, a shorter cut keeps the gap, the missed week and the promise.

Sound design

Felt more than heard.

The music is upbeat and moving: a 124 BPM cinematic-electronic track with a driving kick, a pumping bass and a quick arpeggio on top. Think Apple product launch or a Nike training ad, not a gym playlist. It has two drops. The first lands as the phone turns into Forge Legacy, and the second lands as Jordan passes day 100. Between them, the missed week is a breakdown where the drums fall away. The interface sounds stay subtle on top of all that: haptic-style thumps instead of beeps and air instead of whooshes, with no chimes, bells or "level up" sparkles.

What gets delivered

One shoot, five files.

Video files

  • Desktop hero: 1920 × 1080, 29 s, H.264 MP4 under 5 MB plus a smaller WebM, seamless loop
  • Phone hero: 1080 × 1920, the same film recomposed (not cropped), captions moved to the top
  • Social cut: 9:16 with the full sound mix, for Reels, TikTok and Shorts
  • 15-second cut: shots 1, 4 and 6 (the gap, the missed week, the promise), for ads where every second costs money
  • Poster frame: the new-best moment at 0:05.9, as a JPG

How it behaves on the site

  • Autoplays muted, because browsers block sound. Every line is on screen, so it works silently.
  • A small "Sound on" button in the corner. The sound design rewards anyone who taps it.
  • Shows the poster instead of playing for visitors who have reduced motion turned on.
  • Loads after the headline, so the page doesn't get slower.
  • The App Store preview is a separate edit. Apple wants mostly real in-app footage there, so the 3D shots get cut.
Claims

How the film says "here to stay" without promising what nobody can.

Your research found that "forever" and "never lose your data" fall apart the day any app has a sync bug or shuts down, and people remember Endomondo and Fitbit. The film shows permanence through product facts instead.

Say, because it's true

  • "Your rank never goes down." Rank is designed to never decrease.
  • "We never charge for your history." This is a Product DNA rule.
  • "Sealed" chapters. Chapters are permanent once sealed.
  • "One tap. Even offline." This is already on your site.
  • "A small squad that notices." Squads are small and private.

Don't say or show

  • "Forever", "never lose your data", "remembers everything"
  • Likes, follower counts, leaderboards or a public feed
  • "No streaks". The app has streaks; it just doesn't punish you for breaking one.
  • The size of the program catalogue
  • Any health or medical outcome
