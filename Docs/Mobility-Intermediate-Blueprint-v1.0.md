## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Mobility Intermediate is the **INTERMEDIATE rung of the catalog's supportive mobility practice** — for the athlete who has the mobility practice itself and is ready to deepen it. Its goal is `IMPROVE_MOBILITY` ("flexibility, joint health, movement quality" — `Program-Catalog-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.4), identical to Foundation; what changes is **content depth**. The intent is to extend the athlete into **deeper ranges of motion, more challenging variations (harder poses, more complex flows), and meaningfully longer holds**, building on a body that already tolerates foundational mobility work. Like Foundation, it remains the catalog's **supportive / prehab / recovery practice** serving all athlete types alongside their primary training — not a strength, size, or conditioning stimulus.

### Target Athlete

- **All athlete types** (PEA §5.4) — universal audience, segmented from Foundation by **experience only**.
- The athlete **already familiar with mobility patterns** (PAS §3.2 INTERMEDIATE — familiar with the primary movement patterns): has run a foundational practice, tolerates sustained holds, and is ready for deeper positions.
- Typically a **Mobility Foundation graduate** (or an equivalently experienced practitioner) seeking greater range and more demanding work — not a beginner learning the practice.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| New to structured mobility work / no assumed technique | This program assumes mobility competence and uses deeper, harder positions a beginner cannot yet hold safely | **Mobility Foundation** (Sort 23) |
| Wants a strength, hypertrophy, or conditioning training stimulus | Mobility is supportive/prehab practice, not a primary training stimulus | The relevant primary-goal family — run *alongside* this |
| Wants a maximal/advanced mobility performance ceiling beyond INTERMEDIATE | Mobility is a continuous supportive practice; the family is complete-by-design at INTERMEDIATE (no Mobility Advanced exists or is roadmapped) | — (no such rung; not a journey gap — PEA §7.1) |

### Expected Outcomes

Over the 6-week block: measurably greater range of motion across the major regions, competence in more challenging variations (deeper positions / harder poses / more complex flows than Foundation), and tolerance for meaningfully longer holds — a deepened, sustainable mobility practice with no further required rung.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Every session is **MAIN-only** — no WARM_UP, no COOL_DOWN (PAS-D9).
- Static holds prescribed in `durationSeconds`, dynamic drills in sets × reps (PAS §11.8).
- The **more-challenging-variation lever is visibly activated** (§11.8) — the authored content is demonstrably harder than Foundation's foundational basic-hold set, not the same positions for two more weeks.
- Hold durations are **meaningfully longer / deeper** than Foundation, and lengthen further across the 6 weeks (Time-Based / Model 5).
- Sessions stay within 10–30 minutes (§10.2) and 5–10 holds (§10.1); the deepest/longest holds are still not front-loaded into Week 1.

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 6 weeks; **no mandatory deload** (PAS-D7, under 7 weeks; low-intensity mobility practice carries no fatigue a deload would discharge).
- **Frequency:** 5 sessions/week — near-daily practice, identical cadence to Foundation; the cadence reflects practice consistency, not training stress.
- **Weekly structure:** 5 short MAIN-only mobility sessions per week, rotating focus across the major regions, populated with deeper/harder content than Foundation.
- **Periodization summary:** a single 6-week intermediate block on **Time-Based Progression (Model 5)** applied on **both** of §11.8's dimensions — longer hold durations across the weeks *and* more challenging variations relative to Foundation. No load or sets-volume periodization (mobility volume is holds and durations).

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). MAIN-only, like all MOBILITY programs (no WARM_UP, no COOL_DOWN — PAS-D9). The PAS §11.8 sequence is identical in *shape* to Foundation but populated with harder content:

1. **Open with global mobility or breathwork.**
2. **Progress to joint-specific work** — dynamic mobility drills (sets × reps), at greater range / more complex patterns than Foundation.
3. **End with deep, long-hold static stretches** — sustained holds (`durationSeconds`), deeper positions and longer durations than Foundation.

Sequencing rules (PAS §11.8): static stretching never precedes dynamic work; the deepest/longest holds are never front-loaded into Week 1 — range and hold-duration build across the 6 weeks. MAIN sits within 5–10 holds (§10.1); sessions stay ≤30 minutes (§10.2).

Intermediate emphasis: the athlete already owns the foundational positions, so sessions push into deeper, more demanding variations under control — the structural shape is shared with Foundation, the *content* is not.

---

## Section 4 — Progression Philosophy

**Time-Based Progression (Model 5; PAS §7.1–7.2), on both of §11.8's dimensions:** PAS §11.8 makes the level distinction explicit — "(a) increase hold durations from earlier to later weeks; (b) introduce more challenging variations in intermediate programs." Foundation deliberately used dimension (a) only, on a foundational variation set (Foundation §4). **Mobility Intermediate activates both:**

- **(a) Longer holds across weeks** — hold durations lengthen across the 6 weeks, *starting from and progressing to longer durations than Foundation* (deeper time-under-stretch).
- **(b) More challenging variations** — the §11.8 intermediate lever: deeper positions, harder poses, more complex flows, replacing Foundation's basic-hold set.

Dimension (b) is the load-bearing differentiator of the entire pair: it is the lever Foundation withholds and Intermediate activates. No RPE (mobility carries no load/intensity prescription of that kind); no external load (this family has none).

---

## Section 5 — Modality / Movement-Selection Philosophy

Selection follows PAS §11.8 on the same static + dynamic + breathwork toolkit, but chosen for **greater difficulty**: deeper positions, harder poses, and more complex flows that assume the mobility competence Foundation builds. Where Foundation favors foundational, learnable, accessible holds, Intermediate selects variations that demand more range, more control, and more time under stretch. The selection is **practice-deepening** rather than practice-establishing: the goal is greater range and more demanding work on an already-competent base.

---

## Section 6 — Recovery Role

As with Foundation, recovery is not a *periodization* concern — **the family itself is the recovery / prehab practice.** Mobility Intermediate remains low-intensity, near-daily work; it carries **no deload** (PAS-D7). Its role across the catalog is unchanged: it *is* the joint-health and movement-quality support that athletes in every other family layer onto their primary training. The deeper content raises range demand, not systemic fatigue.

## Section 7 — Predecessor / Successor Logic

- **Predecessor — Mobility Foundation (Sort 23).** Inherits an established mobility-practice habit, tolerance for sustained holds, and readiness for deeper ranges and more challenging variations — the handoff Foundation §7 specifies.
- **Successor — none. Terminal complete-by-design.** There is no Mobility Advanced in the catalog or on the §7.1 roadmap, and none is warranted: mobility is a continuous supportive practice, and an INTERMEDIATE rung is a complete, sensible endpoint. This is a genuine complete-by-design terminal (no journey gap) — distinct from the terminal-by-unbuilt-successor cases (Running Base II, Lower Body Intermediate, Home Strength Foundation) flagged elsewhere.

## Section 9 — Mobility Foundation → Mobility Intermediate Distinction

| Axis | Mobility Foundation (Sort 23) | Mobility Intermediate (Sort 24) |
|---|---|---|
| **Target athlete** | New to structured mobility (no assumed technique) | Experienced practitioner / Foundation graduate (familiar with patterns) |
| **Variation difficulty** | Foundational, accessible, basic holds | **Deeper positions, harder poses, more complex flows** (§11.8 lever) |
| **Hold depth / duration** | Foundational ranges; duration-only progression on a basic set | **Greater ROM demand; meaningfully longer holds**, progressing further |
| **Progression dimensions used** | §11.8 (a) longer holds only | §11.8 (a) longer holds **+ (b) more challenging variations** |
| **Role** | On-ramp to the practice | Deepened, terminal endpoint of the practice |

**Deliberately shared (the leanness):** goal (IMPROVE_MOBILITY), environment (HOME), frequency (5/wk), athlete type (All), progression model (Time-Based / Model 5), deload (none), MAIN-only structure. **The distinction is genuine but rests entirely on authored content depth and target-athlete experience** — the thinnest metadata separation in the 24-program catalog, justified by PAS §11.8's explicit definition of intermediate mobility programs as harder *content*, not merely more weeks.

## Section 10 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE Mobility Intermediate — the Mobility ladder SURVIVES Stage 1 governance.**

**Does Mobility Intermediate independently justify its existence?** Yes. It serves a **distinct athlete** (the experienced practitioner, not the beginner), delivers **distinct content** (the §11.8 more-challenging-variation lever plus deeper/longer holds that Foundation deliberately withholds), and occupies a **distinct role** (the deepened, terminal endpoint of the practice). Its existence does not depend on Mobility Foundation's absence — the two are the on-ramp and the deepening of a single continuous practice, separated by genuine content depth and athlete experience. All three parts of Foundation §9's falsifiable test PASS.

**The pair is RESOLVED** (subject to Stage 2 honoring this Blueprint). Mobility Foundation established the baseline; Mobility Intermediate has now passed the test against it. The catalog's leanest-differentiated pair holds — on content, the only axis available to it, grounded in PAS §11.8.

**Confidence:** High on the governance logic and the §11.8 grounding; the single residual risk is entirely a **Stage 2 authoring risk** — because content is the sole load-bearing axis, the authored holds and variations *must* realize the harder-content promise or the pair would collapse in implementation. This is flagged as the Mobility family's one watch item into Stage 2.

**Scope guard:** Architecture locked/unchanged (PEA v1.4, PAS v1.3, PCA v1.4). Mobility Stage 1 progress: **2 of 2 blueprinted** (Mobility Foundation + this). Outstanding: a **Mobility Stage 1 Review** to close the family. No Stage 2 work has begun.

**Recommended next action:** a **Mobility Stage 1 Review** (confirming both rungs, the resolved pair, and the content-as-sole-axis watch item) — completing the sixth and final family's Stage 1.
