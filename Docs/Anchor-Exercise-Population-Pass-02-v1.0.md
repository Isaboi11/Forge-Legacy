## Section 1 — Push-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Push-Up |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS, SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Push-Up is a bodyweight pressing exercise performed by lowering and raising the body using the arms while holding a straight-line plank position. It trains the chest, shoulders, and triceps using only body weight. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Push-Up builds upper-body pressing strength and core stability in a single bodyweight movement. It is the most accessible pressing exercise in the library, available in any environment without equipment. It is well suited for beginners building a strength base and for advanced athletes as a high-volume conditioning tool. It also reinforces the full-body bracing used in barbell pressing movements.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Start in a plank position with hands slightly wider than shoulder width.
2. Brace your core and keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
3. Lower your chest toward the floor by bending your elbows.
4. Keep your elbows at roughly a 45-degree angle as you lower.
5. Press through your hands to push your body back up.
6. Finish with your arms fully extended.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your core braced throughout the entire movement
> — Keep your hips in line with your shoulders and heels
> — Lower until your chest is just above the floor
> — Press evenly through both hands as you push up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips sag toward the floor as the set goes on
> — The elbows flare out wide instead of staying near 45 degrees
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full elbow extension

**Progressions:** Diamond Push-Up
**Regressions:** Incline Push-Up
**Alternatives:** Dumbbell Bench Press, Cable Chest Press

## Section 2 — Dumbbell Bench Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Dumbbell Bench Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL, BENCH |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS, SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Dumbbell Bench Press is a pressing exercise performed lying on a bench, pressing a dumbbell in each hand from chest height to full arm extension. It trains the chest, shoulders, and triceps with each arm working independently. It allows a deeper range of motion than a barbell bench press.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dumbbell Bench Press builds chest and pressing strength while addressing side-to-side strength imbalances. Each arm must work independently, which builds stabilizer strength that a barbell does not demand. It is well suited for lifters looking to add pressing variety or train around a barbell limitation. It also allows a greater stretch at the bottom of the movement than a barbell press.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie back on the bench holding a dumbbell in each hand at chest height.
2. Plant your feet flat on the floor.
3. Press both dumbbells up until your arms are fully extended.
4. Lower the dumbbells back down under control to chest height.
5. Keep your elbows at roughly a 45-degree angle throughout.
6. Repeat for the full set without locking your elbows aggressively.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep both dumbbells moving at the same pace
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together before each rep
> — Lower the dumbbells until you feel a stretch across your chest
> — Keep your feet planted and core braced throughout

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — One arm presses faster than the other through the set
> — The dumbbells drift forward over the face instead of staying over the chest
> — The lower back arches excessively off the bench

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Push-Up
**Alternatives:** Cable Chest Press, Incline Dumbbell Press

## Section 3 — Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL, BENCH |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press is a vertical pressing exercise performed seated, pressing a dumbbell in each hand from shoulder height to full overhead extension. It trains the shoulders and triceps with the bench providing back support. The seated position removes the lower-body stabilization demand of a standing press.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press builds shoulder strength and size without requiring total-body stabilization. It allows the lifter to focus entirely on the pressing muscles since the bench supports the torso. It is well suited for lifters who want to isolate shoulder pressing strength or train around a core or balance limitation. It also reinforces the same overhead pressing pattern used in standing barbell work.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit on a bench with back support, holding a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder height.
2. Brace your core and keep your back against the bench.
3. Press both dumbbells straight up until your arms are fully extended.
4. Bring the dumbbells close together at the top without touching.
5. Lower the dumbbells back down to shoulder height under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat against the bench throughout
> — Press the dumbbells in a straight line rather than out to the sides
> — Avoid locking your elbows aggressively at the top
> — Lower the dumbbells at the same pace on both sides

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back arches away from the bench during the press
> — The dumbbells drift forward in front of the head
> — Only one arm reaches full extension at the top of the rep

**Progressions:** Overhead Press
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Arnold Press, Shoulder Press Machine

## Section 4 — Landmine Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Landmine Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_DIAGONAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BARBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Landmine Press is a diagonal pressing exercise performed by pressing one end of a barbell, anchored in a landmine attachment, away from the chest at an angle. It trains the shoulders and chest through a shoulder-friendly diagonal pressing path. The fixed pivot point guides the bar along a consistent arc.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Landmine Press builds pressing strength along a path that places less stress on the shoulder joint than a straight vertical press. It is well suited for lifters managing a shoulder limitation or looking for pressing variety. It also builds core stability since the diagonal bar path challenges the trunk to resist rotation. It pairs well with both vertical and horizontal pressing work in a balanced program.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Load one end of a barbell into a landmine attachment.
2. Hold the loaded end of the bar at chest height with both hands.
3. Brace your core and stagger your stance for stability.
4. Press the bar up and away from your chest along its natural arc.
5. Extend your arms fully at the top of the press.
6. Lower the bar back to chest height under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core before each rep to resist rotation
> — Let the bar travel along its natural diagonal arc
> — Keep a stable, staggered stance throughout the set
> — Press with control rather than rushing the lockout

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso rotates to help drive the bar up
> — The bar path drifts away from its natural diagonal arc
> — The stance narrows and balance becomes unstable during the press

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Dumbbell Squeeze Press
**Alternatives:** Half-Kneeling Landmine Press, TRX Push-Up

## Section 5 — Incline Cable Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Incline Cable Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_DIAGONAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Incline Cable Press is a diagonal pressing exercise performed on an incline bench using a cable machine, pressing the handles upward and away from the chest. It trains the upper chest and shoulders through constant cable tension. The incline angle directs more of the work to the upper chest than a flat press.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Incline Cable Press builds upper-chest and shoulder strength under constant tension throughout the full range of motion. The cable resistance keeps tension on the muscle even at the top of the press, unlike free weights. It is well suited for lifters looking to add upper-chest volume to a pressing program. It also offers a lower-impact pressing option for joints that need less compressive loading.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Set an incline bench between two cable stacks set to chest height.
2. Sit back on the bench and grip a handle in each hand.
3. Brace your core and press the handles up and forward.
4. Extend your arms fully at the top of the press.
5. Bring the handles together without letting them touch.
6. Lower the handles back to chest height under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your shoulder blades pulled back into the bench
> — Press the handles along a diagonal line toward the ceiling
> — Squeeze your chest at the top of each rep
> — Control the return rather than letting the cables snap back

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The shoulders round forward off the bench during the press
> — The handles travel straight up instead of along the diagonal path
> — The range of motion shortens near the bottom of the rep

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Resistance Band Diagonal Press
**Alternatives:** Landmine Press, Dumbbell Squeeze Press

## Section 6 — Dumbbell Row

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Dumbbell Row |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Dumbbell Row is a horizontal pulling exercise performed by hinging forward and pulling a dumbbell from a hang to the torso. It trains the back, biceps, and rear shoulders. It can be performed with one arm at a time or both together.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dumbbell Row builds back thickness and pulling strength while allowing each side to work independently. It is well suited for correcting side-to-side strength imbalances that a barbell can mask. It also develops the hip-hinge control shared with deadlift-pattern lifts. It is a flexible exercise that fits strength, hypertrophy, and home-based programs alike.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding a dumbbell, hinged forward at the hips with a flat back.
2. Let the dumbbell hang with your arm fully extended.
3. Brace your core and keep your back flat.
4. Pull the dumbbell up toward your lower ribs.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blade back at the top of the pull.
6. Lower the dumbbell back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat throughout the set
> — Pull with your elbow rather than your hand
> — Keep your hips still as you pull
> — Squeeze your shoulder blade at the top of each rep

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso twists to help lift the dumbbell
> — The lower back rounds as the dumbbell gets heavier
> — The elbow drifts away from the body during the pull

**Progressions:** Single-Arm Dumbbell Row
**Regressions:** Chest-Supported Row
**Alternatives:** Seated Cable Row, Inverted Row

## Section 7 — Chest-Supported Row

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Chest-Supported Row |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL, BENCH |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Chest-Supported Row is a horizontal pulling exercise performed lying chest-down on an inclined bench, pulling dumbbells up toward the torso. It trains the back, biceps, and rear shoulders. The bench removes the need to stabilize the lower back during the pull.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Chest-Supported Row builds back strength without placing any stabilization demand on the lower back. It is well suited for lifters who want to isolate the pulling muscles or who need to manage a lower-back limitation. It also allows heavier focused pulling than a standing row because balance is not a limiting factor. It pairs well with pressing work to balance upper-body training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie chest-down on an inclined bench holding a dumbbell in each hand.
2. Let both dumbbells hang with your arms fully extended.
3. Brace your core against the bench.
4. Pull both dumbbells up toward your lower ribs.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull.
6. Lower the dumbbells back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your chest pressed into the bench throughout
> — Pull with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of each rep
> — Lower with control rather than letting the dumbbells drop

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The head lifts off the bench to help generate momentum
> — Only the arms bend without the shoulder blades engaging
> — The dumbbells swing instead of moving in a straight line

**Progressions:** Dumbbell Row
**Regressions:** Resistance Band Row
**Alternatives:** Seated Cable Row, Cable Row Machine

## Section 8 — Lat Pulldown

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Lat Pulldown |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Lat Pulldown is a vertical pulling exercise performed on a cable machine, pulling a bar down from an overhead position to the upper chest. It trains the back, biceps, and shoulders. It uses an adjustable cable stack, allowing the load to be set precisely.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Lat Pulldown builds vertical pulling strength and back width using a controlled, adjustable cable load. It is well suited for lifters building toward a first Pull-Up or supplementing pull-up training with extra volume. It allows precise load control that a bodyweight pull-up cannot offer. It is a staple back-building exercise across nearly every upper-body program.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit at the lat pulldown machine and secure your knees under the pad.
2. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width.
3. Brace your core and lean back slightly.
4. Pull the bar down toward your upper chest.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the bottom of the pull.
6. Let the bar rise back up under control to full arm extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Pull with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Keep your chest tall throughout the pull
> — Avoid leaning back excessively to move the weight
> — Control the bar on the way up rather than letting it snap

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans back sharply to help pull the bar down
> — The bar stops short of the chest, cutting the range of motion
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears during the pull

**Progressions:** Pull-Up
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown, Straight-Arm Pulldown

## Section 9 — Dumbbell Curl

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Dumbbell Curl |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_CURL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | FOREARMS |

**ABOUT**
> The Dumbbell Curl is an isolation exercise performed by curling a dumbbell in each hand from a hanging position up toward the shoulder. It trains the biceps with the wrist held in a supinated, palm-up position. It is typically performed standing.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dumbbell Curl builds biceps size and strength in a focused, single-joint movement. It is well suited for lifters looking to add arm-specific volume to an upper-body program. It also allows each arm to work independently, addressing side-to-side strength differences. It is one of the most widely used accessory exercises for arm development.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding a dumbbell in each hand at your sides, palms facing forward.
2. Brace your core and keep your elbows close to your torso.
3. Curl both dumbbells up toward your shoulders.
4. Squeeze your biceps at the top of the movement.
5. Lower the dumbbells back down under control to full extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your elbows pinned at your sides throughout
> — Curl with your forearms only, not your shoulders
> — Squeeze at the top of each rep before lowering
> — Lower the dumbbells with control rather than dropping them

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The elbows drift forward away from the torso during the curl
> — The torso swings backward to help lift the dumbbells
> — The dumbbells drop quickly on the way down instead of lowering under control

**Progressions:** Preacher Curl
**Regressions:** Resistance Band Curl
**Alternatives:** Hammer Curl, Cable Curl

**ABOUT**
> The Hammer Curl is an isolation exercise performed by curling a dumbbell in each hand with the palms facing each other throughout the movement. It trains the biceps and forearms with a neutral grip. The neutral wrist position distinguishes it from the standard Dumbbell Curl.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Hammer Curl builds forearm and grip strength in addition to biceps size, more than a standard curl does. It is well suited for lifters who want stronger grip carryover to rows, pull-ups, and carries. It also offers a joint-friendly curling option for wrists that are uncomfortable in a fully supinated position. It complements the Dumbbell Curl as part of a balanced arm program.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding a dumbbell in each hand at your sides, palms facing your body.
2. Brace your core and keep your elbows close to your torso.
3. Curl both dumbbells up toward your shoulders, keeping your palms facing in.
4. Squeeze at the top of the movement.
5. Lower the dumbbells back down under control to full extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your wrists neutral throughout the entire movement
> — Keep your elbows pinned at your sides
> — Curl with your forearms only, not your shoulders
> — Lower the dumbbells with control rather than dropping them

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The wrists rotate away from neutral during the curl
> — The elbows drift forward away from the torso
> — The torso swings to help lift the dumbbells

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.

## Section 11 — Front Plank

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Front Plank |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_EXTENSION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | LOWER_BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Front Plank is a bodyweight core exercise performed by holding a straight-line position facing the floor, balanced on the forearms and toes. It trains the core, lower back, and shoulders to resist spinal extension under tension. It is held statically rather than performed for repetitions.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Front Plank builds core endurance and the anti-extension strength that protects the spine during heavier lifts. It teaches the body to hold a stable, neutral spine under tension, a skill that carries into nearly every other exercise. It is well suited for beginners building core awareness and for advanced lifters as a warm-up activation drill. It requires no equipment and can be performed anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Start on the floor facing down, on your forearms and toes.
2. Position your elbows directly under your shoulders.
3. Brace your core and squeeze your glutes.
4. Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips level with your shoulders and heels
> — Brace your core as if preparing for a light punch to the stomach
> — Keep your neck neutral, eyes toward the floor
> — Breathe steadily rather than holding your breath

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips sag toward the floor as the hold continues
> — The hips pike up higher than the shoulders
> — The neck drops and the head hangs lower than the spine

**Progressions:** Ab Wheel Rollout
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Dead Bug, Bird Dog

## Section 12 — Dead Bug

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Dead Bug |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_EXTENSION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Dead Bug is a bodyweight core exercise performed lying on the back, extending opposite arm and leg while keeping the lower back pressed into the floor. It trains the core to resist spinal extension during limb movement. It is performed with slow, controlled repetitions rather than a static hold.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dead Bug builds core control during limb movement, training the body to keep the spine stable while the arms and legs move independently. It is well suited for beginners learning to brace the core correctly and for anyone restoring core control after a back issue. It also reinforces the same anti-extension skill used in heavier compound lifts. It is a low-impact exercise that places minimal load on the spine.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your back with arms reaching toward the ceiling and knees bent at 90 degrees.
2. Press your lower back flat into the floor.
3. Brace your core before starting any movement.
4. Lower one arm and the opposite leg toward the floor at the same time.
5. Stop just before your lower back lifts off the floor.
6. Return to the starting position and repeat on the other side.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your lower back pressed into the floor throughout
> — Move slowly rather than rushing the limb extension
> — Exhale as you extend your arm and leg
> — Stop the movement before your back arches off the floor

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back arches off the floor as the limbs extend
> — The opposite arm and leg move at different speeds
> — The movement speeds up instead of staying slow and controlled

**Progressions:** Ab Wheel Rollout
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Front Plank, Bird Dog

## Section 13 — Pallof Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Pallof Press |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Pallof Press is a core exercise performed by pressing a cable handle straight out from the chest while resisting the cable's pull to one side. It trains the core's ability to resist rotation. It is performed standing, facing sideways to the cable machine.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Pallof Press builds anti-rotation core strength that protects the spine during twisting and uneven loading. It is well suited for athletes whose sports involve rotational forces and for lifters wanting more complete core training beyond flexion work. It also reinforces the bracing skill used to keep the torso square during single-arm or single-leg lifts. It is a low-impact way to train core stability without spinal loading.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand sideways to a cable machine set at chest height.
2. Hold the handle with both hands at your chest.
3. Step away from the machine to create tension in the cable.
4. Brace your core to resist the cable pulling you sideways.
5. Press the handle straight out in front of your chest.
6. Return the handle back to your chest under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core before you press the handle out
> — Keep your hips and shoulders square to the front
> — Press straight out rather than letting the cable pull your arms sideways
> — Move slowly rather than rushing the press

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso rotates toward the cable machine during the press
> — The hips shift sideways to resist the pull instead of the core bracing
> — The arms bend instead of staying extended through the press

**Progressions:** Half-Kneeling Cable Chop
**Regressions:** Resistance Band Anti-Rotation Hold
**Alternatives:** Side Plank, Plank Reach-Through

| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | GLUTES, LOWER_BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Bird Dog is a bodyweight core exercise performed on the hands and knees, extending opposite arm and leg while keeping the spine stable. It trains the core's ability to resist rotation and extension at the same time. It is performed slowly with a brief pause at full extension.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Bird Dog builds core stability and balance while training opposite-side coordination between the arms and legs. It is well suited for beginners learning core control and for anyone working on lower-back stability. It also reinforces the same cross-body bracing pattern used in walking and running. It is a low-impact exercise appropriate for nearly any training level.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Start on your hands and knees with your back flat.
2. Brace your core before starting any movement.
3. Extend one arm forward and the opposite leg back at the same time.
4. Keep your hips and shoulders level throughout the extension.
5. Hold the extended position briefly.
6. Return to the starting position and repeat on the other side.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat rather than letting it sag or arch
> — Move slowly rather than swinging the arm and leg into place
> — Keep your hips level as the leg extends back
> — Hold a brief pause at full extension before returning

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips rotate to one side as the leg extends
> — The back arches as the arm and leg reach full extension
> — The movement speeds up, losing control at the extended position

**Progressions:** Plank Reach-Through
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.

## Section 15 — Hanging Leg Raise

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Hanging Leg Raise |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_FLEXION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | PULL_UP_BAR |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |

**ABOUT**
> The Hanging Leg Raise is a core flexion exercise performed by hanging from a pull-up bar and raising the legs toward the chest. It trains the core, particularly the lower abdominals, while also demanding grip strength. It is performed from a full dead hang.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Hanging Leg Raise builds lower-abdominal strength and grip endurance in a single hanging movement. It is well suited for lifters who have built a base level of core and grip strength and are ready for a more demanding flexion exercise. It also reinforces the hanging position used in pull-ups and other bar work. It is a strong benchmark exercise for advanced core training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Hang from a pull-up bar with your arms fully extended.
2. Brace your core and squeeze your shoulder blades down and back.
3. Raise your legs up toward your chest, keeping them as straight as comfortable.
4. Pause briefly at the top of the raise.
5. Lower your legs back down under control to a full hang.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Start each rep from a full hang rather than a partial one
> — Brace your core before initiating the leg raise
> — Lower your legs with control rather than letting them drop
> — Keep your shoulder blades engaged throughout the hang

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The body swings to build momentum for the leg raise
> — The legs bend significantly to make the raise easier
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping well short of the chest

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Reverse Crunch
**Alternatives:** Cable Crunch, Sit-Up

## Section 16 — Sit-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Sit-Up |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_FLEXION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |

**ABOUT**
> The Sit-Up is a core flexion exercise performed lying on the back, curling the torso up to a seated position. It trains the core through a full range of spinal flexion. It is performed with the feet anchored or flat on the floor.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Sit-Up builds core flexion strength through a complete range of motion. It is one of the most familiar and accessible core exercises, requiring no equipment. It is well suited for beginners building general core strength and for anyone training core endurance. It also serves as a foundation before progressing to more demanding flexion exercises.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat on the floor.
2. Cross your arms over your chest or place your hands lightly behind your head.
3. Brace your core and curl your torso up off the floor.
4. Continue curling until your torso is upright.
5. Lower your torso back down under control to the starting position.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Lead with your chest rather than pulling with your neck
> — Keep your feet flat and anchored throughout the movement
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than using momentum
> — Exhale as you curl up toward the seated position

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hands pull on the neck to help lift the torso
> — The movement uses a jerking motion instead of a controlled curl
> — The feet lift off the floor partway through the set

**Progressions:** Hanging Leg Raise
**Regressions:** Crunch
**Alternatives:** Reverse Crunch, Cable Crunch

## Section 17 — Russian Twist

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Russian Twist |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT, WEIGHT_PLATE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Russian Twist is a core rotation exercise performed seated, rotating a weight from side to side across the body. It trains the obliques and deep core rotators. It is typically performed with the feet lifted off the floor for added difficulty.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Russian Twist builds rotational core strength that carries over to throwing, swinging, and twisting movements in sport and daily life. It is well suited for athletes training rotational power and for lifters adding variety to their core program. It also builds the oblique strength that supports heavier compound lifts. It can be scaled easily by adjusting the weight or foot position.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit on the floor with your knees bent and lean back slightly.
2. Hold a weight with both hands in front of your chest.
3. Brace your core and lift your feet slightly off the floor.
4. Rotate the weight to one side, tapping it near the floor.
5. Rotate back through center to the opposite side.
6. Continue alternating sides at a controlled pace.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core before starting the rotation
> — Rotate from your torso rather than just swinging your arms
> — Keep your chest lifted throughout the movement
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than rushing the twist

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back rounds as the torso leans back
> — Only the arms swing while the torso stays still
> — The feet drop to the floor partway through the set

**Progressions:** Medicine Ball Rotational Throw
**Regressions:** Bicycle Crunch
**Alternatives:** Standing Trunk Rotation, Cable Woodchop

## Section 18 — Cable Woodchop

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Cable Woodchop |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Cable Woodchop is a core rotation exercise performed on a cable machine, pulling the handle diagonally across the body from high to low or low to high. It trains the obliques and deep core rotators under constant cable tension. It mimics a chopping motion across the torso.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Cable Woodchop builds rotational core strength under constant resistance, unlike bodyweight rotation exercises. It carries over to sports and activities that involve swinging or chopping motions. It is well suited for athletes and lifters looking to add resisted rotational training to their program. It also reinforces hip and core sequencing used in throwing and striking movements.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Set the cable to a high or low position and stand sideways to the machine.
2. Grip the handle with both hands and step away to create tension.
3. Brace your core and rotate your torso away from the machine.
4. Pull the handle diagonally across your body.
5. Pivot your back foot as your torso rotates.
6. Return the handle back across your body under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Rotate through your torso and hips together
> — Keep your arms relatively fixed relative to your chest
> — Brace your core before starting each rep
> — Control the return rather than letting the cable pull you back

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — Only the arms pull the handle while the torso stays still
> — The back foot stays planted instead of pivoting with the rotation
> — The movement turns into a yanking motion instead of a controlled rotation

**Progressions:** Medicine Ball Rotational Throw
**Regressions:** Standing Trunk Rotation
**Alternatives:** Russian Twist, Bicycle Crunch
