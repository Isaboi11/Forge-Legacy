## Section 1 — Chin-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Chin-Up |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | PULL_UP_BAR |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK, BICEPS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Chin-Up is a bodyweight pulling exercise performed hanging from a bar with an underhand grip and pulling the chin above it. It trains the back, biceps, and shoulders through a vertical pulling pattern. The underhand grip involves the biceps more directly than a standard Pull-Up.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Chin-Up builds upper-body pulling strength along with more direct biceps involvement than an overhand-grip Pull-Up. It develops grip, back, and arm strength in a single compound movement. It is well suited for anyone who has built a strength base and is ready for a bodyweight pulling challenge. It also offers grip variety alongside the Pull-Up for complete vertical-pulling development.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Hang from the bar with an underhand grip, hands shoulder-width apart.
2. Brace your core and squeeze your shoulder blades down and back.
3. Pull your body upward by driving your elbows down toward your hips.
4. Continue pulling until your chin clears the bar.
5. Lower yourself back down under control to a full hang.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Start each rep from a full hang with arms straight
> — Pull your elbows down and back rather than just bending your arms
> — Keep your core braced to avoid excessive swinging
> — Lower yourself with control rather than dropping

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The body swings to build momentum instead of pulling with control
> — Only the arms bend without the shoulder blades engaging first
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of a full hang at the bottom

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Negative Pull-Up
**Alternatives:** Pull-Up, Lat Pulldown

## Section 2 — Assisted Pull-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Assisted Pull-Up |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Assisted Pull-Up is a vertical pulling exercise performed on a machine that counterbalances part of the athlete's body weight. It trains the back, biceps, and shoulders through the same motion as a Pull-Up at a reduced relative load. It uses a weighted lever or band system to provide assistance.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Assisted Pull-Up builds vertical pulling strength at a reduced relative body-weight load, making it an accessible entry point before a full Pull-Up. It is well suited for beginners building toward their first unassisted pull-up. It also allows precise load adjustment, reducing assistance gradually as strength improves. It is a common machine found in most commercial gyms.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Set the machine's assistance level and kneel or step onto the platform.
2. Grip the handles slightly wider than shoulder width.
3. Brace your core and squeeze your shoulder blades down and back.
4. Pull your body upward until your chin clears the handles.
5. Lower yourself back down under control to a full hang.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Start each rep from a full hang with arms straight
> — Pull your elbows down and back rather than just bending your arms
> — Reduce the assistance level gradually as you progress
> — Lower yourself with control rather than dropping

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The assistance level is set too high, removing the training effect
> — Only the arms bend without the shoulder blades engaging first
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of a full hang at the bottom

**Progressions:** Pull-Up
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern machine variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Lat Pulldown Machine, Band-Assisted Pull-Up

## Section 3 — Band-Assisted Pull-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Band-Assisted Pull-Up |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | RESISTANCE_BAND, PULL_UP_BAR |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Band-Assisted Pull-Up is a vertical pulling exercise performed with a resistance band looped around the bar and under one foot or knee, reducing the load during the pull. It trains the back, biceps, and shoulders through the same motion as a Pull-Up at a reduced relative load. The band provides the most assistance at the bottom of the movement.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Band-Assisted Pull-Up builds vertical pulling strength at a reduced relative body-weight load, making it an accessible entry point before a full Pull-Up. It is well suited for athletes training at a pull-up bar without access to an assisted-pull-up machine. It also allows assistance to be reduced gradually by switching to a lighter band as strength improves. It requires only a resistance band and a pull-up bar.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Loop a resistance band around the bar and under one foot or knee.
2. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width and hang fully extended.
3. Brace your core and squeeze your shoulder blades down and back.
4. Pull your body upward until your chin clears the bar.
5. Lower yourself back down under control to a full hang.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Choose a band thickness that lets you complete the full range of motion
> — Pull your elbows down and back rather than just bending your arms
> — Reduce the band's assistance over time as you progress
> — Lower yourself with control rather than dropping

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The band is too thick, removing most of the training effect
> — Only the arms bend without the shoulder blades engaging first
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of a full hang at the bottom

**Progressions:** Pull-Up
**Regressions:** Assisted Pull-Up
**Alternatives:** Negative Pull-Up

## Section 4 — Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown is a vertical pulling exercise performed on a cable machine with a wide overhand grip, pulling the bar down to the upper chest. It trains the back, biceps, and shoulders. The wide grip places more emphasis on back width than a narrower grip.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown builds back width using a controlled, adjustable cable load. It offers a grip variation that emphasizes a different part of the back than the standard Lat Pulldown. It is well suited for lifters wanting to train vertical pulling through multiple grip widths. It is a staple back-building exercise across nearly every upper-body program.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit at the lat pulldown machine and secure your knees under the pad.
2. Grip the bar well outside shoulder width.
3. Brace your core and lean back slightly.
4. Pull the bar down toward your upper chest.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the bottom of the pull.
6. Let the bar rise back up under control to full arm extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Pull with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Keep your chest tall throughout the pull
> — Avoid leaning back excessively to move the weight
> — Control the bar on the way up rather than letting it snap

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans back sharply to help pull the bar down
> — The bar stops short of the chest, cutting the range of motion
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears during the pull

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Lat Pulldown, Straight-Arm Pulldown

## Section 5 — Straight-Arm Pulldown

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Straight-Arm Pulldown |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Straight-Arm Pulldown is a vertical pulling exercise performed on a cable machine, pulling a bar down from overhead to the thighs while keeping the arms straight. It trains the back through shoulder extension rather than elbow flexion. It isolates the lats more directly than a bent-arm pulldown.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Straight-Arm Pulldown builds lat strength and size while minimizing biceps involvement, isolating the back more directly than a standard pulldown. It is well suited as an accessory exercise to add lat-specific volume after compound pulling work. It also reinforces the shoulder-extension pattern used in swimming and other overhead-to-side movements. It is a common accessory exercise in back-focused hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach a bar to a high cable pulley and grip it with both hands.
2. Stand facing the machine with your arms extended overhead.
3. Brace your core and keep a slight, fixed bend in your elbows.
4. Pull the bar down toward your thighs in an arcing path.
5. Squeeze your lats at the bottom of the pull.
6. Let the bar rise back up under control to the starting position.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your elbows fixed throughout the entire movement
> — Pull through your lats rather than bending your arms more
> — Keep your torso tall rather than leaning forward
> — Control the return rather than letting the cable pull your arms up quickly

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The elbows bend more as the set fatigues, turning the pulldown into a row
> — The torso leans forward to help pull the bar down
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of the thighs

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Lat Pulldown, Kneeling Pulldown

## Section 9 — Negative Pull-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Negative Pull-Up |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | PULL_UP_BAR |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Negative Pull-Up is a bodyweight pulling exercise performed by starting at the top of a Pull-Up and lowering down as slowly as possible. It trains the back, biceps, and shoulders through the lowering, or eccentric, portion of the movement only. It is used as a stepping stone toward a full Pull-Up.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Negative Pull-Up builds pulling strength through the eccentric portion of the movement, which many athletes can control before they can complete a full pull-up. It is well suited for athletes who can get their chin above the bar with help but cannot yet pull themselves up unassisted. It also reinforces the same shoulder-blade engagement used in a full Pull-Up. It requires only a bar to hang from and something to step on to reach the top position.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Use a box or step to get your chin above the bar.
2. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width.
3. Remove your feet from the support and brace your core.
4. Lower yourself as slowly as possible toward a full hang.
5. Step back up to the top position once you reach full extension.
6. Repeat for the set.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Lower as slowly as you can control on every rep
> — Keep your shoulder blades engaged throughout the descent
> — Use the box or step to reset rather than pulling yourself back up
> — Keep your core braced to avoid excessive swinging

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The descent speeds up partway through instead of staying slow
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears during the lower
> — The body swings instead of lowering in a straight line

**Progressions:** Pull-Up
**Regressions:** Band-Assisted Pull-Up
**Alternatives:** Assisted Pull-Up

## Section 11 — Ab Wheel Rollout

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Ab Wheel Rollout |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_EXTENSION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | LOWER_BACK, SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Ab Wheel Rollout is a core exercise performed kneeling, rolling a wheel forward while keeping the spine stable and then pulling back to the start. It trains the core's ability to resist spinal extension across a long lever arm. It is one of the most demanding anti-extension exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Ab Wheel Rollout builds anti-extension core strength under a much longer lever arm than a Plank provides, making it significantly more demanding. It is well suited for lifters who have already built solid core endurance on the Plank or Dead Bug. It also reinforces the same bracing skill used to protect the spine during heavy squats and deadlifts. It is a strong benchmark exercise for advanced core training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Kneel on the floor holding the ab wheel with both hands.
2. Brace your core and keep your back flat.
3. Roll the wheel forward, extending your body away from your knees.
4. Stop before your lower back begins to sag.
5. Pull the wheel back toward your knees using your core.
6. Return to the starting kneeling position.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your core braced throughout the entire rollout
> — Roll out only as far as you can control the return
> — Keep your back flat rather than letting it sag
> — Pull back with your core rather than just your arms

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back sags as the wheel rolls further out
> — The hips drop toward the floor during the rollout
> — The arms pull the body back instead of the core driving the return

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Front Plank
**Alternatives:** Stability Ball Plank, Forearm Plank to Push-Up

## Section 12 — Stability Ball Plank

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Stability Ball Plank |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_EXTENSION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | LOWER_BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Stability Ball Plank is a core exercise performed holding a plank position with the forearms resting on a stability ball instead of the floor. It trains the core, lower back, and shoulders to resist spinal extension on an unstable surface. The ball adds a balance demand a standard Plank does not have.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Stability Ball Plank builds anti-extension core strength along with added shoulder and core stabilization, since the ball can shift in any direction. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the standard Plank and want an added stability challenge. It also reinforces the small stabilizing muscles around the shoulders and core that a flat surface does not demand as much. It requires only a stability ball.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Kneel behind a stability ball and rest your forearms on top of it.
2. Walk your feet back until your body is in a straight line.
3. Brace your core and squeeze your glutes.
4. Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the ball steady rather than letting it roll excessively
> — Keep your hips level with your shoulders and heels
> — Brace your core throughout the entire hold
> — Breathe steadily rather than holding your breath

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The ball rolls excessively, breaking the straight body line
> — The hips sag toward the floor as the hold continues
> — The hips pike up higher than the shoulders

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Front Plank
**Alternatives:** Forearm Plank to Push-Up

## Section 13 — Forearm Plank to Push-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Forearm Plank to Push-Up |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_EXTENSION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, CHEST |

**ABOUT**
> The Forearm Plank to Push-Up is a core exercise performed by alternating between a forearm plank and a push-up plank position, one arm at a time. It trains the core's ability to resist rotation and extension as the body shifts between positions. It combines a hold with continuous movement.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Forearm Plank to Push-Up builds anti-extension and anti-rotation core strength together, since shifting between forearms and hands challenges the core to resist twisting. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the standard Plank and want a more dynamic core challenge. It also reinforces shoulder stability through the repeated transition between positions. It requires no equipment beyond a flat surface.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Start in a forearm plank position with your body in a straight line.
2. Brace your core and place one hand on the floor under your shoulder.
3. Push up onto that hand, then place the other hand down to reach a push-up plank.
4. Lower one arm back down to your forearm, then the other.
5. Continue alternating which arm leads the transition.
6. Keep your hips as still as possible throughout.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips as still as possible during each transition
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than rushing between positions
> — Keep your body in a straight line throughout
> — Alternate the leading arm evenly across the set

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips rock side to side during the transitions
> — The hips sag or pike during either plank position
> — The pace rushes instead of staying controlled

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Front Plank
**Alternatives:** Stability Ball Plank, Ab Wheel Rollout
