## Section 1 — Foam Rolling IT Band

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Foam Rolling IT Band |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | FOAM_ROLL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | FOAM_ROLLER |
| Primary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | QUADS |

**ABOUT**
> Foam Rolling IT Band is a self-myofascial release exercise performed by rolling the outer thigh over a foam roller. It applies sustained pressure along the outside of the leg. It is performed slowly, pausing on tender areas.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> Foam Rolling IT Band helps reduce tightness along the outer thigh that can build from running or heavy lower-body training. It is well suited as part of a cool-down after lower-body or running sessions. It also supports general hip and knee comfort, since outer-thigh tightness can affect both joints. It requires only a foam roller.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your side with the foam roller positioned under your outer thigh.
2. Support your upper body on your forearm.
3. Stack or stagger your legs for balance and control.
4. Roll slowly from just above your knee up toward your hip.
5. Pause on any tender areas for a few breaths.
6. Continue rolling for the desired duration, then switch sides.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Roll slowly rather than quickly back and forth
> — Pause on tender spots rather than rolling straight through them
> — Keep your core braced to control your body weight on the roller
> — Breathe steadily throughout the rolling

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The rolling speeds up instead of staying slow and controlled
> — The hips twist excessively to avoid the more tender areas
> — The pressure is rushed past tender areas instead of pausing there

**Progressions:** None — self-myofascial release has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.
**Regressions:** None — self-myofascial release has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.
**Alternatives:** Foam Rolling Quads

**ABOUT**
> The Lacrosse Ball Glute Release is a self-myofascial release exercise performed by sitting on a lacrosse ball positioned under the glute. It applies focused, concentrated pressure to a smaller area than a foam roller. It is performed slowly, pausing on tender spots.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Lacrosse Ball Glute Release reaches deeper, more specific tender spots in the glutes than a foam roller's wider surface allows. **HOW TO DO IT**

5. Continue searching and pausing on tender areas for the desired duration.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Move slowly to locate tender spots precisely
> — Pause and breathe once you find a tender area
> — Adjust the amount of weight you place on the ball as needed
> — Keep your movements small and deliberate

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The pressure is rushed past tender areas instead of pausing there
> — Too much weight is applied too quickly on a very tender spot
> — The lower back takes on weight instead of the glute

**Progressions:** None — self-myofascial release has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.
**Regressions:** None — self-myofascial release has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.

## Section 6 — Incline Push-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Incline Push-Up |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT, BENCH |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS, SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Incline Push-Up is a bodyweight pressing exercise performed with the hands elevated on a bench or other raised surface. It trains the chest, shoulders, and triceps with reduced body-weight load compared to a standard Push-Up. The elevated hand position makes the movement more approachable.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Incline Push-Up builds pressing strength at a reduced relative body-weight load, making it an accessible entry point before a full Push-Up. It is well suited for beginners building toward their first standard push-up. It also serves as a useful option for higher-volume conditioning work once fatigue sets in during a Push-Up set. It requires only a bench or other stable raised surface.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Place your hands on a bench, slightly wider than shoulder width.
2. Step back so your body forms a straight line to your feet.
3. Brace your core and keep your body straight from head to heels.
4. Lower your chest toward the bench by bending your elbows.
5. Press through your hands to push your body back up.
6. Finish with your arms fully extended.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your body in a straight line throughout the movement
> — Keep your elbows at roughly a 45-degree angle as you lower
> — Choose a bench height that lets you complete the full range of motion
> — Press evenly through both hands as you push up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips sag or pike instead of staying in a straight line
> — The elbows flare out wide instead of staying near 45 degrees
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full elbow extension

**Progressions:** Push-Up
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Push-Up Progression, Chest Press Machine

## Section 9 — Incline Dumbbell Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Incline Dumbbell Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL, BENCH |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Incline Dumbbell Press is a pressing exercise performed on an inclined bench, pressing a dumbbell in each hand from chest height to full arm extension. It trains the upper chest, shoulders, and triceps. The incline angle directs more work to the upper chest than a flat press.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Incline Dumbbell Press builds upper-chest strength and size that a flat press emphasizes less directly. It allows each arm to work independently, addressing side-to-side strength imbalances. It is well suited for lifters wanting to add upper-chest volume to a pressing program. It pairs well with the Dumbbell Bench Press to train the chest through multiple angles.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Set the bench to a moderate incline and lie back holding a dumbbell in each hand.
2. Position the dumbbells at shoulder height.
3. Plant your feet flat on the floor.
4. Press both dumbbells up until your arms are fully extended.
5. Lower the dumbbells back down under control to shoulder height.
6. Keep your elbows at roughly a 45-degree angle throughout.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep both dumbbells moving at the same pace
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together before each rep
> — Lower the dumbbells until you feel a stretch across your upper chest
> — Keep your feet planted and core braced throughout

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — One arm presses faster than the other through the set
> — The dumbbells drift forward over the face instead of staying over the chest
> — The incline angle is set too steep, shifting the work to the shoulders

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Push-Up
**Alternatives:** Dumbbell Bench Press, Dumbbell Fly

## Section 10 — Decline Bench Press

| Movement Pattern | PUSH_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BARBELL, BENCH |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Decline Bench Press is a pressing exercise performed on a declined bench, pushing a loaded barbell from the lower chest to full arm extension. It trains the lower chest, shoulders, and triceps. The decline angle directs more work to the lower chest than a flat press.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Decline Bench Press builds lower-chest strength and size that a flat or incline press emphasizes less directly. It often allows a slightly shorter, more shoulder-friendly bar path for some lifters. It is well suited for lifters wanting complete chest development across all three pressing angles. It pairs well with the Barbell Bench Press and Incline Dumbbell Press to round out chest training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Secure your legs at the foot of the declined bench.
2. Lie back and grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width.
3. Unrack the bar and hold it over your lower chest with arms extended.
4. Lower the bar under control to your lower chest.
5. Press the bar back up to full arm extension.
6. Re-rack the bar after your final rep.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your legs secured throughout the set
> — Keep your elbows at roughly a 45-degree angle as you lower
> — Control the lowering phase rather than dropping the bar to your chest
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together throughout the lift

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The elbows flare out wide rather than staying at roughly 45 degrees
> — The bar bounces off the chest instead of pausing under control
> — The legs slip from the foot supports during the set

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern barbell variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Barbell Bench Press
**Alternatives:** Cable Chest Press, Dumbbell Fly

## Section 11 — Cable Chest Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Cable Chest Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS, SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Cable Chest Press is a pressing exercise performed on a cable machine, pressing the handles forward from chest height to full arm extension. It trains the chest, shoulders, and triceps under constant cable tension. It can be performed standing or seated, depending on the setup.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Cable Chest Press builds chest and pressing strength under constant tension throughout the full range of motion, unlike free weights. It is well suited for lifters looking for a joint-friendly pressing option with a controlled, guided path. It also offers a useful substitute for athletes managing a shoulder limitation that free-weight pressing aggravates. It is a common accessory exercise in chest-focused programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Set the cable handles to chest height and grip one in each hand.
2. Stand or sit facing away from the machine, staggering your stance if standing.
3. Brace your core and press the handles forward.
4. Extend your arms fully at the end of the press.
5. Bring the handles together without letting them touch.
6. Return the handles back to chest height under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your shoulder blades pulled back throughout the press
> — Press straight forward rather than letting the cables pull your arms out
> — Squeeze your chest at the end of each rep
> — Control the return rather than letting the cables snap back

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The shoulders round forward during the press
> — The torso leans forward excessively to help drive the handles
> — The range of motion shortens near the chest

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Push-Up, Dumbbell Bench Press

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Chest Press Machine builds chest and pressing strength without requiring the balance and bracing skills a barbell or dumbbell press demands. It is well suited for beginners learning the pressing pattern or for lifters training alone without a spotter. It also offers a safer heavy-pressing option for athletes managing a shoulder or balance limitation. It is a common accessory exercise in beginner and hypertrophy-focused programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit in the machine with your back against the pad.
2. Grip the handles at chest height.
3. Brace your core and press the handles forward.
4. Extend your arms fully at the end of the press.
5. Return the handles back to chest height under control.
6. Avoid locking your elbows aggressively at full extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat against the pad throughout
> — Press evenly through both hands as you push forward
> — Control the return rather than letting the weight snap back
> — Adjust the seat so the handles align with your chest

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The back lifts off the pad to help drive the handles forward
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full extension
> — The weight drops quickly on the return instead of lowering under control

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern machine variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.

## Section 13 — Dumbbell Fly

| Movement Pattern | PUSH_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL, BENCH |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Dumbbell Fly is an isolation exercise performed lying on a bench, opening the arms wide and bringing a dumbbell in each hand together above the chest. It trains the chest through a wide, stretching arc rather than a straight press. It is performed with a slight, fixed bend in the elbows throughout.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dumbbell Fly builds chest size and a deep stretch under load that pressing movements do not provide in the same way. It is well suited as an accessory exercise to add chest-specific volume after pressing work. It also reinforces shoulder control through a wide range of motion. It is a common finishing exercise in chest-focused hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie back on the bench holding a dumbbell in each hand above your chest.
2. Keep a slight, fixed bend in your elbows.
3. Lower both dumbbells out to the sides in a wide arc.
4. Lower until you feel a stretch across your chest.
5. Bring the dumbbells back together above your chest.
6. Squeeze your chest at the top of the movement.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the bend in your elbows fixed throughout the movement
> — Lower with control rather than letting the weight drop
> — Squeeze your chest together at the top of each rep
> — Keep your feet planted and core braced throughout

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The elbows bend more as the set fatigues, turning the fly into a press
> — The dumbbells drop too low, straining the front of the shoulder
> — The lower back arches excessively off the bench

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Cable Fly, Incline Dumbbell Press

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Diamond Push-Up builds triceps strength and size more directly than a standard Push-Up, due to the narrower hand position. It is well suited for athletes who have mastered the standard Push-Up and want a more demanding bodyweight pressing challenge. It also serves as a useful triceps-focused option when no equipment is available. It requires no equipment beyond open floor space.

**HOW TO DO IT**

2. Form a diamond shape with your thumbs and index fingers.
3. Brace your core and keep your body in a straight line.
4. Lower your chest toward your hands by bending your elbows.
5. Keep your elbows tracking close to your body as you lower.
6. Press through your hands to push your body back up.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your elbows close to your body throughout the movement
> — Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels
> — Lower until your chest is just above your hands
> — Press evenly through both hands as you push up

**WATCH OUT FOR**

**ABOUT**
> The Dip is a bodyweight pressing exercise performed on parallel bars, lowering and raising the body using the arms. It trains the chest, shoulders, and triceps by lifting the entire body weight. It is one of the most demanding pressing exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dip builds upper-body pressing strength relative to body weight, training the chest and triceps under a heavier relative load than most pressing exercises. It is well suited for lifters who have built a strong base with the Barbell Bench Press and bodyweight pressing work. It also serves as a long-term bodyweight strength benchmark. It is a staple exercise for advanced upper-body pressing development.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Grip the parallel bars and lift your body so your arms are fully extended.
2. Lean your torso forward slightly.
3. Brace your core and bend your elbows to lower your body.
4. Lower until your shoulders are level with your elbows.
5. Press through your hands to push your body back up.
6. Finish with your arms fully extended at the top.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Lean your torso forward slightly to emphasize the chest
> — Lower only as far as your shoulders comfortably allow
> — Keep your core braced to avoid excessive swinging
> — Press through your whole hand rather than just your fingers

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The body drops too low, straining the front of the shoulder
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears during the lower
> — The body swings to build momentum instead of pressing with control

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Diamond Push-Up
**Alternatives:** Barbell Bench Press, Push-Up
