## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Purpose

Muscle Building Intermediate exists to be the **whole-physique** mid-rung of the general Muscle Building ladder. It takes an athlete who has built a base of training tolerance and balanced technique on Muscle Building Foundation and advances them by raising recoverable volume and introducing self-regulated fatigue management (RPE) — while keeping the stimulus distributed across the entire body. Its defining commitment is balance: every major muscle group is trained inside the 10–20 sets/week band (PAS §11.2), with no region prioritized over another. That whole-physique balance is the program's identity, and it is the single trait that separates it from the Lower Body ladder running alongside it.

### Target Athlete

- 1–3 years of structured resistance training with this style, per `Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §3.2's INTERMEDIATE definition.
- Has graduated Muscle Building Foundation, or has equivalent experience self-managing a rep-range (double-progression) model.
- Primary goal is whole-body muscle development — a balanced physique, not a single-region specialization.
- Comfortable using RPE as a permitted intensity/fatigue tool (PAS-D3 — HYPERTROPHY INTERMEDIATE).
- Has 4 days/week available and can recover from a volume load that accumulates across a mesocycle.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Wants lower-body / glute specialization | This program deliberately balances volume across the whole physique; it will not concentrate the majority of weekly sets on the legs | **Lower Body Intermediate** (the same-level program in the family's other ladder) |
| Complete beginner to structured hypertrophy training | Starts mid-range in the volume envelope and assumes self-managed rep ranges | Muscle Building Foundation (Sort 5) |
| Primary goal is maximal strength | Volume-driven, no 1RM-style primary stimulus (Family Research Deliverable 1; PAS §11.2) | Strength family |
| Primary goal is fat loss / conditioning | Not built around a metabolic/work-capacity stimulus | Conditioning family (incl. Body Recomposition) |

### Expected Outcomes

Measurable whole-body hypertrophy stimulus across a 10-week block: rising working volume per muscle group, demonstrable double-progression load increases on the compound anchors of each session, and readiness for the periodized structure of Muscle Building Advanced.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Every major muscle group lands inside 10–20 sets/week (PAS §11.2), verifiable by summing sets across the week's four sessions.
- No region exceeds another by a margin that would make the program read as a specialization rather than a balanced split.
- Week-over-week progression is visible (QC-2) via rising set counts (Volume Accumulation) and/or advancing rep-range targets (Double Progression), not heavier absolute load alone.

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 10 weeks (PAS-D7 → exactly one deload).
- **Frequency:** 4 sessions/week.
- **Split:** **Upper/Lower**, run twice per week (Upper A · Lower A · Upper B · Lower B). PAS §11.2 names Push/Pull/Legs *or* Upper/Lower for HYPERTROPHY; at a 4-day frequency, Upper/Lower is the correct fit (PPL maps cleanly to 3 or 6 days, not 4) and gives each muscle group two exposures per week — the distribution that keeps every group inside the 10–20 sets/week band.
- **Deload:** one, Week 9 (slots 33–36), per PAS §14. Peak training in Week 10 (slots 37–40). The deload maintains all four sessions but cuts primary set volume 40–50% (PAS-D8).
- **Periodization summary:** a single accumulation mesocycle. Volume Accumulation (Model 4) raises working sets across Weeks 1–8, the Week 9 deload discharges accumulated fatigue, and Week 10 is the peak expression of the accumulated volume. Within that arc, Double Progression (Model 2) governs load on every exercise. This is **not** Block Periodization — that model is introduced one rung later, at Muscle Building Advanced, and its absence here is a deliberate distinction (Section 8).

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Every session follows the section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9; WS-A5). WARM_UP is required for HYPERTROPHY (PAS-D9); COOL_DOWN is optional and not specified at Blueprint stage.

**Upper session template:**
1. **WARM_UP** — general raise + dynamic prep for the session's primary pushing/pulling patterns.
2. **MAIN** — a horizontal or vertical **press compound** first (highest stimulus), a **row/pull compound** second (the §11.2 anti-"push/pull neglect" balance is enforced *within the session*, not just across the week), then shoulder, biceps, and triceps isolation last.
3. **(COOL_DOWN optional.)**

**Lower session template:**
1. **WARM_UP** — general raise + dynamic prep for squat/hinge patterns.
2. **MAIN** — a **squat-pattern compound** first, a **hinge-pattern compound** second (quad and posterior-chain balance enforced within the session), then quad, hamstring, glute, and calf isolation last.
3. **(COOL_DOWN optional.)**

Exercise order is fixed by PAS §11.2: compounds first, isolation last; cables and machines are appropriate throughout. No core-compound minimum applies (this is HYPERTROPHY, not STRENGTH — Family Research Deliverable 6).

The architecture's defining test is that the four sessions sum to a **balanced** weekly distribution. With two Upper and two Lower sessions, each major group gets two weekly exposures:

| Muscle group | Trained on | Target weekly sets (PAS §11.2 band) |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | Upper A + Upper B | 10–16 |
| Back (lats/upper back) | Upper A + Upper B | 12–18 |
| Shoulders | Upper A + Upper B | 10–16 |
| Biceps / Triceps | Upper A + Upper B | 8–14 each |
| Quads | Lower A + Lower B | 12–18 |
| Hamstrings | Lower A + Lower B | 10–16 |
| Glutes | Lower A + Lower B | 10–16 |
| Calves | Lower A + Lower B | 8–14 |

The key structural fact for the distinction test (Section 9): **upper-body and lower-body weekly volume are roughly co-equal here** — two full sessions each. No region is starved and none is prioritized. This balanced 50/50 split *is* the program.

## Section 5 — Progression Blueprint

- **Double Progression (Model 2)** on every exercise: a rep range is prescribed (e.g., compounds 6–10, isolation 10–15); the athlete adds load once they hit the top of the range on all sets (PAS §7.1). "Failure" = failing to reach the range top, handled with the family's two-stage rule (Family Research Deliverable 3): one short session repeated, a second consecutive short session triggers a load reduction and restart.
- **Volume Accumulation (Model 4)** layered across the mesocycle: working sets per exercise rise across Weeks 1–8 (e.g., 3 → 4 → 5 on primary movements), reset down at the Week 9 deload, peak in Week 10. This is the program's primary expression of the family's "accumulation, not a peak" identity (Family Research Deliverable 1).
- **RPE** permitted (PAS-D3, INTERMEDIATE) in `notes` to guide the last-set effort as accumulated fatigue rises — not required.

Starting position: **mid-range** of PAS-D11 (Family Research Deliverable 4's INTERMEDIATE curve), accumulating toward the upper-middle by Week 8.

## Section 6 — Volume Blueprint

- **Per session:** 5–8 exercises, 18–30 sets (PAS-D11). Opening weeks sit near the lower-middle (≈18–22 sets); peak weeks approach the upper-middle (≈26–28 sets) as Volume Accumulation adds working sets.
- **Per week:** the Section 4 distribution, every major group inside 10–20 sets/week (PAS §11.2).
- **Session duration:** within HYPERTROPHY's 50–80 min guideline (PAS §10.2); peak-week sessions sit toward the upper end, which is expected and needs no deviation note.
- **Guardrail check:** the per-session and per-muscle numbers sit inside PAS-D11 and §11.2 at both the opening and peak of the accumulation curve — no written deviation justification required.

---

## Section 7 — Recovery Blueprint

- **Rest-day placement:** minimum one full rest day is satisfiable across four sessions (e.g., Upper A / Lower A / rest / Upper B / Lower B / rest / rest). Upper and Lower alternation means consecutive training days never hit the same muscle group, which is itself a recovery feature of the Upper/Lower split.
- **Intra-session rest:** INTERMEDIATE guidance (PAS §10.3) — 90–180s for compounds (recommended), 60–90s for isolation (optional). Reference-only (EPA1-D2).
- **Fatigue management:** RPE notes (PAS-D3) help the athlete read accumulated fatigue as set counts rise — the central recovery challenge in a Volume-Accumulation program (Family Research Deliverable 5).
- **Deload:** Week 9 maintains all four sessions, cuts primary set volume 40–50% (PAS-D8). For the graduate of Muscle Building Foundation, this is their second coached deload — reinforcing, not introducing, the concept.

## Section 8 — Successor Readiness

Muscle Building Intermediate hands Muscle Building Advanced (Sort 7) four things (Family Research Deliverable 8):

1. **Movement competency** across the full Upper/Lower exercise pool — no pattern needs teaching from zero.
2. **Volume tolerance** — the athlete has completed a full accumulation mesocycle and can recover from upper-middle weekly volume, the prerequisite for Advanced's higher load.
3. **One completed structural deload** (Week 9), so Advanced's two deloads are familiar.
4. **Readiness for the specific progression jump** — the family's one genuine *model* jump: from Double Progression + Volume Accumulation to **Double Progression + Block Periodization**, plus a step from 4 to 5 training days/week. Muscle Building Intermediate's single accumulation arc is the conceptual on-ramp to Advanced's multi-phase block structure.

This handoff is itself a distinction marker: Muscle Building Intermediate is defined partly by *what it prepares the athlete for* (a periodized Advanced program), a forward link Lower Body Intermediate does not have (it is terminal).

## Section 9 — Blueprint Stress Test

| # | Risk | Severity | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Convergence with Lower Body Intermediate** (the family's named risk) | **High — headline** | See full analysis below. Verdict: **distinct, conditional on the Lower Body Intermediate Blueprint passing the stated test.** |
| 2 | Adherence under accumulating volume | Medium | Mitigated: Upper/Lower split caps per-session length; RPE lets the athlete self-regulate the last sets; Week 9 deload discharges fatigue before it compounds. |
| 3 | Whole-physique balance silently drifting into an upper- or lower-favored split during authoring | Medium | Mitigated by the Section 4 success criterion — summed weekly sets per group must stay inside 10–20 with no region dominating. This is a QC checkpoint for Stage 2. |
| 4 | Indistinct from its own predecessor (Muscle Building Foundation) | Low | Cleanly distinct: higher starting volume, RPE introduced, mid-range vs low-end accumulation curve, INTERMEDIATE vs BEGINNER. Not the concern here. |

### Headline analysis — Muscle Building Intermediate vs. Lower Body Intermediate

**The risk, stated honestly.** These two programs share level (INTERMEDIATE), category (HYPERTROPHY), environment (GYM), duration (10wk), frequency (4/wk), total workouts (40), goal alignment (BUILD_MUSCLE), progression model (Double Progression + Volume Accumulation), and deload schedule (Week 9, slots 33–36, peak Week 10). On a metadata table they are indistinguishable. This is exactly the profile that led the Strength family to retire Powerbuilding Intermediate — and the family research was explicit that the distinguishing argument must be *proven in authored structure*, not assumed.

**Why this pair is better-positioned than the retired Strength pair.** Powerbuilding Intermediate's claimed differentiators reduced to catalog topology (it was a ladder terminus) and a volume-composition argument that collapsed because its comparator already shared the trait. Muscle Building Intermediate's differentiator is different in kind: **body-region volume distribution**, a training-content axis that `Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §2.2 *already endorsed* when it approved the Lower Body sub-ladder as "a distinct athlete goal … with different structural emphasis." Section 4 makes the distribution concrete from this side: Muscle Building Intermediate is a **balanced 50/50 Upper/Lower split** — two upper sessions, two lower sessions, every major group inside 10–20 sets/week with no region favored.

**The falsifiable test the Lower Body Intermediate Blueprint must pass.** This Blueprint establishes the whole-physique baseline; the burden now shifts to Lower Body Intermediate to demonstrate *concentration* against that baseline. Specifically, the Lower Body Intermediate Blueprint must show that:

1. **The majority of its weekly working sets fall on lower-body muscle groups** (quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves) — not the ~50% this program allots them. A defensible target is ≥65–70% of weekly sets on the lower body.
2. **Upper-body work is reduced to a maintenance allotment** (well below the 10–20 sets/week band — enough to retain, not to grow), rather than the two full upper sessions Muscle Building Intermediate runs.
3. **Its session structure is not derivable** by deleting Muscle Building Intermediate's two Upper sessions and keeping its two Lower sessions. If Lower Body Intermediate is literally "this program minus the upper days," it has failed the test and the pair should be re-examined under the Family Research Deliverable 7 / §2.2 remediation paths (add a real differentiator; formally document the body-region split as the accepted rationale; or retire and redirect).

If the Lower Body Intermediate Blueprint meets all three, the body-region axis is demonstrated, not asserted, and the pair survives — a materially stronger outcome than the Strength family reached, because the differentiator here is genuine training content rather than topology.

**This Blueprint's position:** Muscle Building Intermediate is self-justifying *from its own side* regardless of how the test resolves — it is the natural whole-physique mid-rung of a three-program ladder, distinguished from its own predecessor and successor by level, volume, and (at the Advanced handoff) progression model. Its existence does not depend on Lower Body Intermediate. The convergence risk is therefore not a risk to *this* program's existence; it is a risk to *Lower Body Intermediate's*, and it is that program's Blueprint that must clear the bar above.

## Section 10 — Final Blueprint Recommendation

**Verdict: APPROVE.**

**Strengths:** Muscle Building Intermediate has a clear, self-standing identity — the balanced whole-physique mid-rung of the Muscle Building ladder — that does not depend on any sibling program's absence. Its architecture (Upper/Lower ×2), progression (Double Progression + Volume Accumulation), volume curve (mid-range accumulating), and recovery (Week 9 deload, RPE-regulated) all sit cleanly inside the locked standards (PAS-D7/D8/D11, §11.2) and inherit directly from the family research. It is cleanly distinct from its predecessor and prepares a specific, named progression jump for its successor.

**Weaknesses / open items:** The family's named convergence risk against Lower Body Intermediate is **not fully closed by this Blueprint alone** — by design. This pass establishes the whole-physique baseline and the falsifiable three-part test; closing the risk requires the Lower Body Intermediate Blueprint to demonstrate concentration against that baseline. This is the correct sequencing (the family research recommended authoring both Intermediates early, against each other), and it mirrors how the Strength family's hardest case was only resolved when both sides were authored.

**Confidence:** High for Muscle Building Intermediate's own justification; the residual uncertainty is entirely on the Lower Body Intermediate side and is explicitly handed forward as a test rather than left implicit.

**Recommended next action:** author the **Lower Body Intermediate Blueprint** next, written explicitly against Section 9's three-part test, before either program proceeds to Stage 2 Authoring. Do not declare the Muscle Building family's Stage 1 sequence complete until that Blueprint either passes the test or triggers a remediation decision.
