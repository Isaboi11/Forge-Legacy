### Evidence for "Programs' 1→5 ratio is not a defect"

Pass 1 fixed Goals' original 1→10 gap (10×) and Training's original 1→50 gap (50×) because those were *outlier* ratios — far larger than every other step in their own ladders. Programs' 1→5 (5×) is not an outlier by the same test: it is smaller than the gap Pass 1 already corrected in Goals, and several other already-locked or already-authored ladders contain comparable first-steps (Goals' own corrected ladder still has 1→5 = 5×; Chapters Sealing has 1→5 = 5×). Manufacturing a "3 programs graduated" rung here would not be fixing a gap — it would be adding a rung *because a number was available*, which is the filler this pass is explicitly instructed to avoid.

### Conclusion of Section 1

Of 14 ladders/sub-ladders across the six approved categories, **3 have genuine, evidenced room remaining**: Workout Count (one gap), Chapter Depth (top-end headroom), Chapter Duration (top-end headroom). The other 11 are complete as authored in Pass 1 and are not revisited.

## Section 2 — Remaining Safe Expansion Opportunities

| Category | Opportunity Found? | Detail |
|----------|--------------------|--------|
| **Strength** | **No.** | All five active ladders (Bench, Squat, Deadlift, OHP, Pull-Up) already reach genuinely elite, rare ceilings. Unlike count- or time-based honors, raw lifting capacity does not scale indefinitely with years trained — it has a real physiological ceiling. A 5th tier on any of these ladders (e.g., Bench 500, Squat 600) would be reachable by a vanishingly small, near-professional-powerlifter population, regardless of how many decades the average athlete trains. That fails "every honor must represent a meaningful achievement an athlete would be proud to earn" for nearly everyone — it stops being aspirational and becomes decorative. **A new lift family beyond Overhead Press and Pull-Up was also considered and rejected** — introducing a third new lift (e.g., Barbell Row) was never put through the same Reconciliation scrutiny the approved two families went through; adding it now, unaudited, would be exactly the process shortcut this multi-pass workflow exists to prevent. |
| **Training — Workout Count** | **Yes, one tier.** | The current top end (1000→1500→2500) decelerates correctly, but this pass's explicit "10–20+ year" framing (sharper than Pass 1's looser "decades" framing) justifies one more rung at the very edge of that window: a highly consistent athlete training 4–5×/week for 19–24 years reaches ~5,000 sessions. This is reachable, rare, and meaningfully different from 2,500 — not padding. |
| **Training — Hours Forged** | **No.** | 10,000 hours already carries the "mastery" cultural benchmark established in Pass 1. Extending further (e.g., 15,000) would require a daily training pace sustained for 20+ years — at that point the honor stops rewarding dedication and starts implicitly rewarding overtraining, which conflicts with Product DNA's "Deliberate Interaction... not addictive, not compulsive" UX philosophy. This is a deliberate stop, not an oversight. |
| **Programs — Graduation Volume** | **No.** | 50 programs already sits at the upper edge of the stated 10–20+ year horizon (2–4 programs/year × 12–25 years). Extending to 100 would require 25–50 years, exceeding the design horizon this pass explicitly sets. |
| **Programs — Family Mastery** | **No, unchanged from Pass 1.** | Adding a 5-lineage tier still requires knowing the Program Catalog's current lineage depth, which this pass did not re-verify. Reaffirming an existing, reasoned uncertainty is not the same as finding a new gap — per Rule 9 (carried forward from the first audit), this stays capped at 1/3 until that depth is confirmed. |
| **Goals** | **No.** | The ladder (1/5/10/25/50/100) is already smooth end to end with no outlier ratio, and 100 goals already sits at a realistic 10–20-year ceiling for an actively goal-setting athlete. |
| **Chapters — Sealing** | **No — deliberately capped.** | 50 already required an explicit pacing caveat in Pass 1. Going further would mean encouraging athletes to seal chapters faster just to reach a number, in direct tension with the Product DNA Architecture Guardrail "Chapters Are Sacred... real periods of life." This is the clearest example in the entire catalog of a ceiling that exists for *product-identity* reasons, not architecture reasons. |
| **Chapters — Depth** | **Yes, one tier.** | Depth measures sessions *within* a single chapter, not chapter count — a different, less constrained dimension than Sealing. A single very long, high-frequency chapter (e.g., 2 years at 5–6×/week) can plausibly exceed 600 sessions; a 250-session tier is a natural, reachable next step that rewards sustained intensity inside one life period, the opposite concern from Sealing's "don't rush" problem. |
| **Chapters — Duration** | **Yes, one tier.** | Unlike Sealing, a longer single-chapter duration does not encourage rushing anything — if anything, it rewards the opposite (staying in one chapter longer). A 3-year single chapter is a meaningful, reachable extension within the stated horizon and does not conflict with "Chapters Are Sacred." |
| **Longevity** | **No.** | Already smooth and explicitly capped at 20 years to match this pass's own "10–20+ year" framing. The "+" allows for it, but nothing in the current evidence justifies a named 25-year rung yet — adding one now would be reaching past the stated target rather than responding to a real gap. |

**Net result: 3 genuine opportunities found across 14 ladders.** This is the proof requested by Section 1 — most of the approved-category surface area is already correctly built, not under-built.

## Section 4 — Ceiling Analysis

| Category | Current Ceiling (after Pass 1) | Proposed Ceiling (after Pass 2) | Would Going Further Degrade Quality? |
|----------|-------------------------------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Strength | Bench 405 / Squat 500 / Deadlift 600 / OHP 225 / Pull-Up +100 lbs | **Unchanged.** | **Yes, immediately.** Any 5th tier crosses from "elite recreational" into "competitive powerlifter" territory — reachable by a near-zero share of athletes regardless of years trained. This is a physiological ceiling, not a design choice that can be pushed back with more time. Stop here. |
| Training — Workout Count | 2,500 | **5,000** | Yes, beyond 5,000. A further tier (e.g., 10,000 sessions) would require a pace or career length beyond this pass's own 10–20+ year framing. 5,000 is the correct edge. |
| Training — Hours Forged | 10,000 | **Unchanged.** | Yes, immediately. Further extension starts rewarding pace over dedication, in tension with Product DNA's "Deliberate Interaction" principle. Stop here. |
| Programs — Graduation Volume | 50 | **Unchanged.** | Yes, beyond 50 — requires a career length (25–50 years) beyond the stated horizon. |
| Programs — Family Mastery | 3 lineages | **Unchanged.** | Unknown without verifying current Program Catalog lineage depth — treated as a stop point until that verification happens, not because 3 is provably final. |
| Goals | 100 | **Unchanged.** | Yes, beyond 100 — same career-length-exceeds-horizon reasoning as Programs. |
| Chapters — Sealing | 50 | **Unchanged.** | Yes, immediately — this is the clearest case in the whole catalog. Going further pressures faster chapter-sealing, directly contradicting "Chapters Are Sacred." This ceiling is a product-identity boundary, not a math boundary. |
| Chapters — Depth | 100 | **250** | Yes, beyond 250 — a single chapter exceeding ~3–4 years of sustained high-frequency training starts to strain plausibility even at elite consistency. |
| Chapters — Duration | 2 years | **3 years** | Yes, beyond 3 years — starts to consume a large fraction of the entire stated 10–20-year journey inside one chapter, which stops being "a period of life" and starts being nearly the whole journey. |
| Longevity | 20 years | **Unchanged.** | Not provably impossible to extend, but unjustified by current evidence — the explicit horizon is "10–20+," and nothing supports naming a specific 25-year rung yet. Revisit only if the design horizon itself is explicitly extended. |

**Explicit stop point, stated once for the whole catalog:** every ceiling above was set at the point where one more tier would either (a) become reachable by a non-meaningful sliver of the population, (b) require a career length beyond this pass's own stated 10–20+ year design horizon, or (c) pressure a behavior the Product DNA explicitly protects against (rushing chapters, rewarding pace over deliberateness). All three are quality failures, not architecture failures — exactly the distinction this pass was asked to draw.

## Section 5 — Catalog Saturation Review

| Category | Saturation | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Strength | **Fully exploited.** | All five active ladders complete at 4 tiers each, every ceiling at a real physiological limit. No further honors possible without either redesigning Club (out of scope) or introducing an unaudited new lift family (also out of scope this pass). |
| Training | **Fully exploited** (as of this pass). | Workout Count's one remaining gap is now closed (5,000). Hours Forged was already complete. No further evidenced opportunities remain. |
| Programs | **Mostly exploited.** | Graduation Volume is complete. Family Mastery has one genuinely open question (current lineage depth) that this pass did not resolve — the only category left with a named, specific follow-up rather than a closed door. |
| Goals | **Fully exploited.** | Smooth, complete ladder, correctly capped to the stated horizon. |
| Chapters | **Fully exploited** (as of this pass). | Sealing was already deliberately capped; Depth and Duration's remaining headroom is now closed (250, 3 years). All three sub-ladders are now internally consistent with each other's pacing logic. |
| Longevity | **Fully exploited.** | Smooth, complete, correctly capped to the stated horizon. |

**Overall verdict: the six approved categories are now fully exploited, with one explicitly named open item (Program Family Mastery's lineage-depth question) rather than any unresolved gap.** This is not a claim that no more honors could ever theoretically exist in these categories — it is a claim that no *more honors meeting this pass's own quality bar* (meaningful, non-filler, architecture-compatible, horizon-appropriate) currently exist to be authored.

**Estimated remaining capacity inside the six approved categories before quality begins declining: effectively zero**, with one specific exception (Program Family Mastery, pending lineage-depth verification — likely 0–2 additional honors at most, not a meaningful pool). This is the headline finding of this pass: **the approved categories are saturated.** Any further material growth in catalog depth — to approach the original ~220-honor long-term target — can now only come from resolving the named blockers on the still-reserved categories:

- **Community** — needs new squad-participation statistics (no architecture today)
- **Consistency** — needs the Product DNA Decision Test run explicitly, plus new streak/frequency statistics
- **Comebacks & Resilience** — same Product DNA gate, plus new absence-detection statistics
- **Endurance & Conditioning** — needs an independent roadmap decision on cardio/distance logging (no exercise category exists for it today)
- **Prestige** — needs concrete, non-vague multi-condition criteria before any duplicate/threshold check is even possible

This pass deliberately stops here rather than reaching into any of those five — exactly as instructed. The next material expansion of the Honors Catalog requires decisions outside this catalog's own architecture, not more authoring inside it.
