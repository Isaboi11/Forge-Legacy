## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Hybrid Intermediate is a **concurrent-training** program: it develops **maximal strength and conditioning capacity simultaneously**, as **co-primary** goals (`BUILD_STRENGTH` = "1RM, compound lift focus"; `IMPROVE_CONDITIONING` = "work capacity, anaerobic threshold" — `Program-Catalog-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.4). Neither quality is subordinate to the other, which makes the program's defining challenge the **interference effect** — strength and conditioning compete for recovery, so the program must be deliberately structured to develop both without one blunting the other. This is a programming concern neither sibling Intermediate has (each develops a single primary quality). It is the program for the "hybrid athlete" — tactical, field-sport, or general athletes who must be both strong and conditioned.

### Target Athlete

- **Hybrid athlete** (`Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §5.4) who has graduated **Hybrid Foundation** or has equivalent concurrent-training experience.
- Wants genuine strength **and** genuine conditioning at the same time — not work-capacity alone (Conditioning Intermediate), not body composition in a deficit (Body Recomposition Intermediate), not pure maximal strength (Strength family).
- Has a resistance-training base (carried from Hybrid Foundation) and access to a MIXED environment (gym for strength + outdoor/field for conditioning).

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary goal is work capacity only | This program devotes co-primary emphasis to maximal strength | **Conditioning Intermediate** (Sort 14) |
| Primary goal is fat loss + muscle retention | No deficit orientation; resistance is strength (low-rep) not hypertrophy (8–15) | **Body Recomposition Intermediate** (Sort 15) |
| Primary goal is maximal strength only | Conditioning is a co-primary, not an afterthought; CONDITIONING category | Strength family |
| New to concurrent training | Managing two stimuli at intermediate intensity assumes a base | Hybrid Foundation (Sort 16) |

### Expected Outcomes

Simultaneous, measurable improvement in **both** maximal strength (compound-lift load progression) **and** conditioning (work capacity / performance) over the block — the concurrent-training outcome. Neither quality regresses; the interference effect is managed, not ignored.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Strength and conditioning are **both** developed as primary qualities (visible dedicated work for each), not one serving the other.
- Strength work is genuine maximal-strength structure (heavy compounds, lower reps, longer rest) with progressive overload (QC-2).
- Conditioning is performance-oriented and progressed.
- Strength and hard conditioning are **sequenced to manage interference** (not stacked into mutual compromise).
- WARM_UP + COOL_DOWN each session (PAS-D9); `restSeconds` populated (PAS §11.3).

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 10 weeks (PAS-D7 → one deload).
- **Frequency:** 4 sessions/week.
- **Weekly structure (MIXED environment enables modality separation):** the four sessions are split between **dedicated strength** sessions (gym: heavy compound work) and **dedicated conditioning** sessions (outdoor/field/mixed: performance conditioning) — e.g., 2 strength + 2 conditioning, sequenced so strength and hard conditioning do not fall on consecutive days. This **separation of modalities across sessions** is structurally distinct from the GYM-bound *combined* strength+cardio sessions of both sibling Intermediates, and is what makes managing the interference effect possible. (Exact slot pattern fixed at Stage 2.)
- **Deload:** one, Week 9 (slots 33–36), peak Week 10 (slots 37–40), per PAS §14 — reduces both strength load and conditioning volume while maintaining four sessions (PAS-D8).
- **Periodization summary:** a single block in which strength (load/rep progression on compounds) and conditioning (performance progression) advance in parallel, with volume on each deliberately balanced so the interference effect does not stall either. Double Progression governs both (Section 6).

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). **CONDITIONING requires both WARM_UP and COOL_DOWN** (PAS-D9). Rest and structure per PAS §11.3 (`restSeconds` always populated; circuits named in slots).

- **Strength sessions (gym):** WARM_UP (dynamic + ramp-up) → MAIN: heavy compound lifts first (lower reps, longer rest for genuine strength development), supporting accessories → COOL_DOWN. Within PAS-D11 (4–8 ex / 12–24 sets) and 30–60 min.
- **Conditioning sessions (outdoor/field/mixed):** WARM_UP → MAIN: performance conditioning (intervals, mixed-modal work) → COOL_DOWN.

**§11.3 background nuance (noted, not a blocker):** PAS §11.3 cautions against "complex barbell lifts without adequate instruction — CONDITIONING athletes may not have a strength training background." That caution targets *pure-conditioning beginners*; the Hybrid athlete (graduated Hybrid Foundation) **has** the requisite strength base, so genuine compound strength work is appropriate here. The scope distinction is worth stating so the program's heavy-compound content is not mistaken for a §11.3 violation.

---

## Section 4 — Strength Emphasis

**Genuine maximal-strength development as a co-primary goal** (BUILD_STRENGTH per §3.4 — "1RM, compound lift focus"): heavy compound lifts, lower rep ranges (~4–8), longer inter-set rest (toward the upper end of §10.3 INTERMEDIATE, 90–180s), progressive overload. This is categorically different from:
- Conditioning Intermediate's resistance (sub-hypertrophic, conditioning-density, short rest, in service of conditioning — no strength goal), and
- Body Recomposition Intermediate's resistance (hypertrophy rep ranges ~8–15, moderate load, in a deficit, for muscle retention).

Hybrid's resistance is the only one of the three aimed at **maximal strength**, and the only one prescribed at heavy/low-rep/long-rest structure.

---

## Section 5 — Conditioning Emphasis

**Performance-oriented conditioning as a co-primary goal** (IMPROVE_CONDITIONING): work capacity and conditioning performance, developed in the MIXED environment (often outdoors/field). This differs from:
- Body Recomposition Intermediate's conditioning (caloric-output-oriented, deficit-sustainable — a fat-loss tool, not a performance goal), and
- Conditioning Intermediate's conditioning (the program's sole spine, with resistance subordinate).

In Hybrid, conditioning is co-equal with strength — neither subordinate nor caloric — and the MIXED environment lets it be programmed separately from the gym strength work.

---

## Section 6 — Progression Strategy

- **Double Progression (PAS §7.2, Model 2)** applied to **both** qualities: load/rep progression on the strength compounds (lower rep ranges), and performance progression on the conditioning.
- **Interference-aware balancing:** the defining progression challenge is advancing both without either stalling. Volume on each is managed so the concurrent stimulus remains recoverable; if one quality plateaus, the family two-stage failure response applies to that quality without overloading the other.
- **RPE** permitted (PAS-D3, INTERMEDIATE) in `notes` to autoregulate strength effort against accumulated conditioning fatigue (and vice versa).

The progression *headline* is parallel development of two competing qualities — neither sibling's single-quality progression headline (work density for Conditioning Intermediate; resistance overload-in-a-deficit for Body Recomposition Intermediate).

---

## Section 7 — Recovery Strategy

- **Concurrent-training recovery is the signature consideration.** Two demanding stimuli mean high total recovery load; the program manages it by **modality separation** (MIXED environment lets strength and conditioning occupy different sessions/days) and by **interference-aware sequencing** (heavy strength and hard conditioning not stacked back-to-back).
- **Deload Week 9** reduces both strength load and conditioning volume, maintains frequency (PAS-D8).
- Adequate rest on strength compounds (90–180s, §10.3) for genuine strength development; `restSeconds` populated throughout (§11.3).

Neither sibling manages an interference effect, because neither develops two competing primary qualities — this recovery logic is unique to Hybrid.

---

## Section 8 — Expected Outcomes

Concurrent gains in maximal strength and conditioning over the 10-week block, with the interference effect managed so both advance. The athlete finishes stronger *and* better conditioned — an outcome neither single-emphasis sibling targets.

## Section 9 — Distinction Rationale & Non-Collapse Checks

**Independent justification:** Hybrid Intermediate is justified by a distinct **athlete + goal + environment + intent**: the Hybrid athlete pursuing concurrent strength and conditioning, in a MIXED environment, managing the interference effect. It is distinct from its predecessor (Hybrid Foundation) by level and intermediate strength/conditioning load.

**Non-collapse check 1 — NOT "Conditioning Intermediate with heavier lifting."** Conditioning Intermediate has **no strength goal**; its resistance is conditioning-serving, sub-hypertrophic, density-progressed, short-rest, GYM-bound. Hybrid develops **maximal strength as a co-primary goal** (heavy/low-rep/long-rest compounds + BUILD_STRENGTH alignment + MIXED environment + interference management). You cannot reach Hybrid by adding load to Conditioning Intermediate: doing so adds neither the strength-development programming (low-rep/long-rest progression), nor the strength goal, nor the MIXED environment, nor the concurrent-training recovery logic. Different program.

**Non-collapse check 2 — NOT "Body Recomposition Intermediate with harder conditioning."** Body Recomposition Intermediate's resistance is **hypertrophy** (8–15 reps, moderate load) in a **caloric deficit**, with conditioning for caloric output. Hybrid's resistance is **maximal strength** (heavy, low-rep), with **no deficit**, and conditioning for **performance**. Making Body Recomposition's conditioning harder still leaves hypertrophy-resistance-in-a-deficit — it changes neither the resistance goal/structure, nor the deficit orientation, nor the GYM→MIXED environment, nor the goal alignment. Different program.

Hybrid Intermediate shares goal alignment with **neither** sibling and environment with **neither** sibling — it is the most over-determined-as-distinct of the three Intermediates.

## Section 10 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE — Hybrid Intermediate stands independently.**

It is differentiated from both sibling Intermediates on three independent axes — **goal alignment** (BUILD_STRENGTH + IMPROVE_CONDITIONING, shared with neither), **environment** (MIXED, the family's only one), and **training intent** (concurrent strength + conditioning with interference management) — and it passes both non-collapse checks decisively. There is **no convergence risk**, **no revision needed**, and **no retirement** warranted. This is the easiest of the three Intermediate justifications precisely because its distinction is over-determined; the Conditioning family's only genuine convergence question was the Conditioning Intermediate ↔ Body Recomposition Intermediate pair, already resolved.
