## Section 1 — Lying Leg Curl

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Lying Leg Curl |
| Category | LEGS_AND_GLUTES |
| Movement Pattern | KNEE_ISOLATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | HAMSTRINGS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Lying Leg Curl is a machine-based knee-isolation exercise that trains the hamstrings through a face-down leg-curling motion. It uses a resistance pad positioned across the back of the lower legs. It isolates knee flexion without involving the hips.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Lying Leg Curl builds hamstring strength and size in a focused, single-joint movement. It offers a different body position than the Seated Leg Curl, which some lifters find more comfortable or effective for full hamstring engagement. It also allows precise load control useful for rehabilitation or working around a knee limitation. It is a common accessory exercise alongside squat and hinge training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie face down on the machine with your knees just off the edge of the pad.
2. Position the resistance pad against the back of your ankles.
3. Grip the side handles and brace your core.
4. Curl your legs up toward your glutes against the resistance.
5. Pause briefly at the top of the curl.
6. Lower the weight back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips pressed into the pad throughout
> — Curl through a full range of motion rather than a short partial range
> — Lower back to the start slowly rather than letting it snap back
> — Keep your upper body relaxed and still as your legs curl

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips lift off the pad to help move the weight
> — The weight snaps back quickly instead of returning under control
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping well short of full flexion

**Progressions:** Nordic Hamstring Curl
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern machine variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Seated Leg Curl, Barbell Romanian Deadlift

## Section 2 — Standing Calf Raise

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Standing Calf Raise |
| Category | LEGS_AND_GLUTES |
| Movement Pattern | KNEE_ISOLATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CALVES |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Standing Calf Raise is a machine-based isolation exercise that trains the calves through a standing heel-raise motion. It uses a shoulder-loaded platform that allows the heels to rise and fall freely. It trains the calves through a full standing range of motion.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Standing Calf Raise builds calf strength and size in a focused, single-joint movement performed under load. It is well suited for lifters wanting to add direct calf volume to a lower-body program. It also reinforces the ankle strength used in running, jumping, and walking. It is one of the most common dedicated calf-building exercises in commercial gyms.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Position your shoulders under the pads of the machine.
2. Place the balls of your feet on the platform edge, heels hanging off.
3. Stand tall and brace your core.
4. Rise up onto your toes as high as possible.
5. Pause briefly at the top of the raise.
6. Lower your heels back down below the platform under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Rise as high as possible on each rep rather than using a short range
> — Lower your heels below the platform to get a full stretch
> — Keep your knees nearly straight throughout the movement
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than bouncing

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The range of motion shortens, skipping the bottom stretch
> — The knees bend to help bounce the weight up
> — The movement speeds up into a bouncing rhythm instead of staying controlled

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern machine variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Wall Calf Raise
**Alternatives:** Seated Calf Raise, Single-Leg Calf Raise

## Section 5 — Cable Kickback

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Cable Kickback |
| Category | LEGS_AND_GLUTES |
| Movement Pattern | KNEE_ISOLATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | GLUTES |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HAMSTRINGS |

**ABOUT**
> The Cable Kickback is a cable-based isolation exercise that trains the glutes by extending one leg backward against cable resistance from an ankle cuff. It isolates hip extension on one leg at a time. It is performed standing, facing the cable machine.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Cable Kickback builds focused glute strength under constant cable tension, which keeps the muscle working throughout the entire range of motion. It is well suited for lifters wanting to add direct, isolated glute volume after compound squat and hinge work. It also allows each leg to be trained independently, helping correct side-to-side imbalances. It is a common finishing exercise in glute-focused lower-body programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach an ankle cuff to a low cable pulley and secure it around one ankle.
2. Face the machine and hold the frame for balance.
3. Brace your core and keep a slight bend in your standing knee.
4. Kick your working leg straight back against the cable resistance.
5. Squeeze your glute at the end of the movement.
6. Return your leg back to the starting position under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Squeeze your glute at the end of each kick rather than just moving your leg
> — Keep your torso still rather than swinging to help the kick
> — Move through a controlled range rather than rushing the rep
> — Keep your standing leg stable throughout the set

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans forward to help drive the leg back
> — The lower back arches to add range instead of the glute working harder
> — The working leg swings using momentum instead of a controlled squeeze

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Glute Bridge, Single-Leg Hip Thrust

## Section 6 — Nordic Hamstring Curl

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Nordic Hamstring Curl |
| Category | LEGS_AND_GLUTES |
| Movement Pattern | KNEE_ISOLATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | HAMSTRINGS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Nordic Hamstring Curl is a bodyweight isolation exercise that trains the hamstrings through a slow, controlled forward lowering from kneeling with the ankles anchored. It trains the hamstrings to resist knee extension under body-weight load. It is one of the most demanding hamstring exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Nordic Hamstring Curl builds eccentric hamstring strength that has strong evidence for reducing hamstring strain risk in athletes. It trains the hamstrings under a much greater relative load than any machine leg curl, since the athlete controls their entire body weight. It is well suited only for athletes who have already built a base of hamstring strength on machine leg curls. It is a benchmark exercise for advanced hamstring training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Kneel on a padded surface with your ankles anchored or held by a partner.
2. Keep your torso and hips in a straight line.
3. Brace your core and slowly lower your torso forward.
4. Resist the fall for as long as possible using your hamstrings.
5. Catch yourself with your hands once you can no longer control the descent.
6. Push back up to the starting kneeling position.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Lower as slowly as you can control rather than dropping quickly
> — Keep your hips extended throughout the descent
> — Use your hands to assist back up if your hamstrings give out
> — Keep your body in a straight line from knees to shoulders

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips bend during the descent instead of staying extended
> — The lowering speeds up suddenly instead of staying controlled
> — The athlete attempts this without first building hamstring strength on machine curls

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern bodyweight variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Seated Leg Curl
**Alternatives:** Lying Leg Curl, Barbell Romanian Deadlift

## Section 7 — Sissy Squat

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Sissy Squat |
| Category | LEGS_AND_GLUTES |
| Movement Pattern | KNEE_ISOLATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | QUADS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Sissy Squat is a bodyweight isolation exercise that trains the quads by leaning back onto the toes while bending only at the knees. It isolates knee extension while keeping the hips extended. It is one of the most demanding quad exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Sissy Squat builds quad strength and a deep stretch under load using only body weight. It trains knee extension in near-isolation, since the hips stay extended throughout the movement. It is well suited only for athletes who have already built a base of quad strength on machine leg extensions. It is a strong benchmark exercise for advanced bodyweight quad training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding onto a stable support for balance.
2. Rise onto the balls of your feet.
3. Lean your torso back while bending only at your knees.
4. Lower until you feel a deep stretch across the front of your thighs.
5. Keep your hips extended throughout the descent.
6. Push back up to the starting position using your quads.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips extended rather than bending at the waist
> — Lower only as far as you can control with your quads
> — Use the support for balance rather than to pull yourself up
> — Move slowly through both the lowering and rising phases

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips bend forward, turning the movement into a regular squat
> — The support is used to pull the body back up instead of the quads
> — The descent speeds up instead of staying slow and controlled

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern bodyweight variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Leg Extension
**Alternatives:** Banded Leg Extension, Wall Sit

**ABOUT**

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Banded Leg Extension builds quad strength using minimal equipment, making it a practical option for home training or travel. It is well suited as an accessible entry point before progressing to a machine Leg Extension. It also allows precise load control useful for rehabilitation or working around a knee limitation. It is a flexible accessory exercise for any quad-focused training goal.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit on a chair or bench with the band anchored behind you at ankle height.
2. Loop the band around one ankle.
3. Grip the seat and brace your core.
4. Extend your knee to straighten your leg against the band's resistance.
5. Pause briefly at full extension.
6. Bend your knee back to the starting position under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Extend until your leg is straight without locking your knee aggressively
> — Keep your hips still as your knee extends
> — Lower back slowly rather than letting the band snap your leg back
> — Keep your upper body relaxed throughout the movement

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips shift to help generate momentum
> — The band snaps the leg back quickly instead of returning under control
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full extension

**Progressions:** Leg Extension
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Adductor Machine builds inner-thigh strength in a focused, single-joint movement. It also supports hip health alongside abductor training to keep the hip musculature balanced. It is a common accessory exercise in lower-body and athletic-conditioning programs.

> — Keep your back flat against the pad throughout
> — Keep the movement smooth rather than jerking the weight

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The pads snap open quickly instead of returning under control
> — The hips shift in the seat to help move the weight
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full adduction
