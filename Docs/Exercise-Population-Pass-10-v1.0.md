## Section 1 — Dumbbell Shoulder Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Dumbbell Shoulder Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Dumbbell Shoulder Press is a vertical pressing exercise performed standing, pressing a dumbbell in each hand from shoulder height to full overhead extension. It trains the shoulders and triceps while standing demands additional core and full-body stability. It is performed without back support.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dumbbell Shoulder Press builds shoulder strength and total-body stability since the standing position requires bracing through the whole body. It allows each arm to work independently, addressing side-to-side strength imbalances. It is well suited for lifters who have built a base of pressing strength on the Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press. It pairs well with squat and hinge training as part of a complete strength program.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, holding a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder height.
2. Brace your core and squeeze your glutes.
3. Press both dumbbells straight up until your arms are fully extended.
4. Bring the dumbbells close together at the top without touching.
5. Lower the dumbbells back down to shoulder height under control.
6. Keep your torso upright throughout the lift.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core hard before each rep
> — Press the dumbbells in a straight line rather than out to the sides
> — Squeeze your glutes to avoid leaning back excessively
> — Lower both dumbbells at the same pace

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back arches excessively to help the dumbbells travel overhead
> — The dumbbells drift forward in front of the head
> — Only one arm reaches full extension at the top of the rep

**Progressions:** Arnold Press
**Regressions:** Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press
**Alternatives:** Shoulder Press Machine, Cable Overhead Press

## Section 2 — Push Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Push Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BARBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Push Press is a vertical pressing exercise that uses a slight leg dip and drive to help send the bar overhead. It trains the shoulders, triceps, and legs together in one explosive movement. It allows heavier loads than a strict Overhead Press by adding leg drive.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Push Press builds explosive overhead pressing power by training the legs and shoulders to work together. It carries over to athletic movements that require driving force upward through the whole body. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the strict Overhead Press and want to train heavier loads or more explosive power. It also bridges strength training and athletic power development.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, holding the bar at shoulder height.
2. Brace your core and dip your knees slightly.
3. Drive through your legs explosively as you press the bar overhead.
4. Extend your arms fully as the bar clears your head.
5. Lower the bar back to shoulder height under control.
6. Reset your stance before the next rep.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the leg dip short and quick rather than a full squat
> — Drive through your whole foot as your legs extend
> — Time the arm press to follow right after the leg drive
> — Keep the bar path close to your face on the way up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The leg dip turns into a deep squat instead of a short dip
> — The lower back arches excessively to help the bar travel overhead
> — The arms start pressing before the legs finish driving

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern barbell variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Overhead Press
**Alternatives:** Dumbbell Shoulder Press, Kettlebell Overhead Press

## Section 3 — Pike Push-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Pike Push-Up |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Pike Push-Up is a bodyweight pressing exercise performed in an inverted-V position, bending the elbows to lower the head toward the floor. It trains the shoulders and triceps through a vertical pressing angle using only body weight. It is one of the more approachable vertical-press bodyweight exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Pike Push-Up builds shoulder pressing strength using only body weight, training a vertical pressing angle that a standard Push-Up does not emphasize. It is well suited for athletes building toward a Handstand Push-Up without equipment. It also serves as a useful shoulder-pressing option when no dumbbells or barbells are available. It requires no equipment beyond open floor space.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Start in a plank position, then walk your feet toward your hands.
2. Lift your hips up so your body forms an inverted V.
3. Brace your core and keep your legs relatively straight.
4. Bend your elbows to lower your head toward the floor.
5. Press through your hands to return to the starting position.
6. Keep your hips high throughout the movement.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips lifted high throughout the entire movement
> — Lower your head toward a spot just in front of your hands
> — Keep your elbows tracking at a moderate angle rather than flaring wide
> — Press evenly through both hands as you push back up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips drop, turning the movement into a regular push-up
> — The head lowers too far forward instead of toward the floor
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of a full lower

**Progressions:** Handstand Push-Up
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern bodyweight variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Dumbbell Shoulder Press

## Section 4 — Arnold Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Arnold Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Arnold Press is a vertical pressing exercise that adds a rotation of the dumbbells as they travel from in front of the shoulders to overhead. It trains the shoulders through a wider range of motion than a standard dumbbell press. It is named for the rotating press style popularized by bodybuilder Arnold Schwarzenegger.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Arnold Press builds shoulder strength through a more complete range of motion than a fixed-grip press, since the rotation works the shoulder from a front-facing position through to an overhead lockout. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the standard Dumbbell Shoulder Press and want a more demanding shoulder variation. It also adds rotator-cuff engagement that a straight press does not emphasize as directly. It is a popular accessory exercise in shoulder-focused hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit or stand holding a dumbbell in each hand in front of your shoulders, palms facing you.
2. Brace your core and begin pressing the dumbbells upward.
3. Rotate your palms to face forward as the dumbbells rise.
4. Press until your arms are fully extended overhead.
5. Reverse the rotation as you lower the dumbbells back down.
6. Return to the starting position with palms facing you.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the rotation smooth and continuous throughout the press
> — Press in a straight line rather than letting the dumbbells drift forward
> — Brace your core throughout, especially if performing this standing
> — Lower at the same pace you pressed up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The rotation is rushed or skipped instead of flowing with the press
> — The lower back arches excessively to help the dumbbells travel overhead
> — Only one arm completes the rotation at the same pace as the other

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern dumbbell variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Dumbbell Shoulder Press
**Alternatives:** Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press, Cable Overhead Press

## Section 5 — Shoulder Press Machine

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Shoulder Press Machine |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Shoulder Press Machine is a vertical pressing exercise performed on a selectorized machine, pressing the handles from shoulder height to full overhead extension. It trains the shoulders and triceps along a fixed path. The machine supports the back and removes the need to balance free weights.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Shoulder Press Machine builds shoulder pressing strength without requiring the balance and bracing skills a standing barbell or dumbbell press demands. It is well suited for beginners learning the overhead pressing pattern or for lifters training alone without a spotter. It also offers a safer heavy-pressing option for athletes managing a balance or lower-back limitation. It is a common accessory exercise in beginner and hypertrophy-focused programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit in the machine with your back against the pad.
2. Grip the handles at shoulder height.
3. Brace your core and press the handles straight up.
4. Extend your arms fully at the top of the press.
5. Lower the handles back to shoulder height under control.
6. Avoid locking your elbows aggressively at full extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat against the pad throughout
> — Press evenly through both hands as you push up
> — Control the return rather than letting the weight drop
> — Adjust the seat so the handles align with your shoulders

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The back lifts off the pad to help drive the handles up
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full extension
> — The weight drops quickly on the return instead of lowering under control

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern machine variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern machine variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Cable Overhead Press, Dumbbell Shoulder Press

## Section 6 — Cable Overhead Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Cable Overhead Press |
| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Cable Overhead Press is a vertical pressing exercise performed on a low cable pulley, pressing the handle from shoulder height to full overhead extension. It trains the shoulders and triceps under constant cable tension. It can be performed standing or kneeling.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Cable Overhead Press builds shoulder pressing strength under constant tension throughout the full range of motion, unlike free weights. It is well suited for lifters looking for a joint-friendly pressing option with a controlled, guided path. It also offers a useful substitute for athletes managing a shoulder limitation that free-weight overhead pressing aggravates. It is a common accessory exercise in shoulder-focused programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach a handle to a low cable pulley and grip it in one hand.
2. Stand or kneel facing away from the machine.
3. Brace your core and press the handle straight overhead.
4. Extend your arm fully at the top of the press.
5. Lower the handle back to shoulder height under control.
6. Repeat for the set, then switch sides.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core to avoid leaning to the working side
> — Press in a straight line rather than letting the handle drift forward
> — Squeeze your shoulder at the top of each rep
> — Control the return rather than letting the cable snap back

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans away from the working arm during the press
> — The lower back arches excessively to help the handle travel overhead
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full extension

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Shoulder Press Machine, Arnold Press

**ABOUT**
> The Kettlebell Overhead Press is a vertical pressing exercise performed by pressing a kettlebell from shoulder height to full overhead extension. It trains the shoulders and triceps while the kettlebell's offset weight demands extra stabilization. It is typically performed one arm at a time.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Kettlebell Overhead Press builds shoulder pressing strength along with the extra stabilization demand created by the kettlebell's offset center of mass. It is well suited for lifters already training with kettlebells who want to add a pressing movement to their routine. It also reinforces core bracing, since the uneven load challenges the trunk during a one-arm press. It requires only a single kettlebell.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding a kettlebell at shoulder height, resting against your forearm.
2. Brace your core and squeeze your glutes.
3. Press the kettlebell straight up until your arm is fully extended.
4. Keep your wrist stacked under the kettlebell at the top.
5. Lower the kettlebell back to shoulder height under control.
6. Repeat for the set, then switch sides.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core to avoid leaning to the working side
> — Keep your wrist stacked directly under the kettlebell throughout
> — Press in a straight line rather than letting the kettlebell drift forward
> — Squeeze your glutes to avoid leaning back excessively

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans away from the working arm during the press
> — The wrist drifts behind the kettlebell, losing the stacked position
> — The lower back arches excessively to help the kettlebell travel overhead

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern kettlebell variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern kettlebell variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.

## Section 8 — Handstand Push-Up

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

| Category | PUSH |
| Movement Pattern | PUSH_VERTICAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Handstand Push-Up is a bodyweight pressing exercise performed upside down against a wall, bending the elbows to lower the head toward the floor. It trains the shoulders and triceps by pressing the entire body weight overhead. It is one of the most demanding pressing exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Handstand Push-Up builds shoulder pressing strength relative to body weight, training the same overhead pressing pattern as a barbell Overhead Press without any equipment. It is well suited for athletes who have already mastered the Pike Push-Up and built significant shoulder strength. It also serves as a long-term bodyweight strength benchmark. It is a staple exercise for advanced bodyweight upper-body training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Kick up into a handstand against a wall, hands shoulder-width apart.
2. Brace your core and keep your body in a straight line.
3. Bend your elbows to lower your head toward the floor.
4. Lower until your head lightly touches the floor.
5. Press through your hands to return to the starting position.
6. Keep your legs against the wall for support throughout.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your core braced to maintain a straight body line
> — Lower under control rather than dropping quickly
> — Keep your elbows tracking at a moderate angle rather than flaring wide
> — Use the wall for support and balance throughout

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The body line bows or arches during the lower
> — The descent speeds up instead of staying slow and controlled
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping well short of the floor

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Pike Push-Up
**Alternatives:** Overhead Press

| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Dumbbell Lateral Raise is an isolation exercise performed by raising a dumbbell in each hand out to the sides until level with the shoulders. It trains the side shoulders specifically. It is performed standing with a slight, fixed bend in the elbows.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dumbbell Lateral Raise builds shoulder width and size by isolating the side shoulder muscle, which pressing movements train less directly. It is well suited as an accessory exercise to add shoulder-specific volume after pressing work. It also supports balanced shoulder development alongside front-and-rear-focused pressing and pulling exercises. It is one of the most common shoulder accessory exercises in hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding a dumbbell in each hand at your sides.
2. Keep a slight, fixed bend in your elbows.
3. Raise both dumbbells out to the sides until level with your shoulders.
4. Pause briefly at the top of the raise.
5. Lower the dumbbells back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Lead with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Raise only to shoulder height rather than above it
> — Keep the bend in your elbows fixed throughout the movement
> — Lower with control rather than letting the weight drop

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans side to side to help lift the weight
> — The dumbbells rise above shoulder height, shifting tension off the side shoulder
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears during the raise

| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Triceps Pressdown is an isolation exercise performed on a cable machine, pressing a bar or rope attachment down from chest height to full arm extension. It trains the triceps specifically. It is performed standing, facing the cable machine.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Triceps Pressdown builds triceps size and strength in a focused, single-joint movement under constant cable tension. It is well suited as an accessory exercise to add triceps-specific volume after pressing work. It also allows precise load control, making it accessible across a wide range of training levels. It is one of the most common triceps accessory exercises in hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach a bar or rope to a high cable pulley and grip it with both hands.
2. Stand facing the machine with your elbows tucked at your sides.
3. Brace your core and keep your upper arms still.
4. Press the attachment down until your arms are fully extended.
5. Pause briefly at full extension.
6. Let the attachment rise back up under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your elbows pinned at your sides throughout
> — Press with your forearms only, not your shoulders
> — Squeeze your triceps at the bottom of each rep
> — Control the return rather than letting the weight snap back

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The elbows drift forward away from the torso during the press
> — The torso leans forward to help push the weight down
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full extension

| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS, CORE |

**ABOUT**
> The Half-Kneeling Landmine Press is a diagonal pressing exercise performed from a half-kneeling position, pressing one end of a barbell anchored in a landmine attachment away from the chest. It trains the shoulders and chest along the same diagonal path as the standing Landmine Press. The kneeling stance removes leg drive and adds a core-stability demand.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Half-Kneeling Landmine Press builds pressing strength along a shoulder-friendly diagonal path while the kneeling stance removes the ability to use the legs to help the lift. It is well suited for lifters wanting to isolate the upper body's contribution to the Landmine Press. It also builds core and hip stability, since the half-kneeling position challenges the trunk to stay square. It pairs well with the standing Landmine Press in a complete pressing program.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Load one end of a barbell into a landmine attachment.
2. Kneel on one knee with the opposite foot planted in front of you.
3. Hold the loaded end of the bar at chest height with one hand.
4. Brace your core and keep your hips square.
5. Press the bar up and away from your chest along its natural arc.
6. Lower the bar back to chest height under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips square throughout the press
> — Brace your core before each rep to resist leaning
> — Let the bar travel along its natural diagonal arc
> — Press with control rather than rushing the lockout

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips rotate to help drive the bar up
> — The torso leans to the working side during the press
> — The bar path drifts away from its natural diagonal arc

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern barbell variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.

**ABOUT**
> The Dumbbell Squeeze Press is a diagonal pressing exercise performed lying on a bench, pressing two dumbbells together while extending the arms. It trains the chest and shoulders while the inward squeeze adds extra chest activation. The dumbbells stay in contact throughout the movement.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Dumbbell Squeeze Press builds chest activation through the inward pressing force, which a standard press does not emphasize. It is well suited as an accessible diagonal-press accessory exercise requiring only dumbbells and a bench. It also offers a joint-friendly pressing option since the squeeze encourages a controlled, stable bar path. It is a useful entry point before progressing to the Landmine Press.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie back on the bench holding two dumbbells together above your chest.
2. Press the dumbbells firmly against each other throughout.
3. Lower the dumbbells toward your chest while maintaining the squeeze.
4. Press the dumbbells back up to full extension.
5. Keep the dumbbells in contact throughout the entire set.
6. Squeeze your chest at the top of each rep.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Press the dumbbells together firmly throughout the entire set
> — Lower under control rather than letting the dumbbells drop
> — Keep your elbows at a moderate angle rather than flaring wide
> — Squeeze your chest at the top of each rep

**WATCH OUT FOR**

| Movement Pattern | PUSH_DIAGONAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | TRX |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CHEST |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS, SHOULDERS, CORE |

**ABOUT**
> The TRX Push-Up is a diagonal pressing exercise performed with the hands gripping suspended straps instead of a fixed floor. It trains the chest, shoulders, and core, since the unstable straps demand extra stabilization. It is the only suspension-based pressing exercise in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The TRX Push-Up builds pressing strength along with significant core and shoulder stabilization, since the suspended handles can move in any direction. It is well suited for athletes who have mastered the standard Push-Up and want an added stability challenge. It also allows the difficulty to be adjusted easily by changing body angle relative to the anchor point. It requires only a TRX or other suspension trainer.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Set the straps to roughly mid-length and grip a handle in each hand.
2. Lean forward into a plank position with your arms extended.
3. Brace your core and keep your body in a straight line.
4. Lower your chest toward your hands by bending your elbows.
5. Press through your hands to push your body back up.
6. Keep the handles steady throughout the movement.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your body in a straight line throughout the movement
> — Keep the handles close to your body rather than letting them drift apart
> — Adjust your foot position to change the difficulty as needed
> — Press evenly through both hands as you push up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips sag or pike instead of staying in a straight line
> — The handles drift apart or swing during the movement
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full elbow extension

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
