## Section 1 — Seated Cable Row

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Seated Cable Row |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Seated Cable Row is a horizontal pulling exercise performed seated at a cable machine, pulling a handle toward the torso. It trains the back, biceps, and rear shoulders under constant cable tension. It is performed with the legs extended and feet braced against a platform.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Seated Cable Row builds back thickness and pulling strength under constant tension throughout the full range of motion, unlike free weights. It is well suited for lifters wanting a controlled, guided pulling path. It also allows easy load adjustment, making it accessible across a wide range of training levels. It is a staple back-building exercise in nearly every pulling program.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit at the cable row machine with your feet braced against the platform.
2. Grip the handle with both hands and extend your arms.
3. Brace your core and sit tall.
4. Pull the handle toward your lower ribs.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull.
6. Extend your arms back out under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Pull with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Keep your torso tall throughout the pull
> — Avoid leaning back excessively to move the weight
> — Control the return rather than letting the cable snap your arms forward

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans back sharply to help pull the handle in
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears during the pull
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of full arm extension

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Cable Row Machine, Dumbbell Row

## Section 2 — T-Bar Row

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | T-Bar Row |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BARBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The T-Bar Row is a horizontal pulling exercise performed straddling a loaded bar anchored at one end, pulling the handles toward the torso. It trains the back, biceps, and rear shoulders. The anchored bar provides a fixed, supported pulling path.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The T-Bar Row builds back thickness and pulling strength while the anchored bar path offers more stability than a free-standing Barbell Row. It is well suited for lifters wanting to add heavy, focused back volume after compound rowing work. It also allows a slightly more upright torso position, which some lifters find more comfortable under heavy load. It is a common accessory exercise in back-focused hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Straddle the anchored bar with a V-handle or grip attachment loaded.
2. Hinge forward at your hips, keeping your back flat.
3. Grip the handles and let your arms hang fully extended.
4. Pull the handles up toward your torso.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull.
6. Lower the handles back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat throughout the entire set
> — Pull with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Keep your hips still as you pull
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of each rep

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back rounds as the weight gets heavier
> — The torso rises up toward vertical during the pull
> — The handles swing instead of traveling in a straight line

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern barbell variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Chest-Supported Row
**Alternatives:** Barbell Row, Cable Row Machine

## Section 3 — Inverted Row

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Inverted Row |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | PULL_UP_BAR |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Inverted Row is a horizontal pulling exercise performed hanging beneath a bar, pulling the chest up toward it while the body stays in a straight line. It trains the back, biceps, and rear shoulders using body weight. It is one of the more demanding bodyweight rowing exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Inverted Row builds back and pulling strength relative to body weight, training the same horizontal pulling pattern as a Barbell Row or Dumbbell Row without external load. It is well suited for athletes building toward pull-ups or wanting a no-equipment-beyond-a-bar pulling option. It also allows the difficulty to be adjusted by changing body angle relative to the bar. It is a staple bodyweight back exercise in calisthenics-based training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Set a bar at a height that allows you to hang with your body at an angle.
2. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width and hang with arms extended.
3. Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
4. Brace your core and pull your chest up toward the bar.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull.
6. Lower yourself back down under control to full arm extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your body in a straight line throughout the pull
> — Pull with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of each rep
> — Adjust your foot position to change the difficulty as needed

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips sag, breaking the straight body line
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of the bar
> — The body swings to build momentum instead of pulling with control

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Resistance Band Row, Seated Cable Row

## Section 4 — Single-Arm Dumbbell Row

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Single-Arm Dumbbell Row |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Single-Arm Dumbbell Row is a horizontal pulling exercise performed bracing one hand on a bench while pulling a dumbbell with the other. It trains the back, biceps, and rear shoulders on one side at a time. The supported stance allows a heavier load than a standing two-arm row.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Single-Arm Dumbbell Row builds back thickness and pulling strength on each side independently, exposing and correcting imbalances a two-arm row can hide. It allows a heavier relative load since the bracing arm and knee provide support. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the standard Dumbbell Row and want a more demanding unilateral challenge. It also reinforces core control, since the torso must resist rotation during the pull.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Place one hand and one knee on a bench for support.
2. Hold a dumbbell in your free hand, arm hanging fully extended.
3. Brace your core and keep your back flat.
4. Pull the dumbbell up toward your lower ribs.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blade back at the top of the pull.
6. Lower the dumbbell back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat and roughly parallel to the floor
> — Pull with your elbow rather than your hand
> — Keep your hips and torso still as you pull
> — Squeeze your shoulder blade at the top of each rep

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso twists to help lift the dumbbell
> — The lower back rounds as the dumbbell gets heavier
> — The elbow drifts away from the body during the pull

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Dumbbell Row
**Alternatives:** Chest-Supported Row, Seated Cable Row

| Equipment Tag(s) | RESISTANCE_BAND |

| Secondary Muscle(s) | BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Band Pull Apart is a horizontal pulling exercise performed by holding a resistance band with both hands and pulling it apart at chest height. It trains the rear shoulders and upper back. It is one of the most basic pulling exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Band Pull Apart builds rear-shoulder and upper-back strength using the lightest equipment of any pulling exercise in the library. It is well suited as a warm-up activation drill before pressing or pulling training. It also helps balance the shoulders against heavy pressing volume, since the rear shoulders are easy to neglect. It requires only a light resistance band.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Hold a resistance band with both hands at chest height, arms extended.
2. Brace your core and keep your chest tall.
3. Pull the band apart by moving your hands out to the sides.
4. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull.
5. Return your hands back together under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Pull with your shoulder blades rather than just your arms
> — Keep your arms at chest height throughout the movement
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the end of each rep
> — Control the return rather than letting the band snap your hands together

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The arms drop below chest height during the pull
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears
> — The movement turns into a yanking motion instead of a controlled pull

**Progressions:** Resistance Band Row

## Section 8 — Rear Delt Fly

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Rear Delt Fly is an isolation exercise performed bent forward at the hips, raising a dumbbell in each hand out to the sides. It trains the rear shoulders specifically. It is performed with a slight, fixed bend in the elbows throughout.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Rear Delt Fly builds rear-shoulder size and strength in a focused, single-joint movement that rowing exercises train less directly. It is well suited as an accessory exercise to balance heavy pressing volume in a program. It also supports shoulder health, since underdeveloped rear shoulders are commonly linked to rounded-shoulder posture. It is a common accessory exercise in shoulder-focused hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand and hinge forward at your hips, holding a dumbbell in each hand.
2. Let both dumbbells hang directly below your shoulders.
3. Keep a slight, fixed bend in your elbows.
4. Raise both dumbbells out to the sides until level with your torso.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of the raise.
6. Lower the dumbbells back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Lead with your elbows rather than your hands
> — Keep your back flat throughout the hinge
> — Keep the bend in your elbows fixed throughout the movement
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top of each rep

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso rises up toward vertical to help lift the dumbbells
> — The lower back rounds during the hinge position
> — The dumbbells swing using momentum instead of a controlled raise

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Face Pull, Band Pull Apart

## Section 9 — Face Pull

| Field | Value |

| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_HORIZONTAL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Face Pull is a horizontal pulling exercise performed on a cable machine, pulling a rope attachment toward the face while flaring the elbows out wide. It trains the rear shoulders and upper back. It is performed standing, facing the cable machine.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Face Pull builds rear-shoulder and upper-back strength while also reinforcing healthy shoulder rotation, since the wide elbow position encourages external rotation at the top of the pull. It is well suited as an accessory exercise to balance heavy pressing volume in a program. It also supports shoulder health for athletes who do significant overhead or pressing work. It is one of the most commonly prescribed shoulder-health accessory exercises.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach a rope to a high cable pulley and grip one end in each hand.
2. Step back to create tension and stagger your stance.
3. Brace your core and pull the rope toward your face.
4. Flare your elbows out wide as you pull.
5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull.
6. Return the rope forward under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Pull toward your face rather than your chest
> — Flare your elbows out wide as the rope comes in
> — Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the end of each rep
> — Control the return rather than letting the cable pull your arms forward

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The elbows stay low instead of flaring out wide
> — The torso leans back to help pull the rope in
> — The shoulders shrug up toward the ears during the pull

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Band Pull Apart
**Alternatives:** Rear Delt Fly

## Section 10 — Barbell Curl

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Barbell Curl |
| Category | PULL |
| Movement Pattern | PULL_CURL |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BARBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BICEPS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | FOREARMS |

**ABOUT**
> The Barbell Curl is an isolation exercise performed by curling a barbell from a hanging position up toward the shoulders. It trains the biceps using both arms together on a single bar. It is typically performed standing.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Barbell Curl builds biceps size and strength while allowing a heavier total load than a dumbbell curl, since both arms work together on a single bar. It is well suited for lifters looking to add heavy arm-specific volume to an upper-body program. It is one of the most widely used barbell accessory exercises for arm development. It pairs well with dumbbell curl variations to train the biceps from multiple angles.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding a barbell with an underhand grip at shoulder width.
2. Brace your core and keep your elbows close to your torso.
3. Curl the bar up toward your shoulders.
4. Squeeze your biceps at the top of the movement.
5. Lower the bar back down under control to full extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your elbows pinned at your sides throughout
> — Curl with your forearms only, not your shoulders
> — Squeeze at the top of each rep before lowering
> — Lower the bar with control rather than dropping it

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The elbows drift forward away from the torso during the curl
> — The torso swings backward to help lift the bar
> — The bar drops quickly on the way down instead of lowering under control

**Progressions:** Preacher Curl
**Regressions:** Resistance Band Curl
**Alternatives:** Dumbbell Curl, Cable Curl

**ABOUT**

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Preacher Curl builds biceps size and strength in the strictest possible form, since the angled pad eliminates body english entirely. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the standard Dumbbell Curl and want a more isolated challenge. It also emphasizes the stretched position at the bottom of the curl more than a standing variation. It is a common finishing exercise in arm-focused hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit at the preacher bench with the back of your arm braced against the pad.
2. Hold a dumbbell with your arm extended.
3. Brace your core and keep your arm pressed into the pad.
4. Curl the dumbbell up toward your shoulder.
5. Squeeze your bicep at the top of the movement.
6. Lower the dumbbell back down under control to full extension.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your arm pressed firmly against the pad throughout
> — Curl with your forearm only, not your shoulder
> — Squeeze at the top of each rep before lowering
> — Lower the dumbbell with control rather than dropping it

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The upper arm lifts off the pad to help lift the weight
> — The shoulder rises to assist the curl
> — The dumbbell drops quickly on the way down instead of lowering under control
