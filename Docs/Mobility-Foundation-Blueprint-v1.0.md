## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Mobility Foundation is the catalog's **entry point into structured mobility practice** — the BEGINNER rung of the one family that serves *all* athlete types. Its goal is `IMPROVE_MOBILITY` ("flexibility, joint health, movement quality" — `Program-Catalog-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.4). The intent is to build a **foundational mobility base**: teach the athlete the practice itself — foundational ranges of motion, basic static holds, and basic dynamic drills — across near-daily, low-intensity sessions, so they can later progress to deeper ranges and more challenging variations in Mobility Intermediate. It is the catalog's **supportive / prehab / recovery practice**, designed to run *alongside* an athlete's primary training (strength, hypertrophy, running, hybrid, or general), not to deliver a strength, size, or conditioning stimulus of its own.

### Target Athlete

- **All athlete types** (PEA §5.4) — the family's audience is universal, segmented only by experience.
- The athlete **new to structured mobility work** (PAS §3.2 BEGINNER — no assumed technique): learning to hold positions, breathe through them, and move through foundational ranges.
- Wants a low-barrier, train-anywhere joint-health and movement-quality practice to support their primary program — not a standalone performance ladder.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Already familiar with mobility patterns, wants deeper ranges / harder poses | This program teaches foundational ranges and basic holds; the more-challenging-variation lever is deliberately withheld here (§4) | **Mobility Intermediate** (Sort 24) |
| Wants a strength, hypertrophy, or conditioning training stimulus | Mobility is supportive/prehab practice, not a primary training stimulus | The relevant primary-goal family (Strength / Muscle Building / Conditioning) — run *alongside* this |
| Wants a maximal/advanced mobility performance ceiling | Mobility is a continuous supportive practice; the family is complete-by-design at INTERMEDIATE (no Mobility Advanced exists or is roadmapped) | — (no such rung; not a journey gap — PEA §7.1) |

### Expected Outcomes

Over the 4-week block: an established mobility-practice habit, competent execution of foundational static holds and basic dynamic drills, tolerance for sustained holds (the durations having lengthened week to week), and readiness for the deeper ranges and more challenging variations of Mobility Intermediate.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Every session is **MAIN-only** — no WARM_UP, no COOL_DOWN (PAS-D9); the family's unique structural signature.
- Static holds are prescribed in `durationSeconds`; dynamic drills in sets × reps — never `sets` for a static hold (PAS §11.8).
- Progression is visible (Time-Based / Model 5): **hold durations lengthen** from earlier to later weeks — not added load, not harder variations (those are Intermediate's lever).
- Sessions stay within 10–30 minutes (§10.2) and 5–10 holds (§10.1); the deepest/longest holds are never front-loaded into Week 1.

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 4 weeks; **no mandatory deload** (PAS-D7, under 7 weeks; a low-intensity mobility practice carries no fatigue a deload would discharge).
- **Frequency:** 5 sessions/week — near-daily practice, reflecting *consistency*, not training stress.
- **Weekly structure:** 5 short mobility sessions per week, each MAIN-only, rotating focus across the body's major regions so no single area is worked every day to the exclusion of others.
- **Periodization summary:** a single short beginner block on **Time-Based Progression (Model 5)** — the progressed variable is **hold duration** (seconds), lengthening across the 4 weeks. No load, no volume-by-sets periodization (mobility volume is measured in holds and durations, not sets or weight).

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). MOBILITY is the catalog's one **MAIN-only** family: **no WARM_UP and no COOL_DOWN** (PAS-D9). Each session is a single MAIN section organized per PAS §11.8:

1. **Open with global mobility or breathwork** — gentle whole-body movement or controlled breathing to begin.
2. **Progress to joint-specific work** — dynamic mobility drills (sets × reps) for the session's focus regions.
3. **End with deep, long-hold static stretches** — sustained holds (`durationSeconds`) once the body is prepared.

Sequencing rules (PAS §11.8): **static stretching never precedes dynamic work**; the deepest/longest holds are **never front-loaded into Week 1** — range and hold-duration build across the block. MAIN sits within 5–10 holds (PAS-D11 / §10.1); sessions stay ≤30 minutes (§10.2), the shortest in the catalog.

Beginner emphasis: learn the position and the breathing first; foundational ranges before depth.

---

## Section 4 — Progression Philosophy

**Time-Based Progression (Model 5; PAS §7.1–7.2):** the progressed variable is **hold duration in seconds**, lengthening from earlier to later weeks within the program. PAS §11.8 makes the level distinction explicit and two-dimensional: (a) **longer holds** across weeks — used here — and (b) **more challenging variations in intermediate programs** — deliberately **withheld** at this level.

At Mobility Foundation, progression is therefore **duration-only on a foundational variation set**: the athlete holds the same basic, learnable positions for progressively longer as the weeks advance. The **harder-variation lever (deeper positions, more demanding poses, more complex flows) is reserved for Mobility Intermediate** — this is the single most important content boundary in the family, because it is the lever the entire Foundation ↔ Intermediate distinction turns on (§9). No RPE at BEGINNER (PAS-D3); no load (this family has none).

---

## Section 5 — Modality / Movement-Selection Philosophy

Selection follows PAS §11.8: a toolkit of **static holds** (`durationSeconds`) and **dynamic mobility drills** (sets × reps), plus breathwork. At BEGINNER, selection favors **foundational, learnable positions and basic ranges** — accessible holds and drills the new-to-mobility athlete can perform with control, across the major regions, with adequate breathing. Avoid deep/demanding positions and complex flows that assume mobility competence the beginner does not yet have — those belong to Mobility Intermediate. The selection is **practice-establishing**: the goal is a competent, repeatable foundational mobility practice, not maximal range.

---

## Section 6 — Recovery Role

Recovery is not a *periodization* concern in this family — **the family itself is the recovery / prehab practice.** Mobility Foundation is low-intensity, near-daily work; it carries **no deload** (PAS-D7), because it accumulates none of the fatigue a deload discharges. Its role across the whole catalog is to *be* the joint-health and movement-quality support that athletes in every other family layer onto their primary training. The 5-day cadence is practice consistency, not training stress.

---

## Section 7 — Predecessor / Successor Logic

- **Predecessor — none.** Mobility Foundation is the family's BEGINNER entry — the on-ramp to structured mobility practice.
- **Successor — Mobility Intermediate (Sort 24).** Hands forward an established mobility-practice habit, tolerance for sustained holds, and readiness for deeper ranges and more challenging variations. Mobility Intermediate is **terminal complete-by-design** — there is no Mobility Advanced in the catalog or on the §7.1 roadmap, and none is warranted: mobility is a continuous supportive practice, and an INTERMEDIATE rung is a complete, sensible endpoint (no journey gap — distinct from the terminal-by-unbuilt-successor cases elsewhere).

## Section 8 — Baseline Statement (for the Mobility Intermediate test)

Mobility Foundation is **foundational-range, basic-hold, basic-variation, duration-progressed practice**: the new-to-mobility athlete learns accessible positions across the major regions and holds them for progressively longer across 4 weeks, with the more-challenging-variation lever deliberately withheld. This is the fixed reference Mobility Intermediate must prove it diverges from — not by running longer, but by deeper content.

## Section 9 — Three-Part Falsifiable Test for Mobility Intermediate

**The question:** *Can Mobility Intermediate be meaningfully different from Mobility Foundation, given the two are the leanest-differentiated pair in the catalog — identical on goal (IMPROVE_MOBILITY), environment (HOME), frequency (5/wk), athlete type (All), progression model (Time-Based), and deload (none), differing only on level + duration (4 vs 6 wk) + total (20 vs 30)?*

Because no goal, environment, frequency, or model axis separates the pair, **the entire distinction rests on authored content depth.** Mobility Intermediate's Blueprint must demonstrate **all three** (evaluated with level/duration metadata hidden):

| # | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **More challenging variations** | Built around deeper positions, harder poses, and/or more complex flows than Mobility Foundation's foundational, basic-hold set — the explicit §11.8 "introduce more challenging variations in intermediate programs" differentiator, shown in authored detail. This is the lever Foundation deliberately withholds (§4). |
| 2 | **Meaningfully longer / deeper holds** | Greater range-of-motion demand and longer hold durations than Foundation — **not the same foundational holds prescribed for two more weeks.** |
| 3 | **Non-derivability** | A reviewer can tell Mobility Intermediate is *not* Mobility Foundation run two weeks longer. **If Mobility Intermediate is merely "Mobility Foundation extended" — same variations, same depth, more weeks — it FAILS** → re-examine the pair under the family's governing principle (Family-Research Deliverable 7 / PEA §2.2 remediation). |

**The subtlety to flag for Mobility Intermediate's authoring:** because the metadata distinction is the leanest in the catalog (level + duration only), the burden of differentiation falls **almost entirely on authored content**. A longer block alone does not pass — the variations themselves must be demonstrably harder and the holds demonstrably deeper. Note also that retirement is **not** an available remedy here: the family sits at the 2-program minimum (PEA §2.1), so a failing test points to re-differentiation (deeper Intermediate content), not removal.

## Section 10 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE Mobility Foundation (baseline established).**

**Why it independently justifies its place in the catalog:** Mobility Foundation is the catalog's **entry point into structured mobility practice** — the BEGINNER rung of the one family serving *all* athlete types, the supportive/prehab/recovery practice every other family's athlete can layer onto their primary training. It stands on its athlete (the new-to-mobility practitioner), its role (the universal mobility on-ramp), and its training content (foundational-range, basic-hold, duration-progressed practice). Its existence does not depend on Mobility Intermediate's absence — it is the family's beginner baseline, distinct from its successor by level and authored content depth.

**The pair is NOT resolved.** This pass establishes the baseline and the three-part falsifiable test; closing the catalog's leanest-pair convergence question requires Mobility Intermediate's own Blueprint to pass that test (§9). **Mobility Intermediate is not authored in this pass.**

**Confidence:** High for Mobility Foundation's own justification; the residual uncertainty is entirely on the Mobility Intermediate side and is handed forward as a test — fittingly, the catalog's leanest pair is the final family's Stage 1 test.

**Scope guard:** Architecture locked/unchanged (PEA v1.4, PAS v1.3, PCA v1.4). Mobility Stage 1 progress: **1 of 2 blueprinted** (this). Outstanding: **Mobility Intermediate** (must pass §9's three-part test), then a Mobility Stage 1 Review. No Stage 2 work has begun.

**Recommended next action:** author the **Mobility Intermediate Blueprint** against §9's three-part test (resolving the family's single convergence pair), then a Mobility Stage 1 Review — after which, per the Governance Review, the catalog naming/positioning reconciliation (Production Standard §1) precedes large-scale Stage 2.
