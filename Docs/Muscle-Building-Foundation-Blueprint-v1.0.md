## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity

Muscle Building Foundation is the **whole-physique entry point** to the Muscle Building family. It introduces a beginner to structured hypertrophy training on an Upper/Lower split, building balanced muscle across the entire body while teaching the two skills the rest of the family depends on: executing a prescribed rep range with good technique, and tolerating training volume that accumulates across a block. Its commitment is balance at a beginner-appropriate dose — every major muscle group trained, none specialized, volume kept at the low end of the envelope so a novice adapts and recovers.

### Target Athlete

- New to structured resistance training in this style, per `Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §3.2's BEGINNER definition (little assumed technique; not yet experienced self-managing rep ranges).
- Goal is whole-body muscle development — a balanced physique.
- Can train 4 days/week.
- Wants a guided introduction to hypertrophy training, not a specialization.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Wants lower-body / glute specialization from the start, and/or can train only 3 days/week | This program is a balanced 4-day split; it neither concentrates lower-body volume nor offers a 3-day option | **Lower Body Foundation** (the family's other BEGINNER door) |
| Already self-manages rep ranges and tolerates mid-range volume | Starts at the low end of the volume envelope; technique-introduction emphasis | Muscle Building Intermediate (Sort 6) |
| Primary goal is maximal strength | Volume-driven, no 1RM-style primary stimulus (Family Research Deliverable 1; PAS §11.2) | Strength family |
| Primary goal is fat loss / conditioning | Not built around a metabolic stimulus | Conditioning family (incl. Body Recomposition) |

### Expected Outcomes

Balanced whole-body hypertrophy stimulus across an 8-week block, technical competence on the core Upper/Lower movement pool, the ability to self-manage a rep range under double progression, and one completed structural deload — the readiness package Muscle Building Intermediate assumes.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Every major muscle group lands inside the lower portion of the 10–20 sets/week band (PAS §11.2) — a beginner dose, not a peak.
- Upper and lower volume are roughly co-equal (balanced split).
- Week-over-week progression is visible (QC-2) via rising set counts and/or advancing rep-range targets.

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 8 weeks (PAS-D7 → exactly one deload).
- **Frequency:** 4 sessions/week.
- **Split:** **Upper/Lower**, run twice (Upper A · Lower A · Upper B · Lower B) — the correct fit for a 4-day frequency (PAS §11.2), giving each muscle group two weekly exposures, the distribution that keeps every group inside the band.
- **Deload:** one, Week 7 (slots 25–28), per PAS §14; peak Week 8 (slots 29–32). Deload maintains all four sessions, cuts primary set volume 40–50% (PAS-D8). For most athletes this is their first coached deload.
- **Periodization summary:** a single accumulation mesocycle (Volume Accumulation, Model 4) raising working sets across Weeks 1–6, deload Week 7, peak Week 8; Double Progression (Model 2) governs load within each exercise. No Block Periodization (that arrives only at Muscle Building Advanced).

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9; WS-A5). WARM_UP required for HYPERTROPHY (PAS-D9); COOL_DOWN optional. Exercise order per PAS §11.2: compounds first, isolation last; cables/machines appropriate; no core-compound minimum.

**Upper session:** WARM_UP → MAIN (press compound first; row/pull compound second — push/pull balance enforced within the session; then shoulder, biceps, triceps isolation) → (COOL_DOWN optional).

**Lower session:** WARM_UP → MAIN (squat-pattern compound first; hinge-pattern compound second — quad/posterior-chain balance within the session; then quad, hamstring, glute, calf isolation) → (COOL_DOWN optional).

Beginner emphasis: movement quality gates volume advancement (Family Research Deliverable 3); exercise selection favors stable, learnable patterns over maximal-loading variants.

---

## Section 4 — Weekly Muscle-Group Coverage

Two Upper + two Lower sessions give each major group two exposures; volume sits at the **low end** of each band (beginner dose):

| Muscle group | Trained on | Target weekly sets |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | Upper A + B | 8–12 |
| Back | Upper A + B | 10–14 |
| Shoulders | Upper A + B | 8–12 |
| Biceps / Triceps | Upper A + B | 6–10 each |
| Quads | Lower A + B | 10–14 |
| Hamstrings | Lower A + B | 8–12 |
| Glutes | Lower A + B | 8–12 |
| Calves | Lower A + B | 6–10 |

**Upper and lower weekly volume are roughly co-equal** — the balanced 50/50 whole-physique distribution, at a beginner dose. This balance is the baseline the Lower Body Foundation Blueprint is tested against.

## Section 5 — Progression Blueprint

- **Double Progression (Model 2):** rep ranges prescribed (compounds ~6–10, isolation ~10–15); add load when the top of the range is reached on all sets. The two-stage failure rule applies (Family Research Deliverable 3).
- **Volume Accumulation (Model 4):** working sets rise across Weeks 1–6 (e.g., 2 → 3 → 4 on primaries), reset at the Week 7 deload, peak Week 8 — the beginner expression of "accumulation, not a peak."
- **RPE:** NOT permitted at BEGINNER (PAS-D3). Effort is guided by the rep-range target and technique, not RPE notes.

Starting position: **low end** of PAS-D11 (Family Research Deliverable 4's BEGINNER curve) — a novel stimulus produces growth cheaply, and low volume protects recovery and adherence.

---

## Section 6 — Volume Blueprint

- **Per session:** 5–8 exercises, 18–30 sets (PAS-D11), opening near the lower bound (~18–20) and rising modestly toward peak.
- **Per week:** the Section 4 distribution; every group in the lower portion of its 10–20 band.
- **Session duration:** within HYPERTROPHY's 50–80 min guideline (PAS §10.2), toward the lower-middle for a beginner.
- **Guardrail check:** inside PAS-D11 and §11.2 throughout — no deviation justification required.

---

## Section 7 — Recovery Blueprint

- **Rest-day placement:** ≥1 full rest day across four sessions; Upper/Lower alternation gives built-in recovery (consecutive days never hit the same group).
- **Intra-session rest:** BEGINNER guidance (PAS §10.3) — 60–90s typical on compounds (athlete self-regulates), accessory rest omitted; reference-only (EPA1-D2).
- **Fatigue management:** structural and prescribed, not self-regulated (no RPE at BEGINNER). The Week 7 deload is coached as a concept, likely the athlete's first.
- **Deload:** Week 7 maintains frequency, cuts primary volume 40–50% (PAS-D8).

---

## Section 8 — Successor / Predecessor Logic

- **Predecessor — none.** This is a BEGINNER entry point; an athlete arrives here with no prior Forge program required.
- **Successor — Muscle Building Intermediate (Sort 6).** Hands forward the four readiness items (Family Research Deliverable 8): movement competency across the Upper/Lower pool, volume tolerance from a completed accumulation block, one completed deload, and readiness for the Intermediate jump (higher volume + the introduction of RPE) — all within the same Double Progression + Volume Accumulation models. This forward link (into a non-terminal ladder) is part of the program's identity.

Note the two BEGINNER doors of this family — Muscle Building Foundation and Lower Body Foundation — **do not converge** (Family Research Deliverable 7). Each feeds its own ladder (this one → Muscle Building Intermediate; Lower Body Foundation → Lower Body Intermediate). This is the opposite of the Strength family, whose two BEGINNER doors converged into one ladder.
