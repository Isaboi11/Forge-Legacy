## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Bodyweight Performance is the **advanced calisthenics capstone** of the Bodyweight ladder: it takes the bodyweight-strength base (from Bodyweight Strength) and develops **advanced skill and power** — explosive movement and high-level static-strength skills. Its goals are `BUILD_STRENGTH` and `GENERAL_FITNESS`, but at this rung **strength is the base, not the target** — the target is *expressing* that strength as advanced calisthenics performance. This is the qualitative shift the name "Performance" implies, and the distinct outcome the ladder culminates in.

### Target Athlete

- **General athlete** (PEA §5.4), **advanced** (PAS §3.2: "3+ years, understands periodization, can self-regulate intensity, advanced technique assumed"), who has graduated **Bodyweight Strength** or has equivalent advanced bodyweight strength.
- Trains bodyweight-only and pursues advanced calisthenics skill/power feats.
- Can train 4 days/week and self-regulate high-skill, high-CNS work.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Lacks an advanced bodyweight-strength base | This program assumes the strength to attempt advanced skills (levers, planche, handstand work) | **Bodyweight Strength** (Sort 19) |
| Wants strength, not skill/power | The target here is skill+power expression, not maximal strength accrual | Bodyweight Strength |
| New/intermediate to calisthenics | ADVANCED content beyond intermediate reach | Bodyweight Foundation / Bodyweight Strength |
| Wants equipment-based or gym training | Bodyweight-only | Home Strength Foundation / Strength family |

### Expected Outcomes

Advanced calisthenics **skill and power** feats over the 10-week block — progress on explosive movements and high-level static skills (the specific milestones set in authoring) — the distinct capstone outcome of the Bodyweight ladder. No further in-family rung (terminal).

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Content is genuinely ADVANCED: skill/power movements (explosive + static-strength skills) that require the Bodyweight Strength base and are beyond intermediate reach.
- A **performance dimension beyond strength** is present (power and/or skill/balance), not merely harder strength reps.
- Progression is visible (QC-2) as **skill-milestone advancement** (and supporting strength/power gains), not just added weeks/reps.
- Bodyweight-only (verifiable against §11.7's "not available" list); WARM_UP each session (PAS-D9).

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 10 weeks (PAS-D7 → one deload).
- **Frequency:** 4 sessions/week.
- **Weekly structure:** 4 skill/power-forward sessions — each pairing an explosive/power focus and/or an advanced static-skill focus with supporting strength work, across push/pull/lower-body/skill domains. WARM_UP required (thorough, given the skill/power demand); COOL_DOWN optional (PAS-D9).
- **Deload:** one, Week 9 (slots 33–36), peak Week 10 (slots 37–40), per PAS §14 — reduces skill/power volume and CNS load, maintains four sessions (PAS-D8).
- **Periodization summary:** a single block on **skill-milestone progression** (Section 6), with supporting strength/power work — the ADVANCED application of the FULL_BODY linear-progression rule.

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). FULL_BODY requires **WARM_UP** (emphasized here for skill/power readiness); COOL_DOWN optional (PAS-D9). Equipment ceiling per §11.7 (bodyweight, pull-up bar, bands; no barbell/rack/cables/machines). Each session: WARM_UP (thorough dynamic + skill-specific prep) → MAIN: a power/explosive element (plyometric push/pull/jump) and/or an advanced static-skill element (lever/planche/handstand progression), plus supporting strength and accessory work → (COOL_DOWN optional). MAIN sits within PAS-D11 (4–7 exercises / 12–20 sets), 40–70 min (§10.2); skill/power work uses low-rep, high-quality efforts with adequate rest (skill and power degrade under fatigue).

---

## Section 4 — Advanced Calisthenics Content (test part 1)

The program's content is genuinely ADVANCED and requires the Bodyweight Strength base:
- **Explosive / plyometric calisthenics:** clapping/explosive push-ups, plyometric pull-ups, jump and depth variations.
- **Advanced static-strength skills:** front/back lever progressions, planche progressions, handstand and handstand push-up work.
- **Muscle-ups** and advanced unilateral progressions (toward one-arm push-up / one-arm pull-up).

None of these are achievable by an intermediate without the strength base Bodyweight Strength builds — which is precisely why this rung sits above it.

---

## Section 5 — Performance Dimension Beyond Strength (test part 2)

Bodyweight Performance introduces training **qualities that Bodyweight Strength does not target**:
- **Power / explosiveness** — plyometric work develops rate of force production, a distinct quality from the grinding maximal-strength effort of Bodyweight Strength.
- **Skill / balance / static control** — handstands and lever/planche holds develop coordination and isometric control, qualities absent from a pure strength-variation program.

So "Performance" is not "more strength" — it is strength **expressed** as power and skill. This is the qualitative shift that distinguishes the ADVANCED rung from the INTERMEDIATE strength rung.

---

## Section 6 — ADVANCED Progression Model (test part 3 / req)

Bodyweight Performance progresses by **skill-milestone progression** — advancing through defined skill levels rather than (only) adding reps. This is the **Linear Progression model (PAS §7.2 — FULL_BODY) applied to skill acquisition**: each skill has a regression-to-progression ladder (e.g., tuck → advanced-tuck → straddle → full front lever / planche; band-assisted → strict → explosive rep quality), and the athlete advances milestone by milestone, with supporting strength/power volume progressed beneath it.

**Progression-model note (honest, not a blocker):** PAS §7.2 assigns FULL_BODY → Linear Progression at all levels; PAS §7.1's Model 1 program list names only Bodyweight Foundation and Bodyweight Strength as rep-based variants, and does **not** separately list Bodyweight Performance — an omission, not an exclusion. Block Periodization (§7.1 Model 3) is explicitly scoped to STRENGTH/HYPERTROPHY ADVANCED, RUNNING, CYCLING — **not FULL_BODY** — so it is correctly **not** invoked here. This Blueprint therefore defines Bodyweight Performance's progression as Linear Progression *applied as skill-milestone advancement* — fully within the locked rule, **no new model and no architecture change proposed.** (A future review could choose to name this variant in §7.1 alongside the rep-based variant; that is a cosmetic catalog-doc note, not a blocker, and is not actioned here.)

## Section 7 — Recovery Strategy

- **10 weeks / 4 days with a Week 9 deload.** Skill and power work (plyometrics, max-effort static holds) is high-CNS; sessions use low-rep, high-quality efforts with adequate rest, and the deload discharges accumulated neural/skill fatigue.
- ADVANCED self-regulation (PAS §3.2): the advanced athlete manages skill-practice fatigue and avoids grinding failed skill reps.

---

## Section 8 — Expected Outcomes

By the end of the 10-week block: demonstrable advanced calisthenics skill and power feats (the milestones set in authoring — e.g., progress on levers, planche, handstand work, explosive movements) — the distinct, performance-level capstone of the Bodyweight ladder.

## Section 10 — Distinction from Bodyweight Strength & Four-Part Test Evaluation

### Bodyweight Strength → Bodyweight Performance distinction

| | Bodyweight Strength (INT) | Bodyweight Performance (ADV) |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Maximal bodyweight **strength** via leverage-variation ladders | Advanced **skill + power** (strength is the base, not the target) |
| Content | Harder strength variations (one-arm progressions, etc.) | Explosive/plyometric + static-strength skills (levers, planche, handstand) + muscle-ups |
| Qualities trained | Strength | Strength + **power** + **skill/balance** |
| Progression | Rep-based linear advancing strength variations | **Skill-milestone** progression |
| Level | INTERMEDIATE | ADVANCED |

### Four-part test (handed forward by the Bodyweight Strength Blueprint)

| # | Test | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Advanced calisthenics content** | **PASS** | §4: explosive/plyometric + static-strength skills (levers/planche/handstand) + muscle-ups + advanced unilateral — require the strength base, beyond intermediate reach. |
| 2 | **Performance dimension beyond strength** | **PASS** | §5: adds power (plyometrics) and skill/balance (static holds, handstands) — qualities Bodyweight Strength does not target. |
| 3 | **Genuine INT → ADV jump** | **PASS** | ADVANCED content assuming the Strength base; a defined ADVANCED progression model (§6, skill-milestone); content an intermediate cannot perform. |
| 4 | **Non-derivability** | **PASS** | Not "Bodyweight Strength with more weeks / harder variations" — a categorically different skill+power target a reviewer can distinguish. |

**Does the ladder culminate in a genuinely different outcome? YES.** The three rungs produce three distinct outcomes: **movement competency** (Foundation) → **maximal bodyweight strength** (Strength) → **advanced skill + power performance** (Performance). The ladder escalates coherently and ends in a distinct capstone, not a stretched repetition.

**Honest line (the continuum acknowledged):** strength and skill/power lie on a continuum — a one-arm push-up is both a strength feat and a skill. The genuine line: Bodyweight Strength targets *maximal strength via leverage* (grinding, max-effort); Bodyweight Performance adds *power* (explosive — a different quality) and *skill/balance* (static control — a coordination quality). Strength, power, and skill are recognized as distinct trainable qualities, so the rung is genuinely different, not merely "harder strength" — stated plainly rather than overclaimed.
