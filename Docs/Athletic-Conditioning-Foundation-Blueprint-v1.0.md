## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Athletic Conditioning Foundation is the **beginner work-capacity entry** to the Conditioning family. Its goals are `IMPROVE_CONDITIONING` ("work capacity, anaerobic threshold, general fitness") and `GENERAL_FITNESS` ("well-rounded physical capability, beginner baseline") per `Program-Catalog-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.4. The intent is to **introduce the conditioning methodology** — work-to-rest structure, circuits, combined strength + cardio — at a beginner dose, building a foundation of work capacity and well-rounded fitness that the rest of the ladder assumes. It is conditioning-forward, but deliberately sub-threshold: the goal is to build a base and teach the method, not to chase a performance ceiling.

This conditioning-forward, general-fitness, beginner-dose identity is the **Foundation baseline** the other two Conditioning Foundations are measured against (Section 10).

### Target Athlete

- **General athlete** (`Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §5.4) new to structured conditioning, per `Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §3.2's BEGINNER definition (little assumed technique).
- Wants well-rounded fitness and a work-capacity base — not fat loss as a primary aim (Body Recomposition ladder), not concurrent strength development (Hybrid ladder), not a single-modality goal.
- Can train **3 days/week** — the beginner-appropriate cadence.
- May have little or no strength-training background (informs the resistance role, Section 5).

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary goal is fat loss + muscle retention | This program is conditioning-forward and general-fitness, not resistance-forward/deficit | **Body Recomposition Foundation** (Sort 13) |
| Primary goal is concurrent strength + conditioning | No dedicated strength development; GYM not MIXED | **Hybrid Foundation** (Sort 16) |
| Already an intermediate conditioning athlete | Beginner density / 3-day; Linear or Volume Accumulation | Conditioning Intermediate (Sort 14) |
| Primary goal is maximal strength or hypertrophy | Conditioning-driven, not load-/volume-driven | Strength / Muscle Building families |

### Expected Outcomes

A measurable beginner-level improvement in work capacity and well-rounded fitness over the 8-week block, plus competence in the conditioning method (work:rest, circuits) — the readiness package Conditioning Intermediate assumes.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Conditioning (energy-system) work is the clear majority of session emphasis at a beginner dose; resistance is simple and supporting.
- Work capacity accumulates across the block (QC-2 visible as added rounds / sets / duration), not via threshold-chasing.
- Every session has WARM_UP and COOL_DOWN (PAS-D9); `restSeconds` is populated (PAS §11.3); resistance favors simple, learnable movements (PAS §11.3 complex-barbell caution).

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 8 weeks (PAS-D7 → one deload).
- **Frequency:** **3 sessions/week** — the family's only 3-day program, and a deliberate beginner-recovery choice (the analogue of Lower Body Foundation's 3-day exception in Muscle Building).
- **Weekly structure:** three combined strength + cardio sessions (PAS §11.3) at **beginner density** — longer rest, lower intensity, simpler circuits than Conditioning Intermediate. Each session has warm-up and cool-down.
- **Deload:** one, Week 7 (slots 19–21), peak Week 8 (slots 22–24), per PAS §14 — reduces work density / volume while maintaining all three sessions (PAS-D8 generalized).
- **Periodization summary:** a single beginner block in which **work capacity accumulates gradually** — Volume Accumulation (add rounds/sets/duration over the weeks) or Linear Progression, per PAS §7.2's CONDITIONING BEGINNER rule. **Not** Double Progression (that arrives at Conditioning Intermediate) — a genuine model-level distinction from the Intermediate baseline.

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). **CONDITIONING requires both WARM_UP and COOL_DOWN** (PAS-D9). Structure/rest per PAS §11.3 (combine strength + cardio; work-to-rest ratios primary; `restSeconds` always populated; circuits named in slots).

Each session: WARM_UP (dynamic raise) → MAIN (the day's beginner conditioning stimulus — a simple circuit or interval block at moderate density, with simple resistance interleaved) → COOL_DOWN. MAIN sits within PAS-D11 (4–8 exercises / 12–24 sets) and 30–60 min (§10.2).

**Beginner resistance caution (PAS §11.3):** "avoid complex barbell lifts without adequate instruction — CONDITIONING athletes may not have a strength training background." This applies in full here (true beginners), so resistance favors **machine, bodyweight, dumbbell, and simple movements**, not technical barbell work. This is a defining feature of the Foundation baseline and contrasts with how a hybrid program (whose athlete has a strength base) would treat resistance.

---

## Section 4 — Conditioning Emphasis

Conditioning is the **majority** of session emphasis — the program's spine — but at **beginner intensity and density**: introduce intervals and circuits, build aerobic and basic anaerobic capacity, and progressively raise work density over the block **without** pushing the anaerobic-threshold ceiling that defines the Intermediate program. The emphasis is base-building and method-learning, not performance maximization.

---

## Section 5 — Resistance-Training Role

Resistance is a **supporting minority** with a dual purpose: (1) it serves the conditioning (loaded circuit elements, simple compound/accessory movements at conditioning densities), and (2) it builds the **general-fitness movement competency** the GENERAL_FITNESS goal implies — teaching a beginner basic, safe resistance patterns. It is light-to-moderate, simple-movement, and sub-hypertrophic. It is **not** a strength-development block (that is the Hybrid ladder) and **not** hypertrophy/deficit work (that is the Body Recomposition ladder).

---

## Section 6 — Progression Strategy

- **Linear Progression or Volume Accumulation** (PAS §7.2, Models 1/4 — CONDITIONING BEGINNER): work capacity accumulates gradually across the 8 weeks (add rounds/sets/duration, or linearly progress simple loads), reset at the Week 7 deload.
- **No RPE** at BEGINNER (PAS-D3) — effort is guided by the prescribed work:rest structure and simple targets, not self-regulated RPE.
- The progression *headline* is **gradual work-capacity accumulation**, distinct from Conditioning Intermediate's Double-Progression-driven density compression — a clean model-level differentiator between the two rungs.

---

## Section 7 — Recovery Strategy

- **The 3-day frequency IS the primary recovery mechanism** — three sessions with full rest days between them is the deliberate beginner dose; a conditioning novice cannot recover from the 4-day cadence the other Foundations run.
- **Deload Week 7** reduces work density / volume, maintains all three sessions (PAS-D8).
- **Beginner rest periods** (PAS §10.3 — 60–90s typical, athlete self-regulates) on resistance; `restSeconds` always populated (PAS §11.3) so the work:rest structure is explicit.

---

## Section 8 — Expected Outcomes

By the end of the block: an improved beginner work-capacity base and well-rounded general fitness, plus competence in the conditioning method — leaving the athlete ready for Conditioning Intermediate's higher frequency (3→4 days), Double-Progression model, and tighter IMPROVE_CONDITIONING focus.

---

## Section 9 — Predecessor / Successor Logic

- **Predecessor — none.** This is a BEGINNER entry point into the Conditioning ladder; an athlete arrives with no prior Forge program required.
- **Successor — Conditioning Intermediate (Sort 14).** Hands forward work-capacity tolerance at a beginner dose, conditioning-method competence, one completed deload, and readiness for the model step (Linear/Volume Accumulation → Double Progression) and the frequency step (3 → 4 days). The Intermediate also narrows the goal set (drops GENERAL_FITNESS, keeps IMPROVE_CONDITIONING) — appropriate as the athlete moves from "beginner baseline" toward focused work capacity.
- **Naming asymmetry (noted, not fixed):** the ladder runs *Athletic Conditioning* Foundation → *Conditioning* Intermediate — mismatched name prefixes (flagged in `Conditioning-Family-Research-v1.0.md` as a minor clarity item, plausibly part of the open naming/positioning conflict, Production Standard §1). Not a governance blocker.

## Section 10 — Distinction Rationale, Foundation Baseline & Forward Convergence Tests

### Distinction (from its own side)

Athletic Conditioning Foundation is self-justifying as the **General-athlete BEGINNER conditioning entry**: distinct from its successor by level, frequency (3→4), goal set (it carries GENERAL_FITNESS, dropped at the Intermediate), and progression model (Linear/Volume Accumulation → Double Progression). It is the family's **only 3-day program**.

### Foundation Baseline (the reference the other two Foundations are measured against)

**Athletic Conditioning Foundation is: conditioning-forward at a beginner dose, general-fitness-oriented, 3-day, simple-movement resistance, Linear/Volume-Accumulation, sub-threshold.** This is the fixed reference for the remaining Foundation-layer convergence work.

### Forward Convergence Test #1 — Body Recomposition Foundation (Sort 13; BEG / GYM / 8wk / **4d** / 32; LOSE_FAT + BUILD_MUSCLE)

The Foundation analogue of the resolved Conditioning Intermediate ↔ Body Recomposition Intermediate pair (both GYM, BEGINNER). When authored, Body Recomposition Foundation must demonstrate the **same emphasis inversion** its Intermediate did — **resistance-forward + fat-loss/deficit-oriented**, vs this program's conditioning-forward/general-fitness baseline.
- **Head start the Intermediate pair lacked:** the two Foundations already differ on **frequency (4 vs 3)** and **total (32 vs 24)**, so there is more metadata separation here than at the Intermediate level.
- **But the test still applies:** do not let the frequency/total gap substitute for proving distinct authored content. Body Recomposition Foundation must still show resistance-forward emphasis and a fat-loss intent that a reviewer can distinguish from this conditioning-forward baseline.

### Forward Convergence Test #2 — Hybrid Foundation (Sort 16; BEG / **MIXED** / 8wk / **4d** / 32; BUILD_STRENGTH + IMPROVE_CONDITIONING)

Hybrid Foundation **shares IMPROVE_CONDITIONING** with this program but differs on goal (adds BUILD_STRENGTH), environment (MIXED vs GYM), and frequency (4 vs 3). Lower convergence risk, but when authored it must demonstrate a **genuine concurrent-strength introduction + MIXED-environment structure** — not "Athletic Conditioning Foundation + some lifting."
- **Open design question to resolve at that Blueprint:** how a **BEGINNER** hybrid program introduces strength while honoring PAS §11.3's complex-barbell caution. The Hybrid *Intermediate* athlete had a strength base that made heavy compounds appropriate; the Hybrid *Foundation* athlete may not. Hybrid Foundation must show how it builds strength foundations for a beginner without the technical-barbell content the caution warns against — a real Stage-1 design tension specific to that program.

## Section 11 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE Athletic Conditioning Foundation (Foundation baseline established).**

It is a clear, self-standing beginner conditioning program — the General-athlete entry to the Conditioning ladder, distinct from its successor on level, frequency, goal set, and progression model, and the family's only 3-day program. Its architecture (3-day beginner conditioning), progression (Linear/Volume Accumulation), resistance role (simple-movement, conditioning-and-general-fitness-serving), and recovery (3-day cadence + Wk7 deload) all sit inside the locked CONDITIONING/BEGINNER standards (PAS-D7/D8/D9/D11, §7.2, §10.2, §11.3).

**The Foundation layer is NOT resolved.** This pass establishes the baseline and hands forward the two convergence tests (Section 10); closing the Foundation layer requires Body Recomposition Foundation and Hybrid Foundation to be authored against those tests. **Neither is authored in this pass.**

**Scope guard — the Conditioning family's Stage 1 sequence is NOT complete.** Four of six programs are now blueprinted (all three Intermediates + this Foundation). **Outstanding Stage 1 Blueprints: Body Recomposition Foundation (13) and Hybrid Foundation (16)**, followed by a Conditioning Family Stage 1 Review. Architecture is locked; Content Production is in progress; no Stage 2 work has begun.

**Confidence:** High for Athletic Conditioning Foundation's own justification; the Foundation-layer convergence questions are handed forward explicitly as tests rather than left implicit.

**Recommended next action:** author **Body Recomposition Foundation** against Forward Convergence Test #1 (the emphasis-inversion test, with the frequency/total head-start noted), then **Hybrid Foundation** against Forward Convergence Test #2 (concurrent-strength + the beginner-barbell design question), then run a Conditioning Family Stage 1 Review.
