## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity

Muscle Building Advanced is the only program in the family built around **periodized block training** rather than a single accumulation arc. Muscle Building Foundation and Intermediate both run one accumulation mesocycle (Volume Accumulation layered on Double Progression). Muscle Building Advanced replaces that with a 12-week, multi-block structure — distinct accumulation, intensification, and peak phases with shifting rep schemes and volume targets per block (PAS §7.1 Block Periodization) — trained at 5 days/week, the family's highest frequency. It exists for the athlete who has exhausted what a single-arc model delivers and is ready to train inside a structure that plans the variation for them.

### Target Athlete

- 3+ years of training experience with this style, per `Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §3.2's ADVANCED definition ("understands periodization; assumes baseline technique; can manage complex programming").
- Has graduated Muscle Building Intermediate, or has equivalent experience with double-progression hypertrophy training and a completed accumulation block.
- Pursuing whole-body muscle development at the highest training commitment the family offers.
- Comfortable using RPE as an intensity/fatigue tool (PAS-D3 — ADVANCED) and managing a 5-day schedule.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Cannot commit to 5 days/week | This program's volume and frequency assume 5 sessions | Muscle Building Intermediate (4 days) |
| New to periodized training / has not run an accumulation block | Block Periodization assumes the athlete can manage phase-based variation | Muscle Building Intermediate (Sort 6) |
| Wants lower-body specialization | Whole-physique program | Lower Body ladder |
| Primary goal is maximal strength | Volume-driven; no 1RM-style primary stimulus | Strength family |

### Expected Outcomes

Whole-body hypertrophy across a periodized 12-week block, with phase-appropriate variation (higher-volume accumulation, higher-load intensification, a peak expression), demonstrable progression within each block, and the highest sustained training volume in the family — managed by the block structure rather than by raw set escalation.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Phase boundaries are legible in slot names (e.g., "Week 1 — Push: Accumulation"), per PAS §7.1's Block Periodization encoding.
- Every major muscle group sits inside 10–20 sets/week (PAS §11.2); the upper end of PAS-D11 is permissible at ADVANCED (PAS §10.2) and expected in accumulation phases.
- Each block shows a distinct rep/volume character; progression is visible within and across blocks (QC-2).

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 12 weeks (PAS-D7 → two deloads).
- **Frequency:** 5 sessions/week — the family's highest.
- **Split:** **Push / Pull / Legs + Upper / Lower** (a 5-day hybrid). This is the natural 5-day fit under PAS §11.2 (Push/Pull/Legs *or* Upper/Lower): it gives most muscle groups roughly twice-weekly frequency while keeping per-session volume inside PAS-D11. (The exact slot pattern is fixed at Stage 2; the Blueprint commits only to a 5-day, twice-most-groups structure.)
- **Deloads:** two — Week 4 (slots 16–20, concluding the opening block) and Week 10 (slots 46–50), per PAS §14. Peak training Weeks 11–12 (slots 51–60). Each deload maintains frequency and cuts primary set volume 40–50% (PAS-D8).
- **Periodization summary:** three blocks across 12 weeks —
  - **Block 1 — Accumulation (Wks 1–3, deload Wk 4):** highest volume, moderate loads, upper-end PAS-D11.
  - **Block 2 — Intensification (Wks 5–9, deload Wk 10):** volume trimmed, loads and rep-range targets advanced via Double Progression.
  - **Block 3 — Peak (Wks 11–12):** the realized expression of the block's progress.

  Double Progression (Model 2) governs load *within* each block; Block Periodization (Model 3) governs the *variation across* blocks. This layering (one model for within-phase load, one for phase structure) is exactly the pattern PAS §7.2 endorses and the prior family used at Strength Foundation III.

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9; WS-A5). WARM_UP required (PAS-D9); COOL_DOWN optional. Order per PAS §11.2: compounds first, isolation last; cables/machines appropriate; no core-compound minimum. Phase label carried in the slot name (PAS §7.1).

- **Push day:** chest/shoulder/triceps — press compound(s) first, isolation last.
- **Pull day:** back/biceps/rear-delt — row/pull compound(s) first, isolation last.
- **Legs day:** squat- and hinge-pattern compounds first, lower-body isolation last.
- **Upper day:** balanced push/pull, compound-led.
- **Lower day:** squat/hinge balance, compound-led.

Rep schemes and set counts shift by **block**, not week-to-week within a block — the defining feature distinguishing this from the Intermediate program's continuous accumulation.

## Section 4 — Weekly Muscle-Group Coverage

The 5-day PPL+UL structure gives most major groups two weekly exposures, with weekly set totals shifting by block:

| Muscle group | Accumulation (Wks 1–3) | Intensification (Wks 5–9) |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | 16–20 | 12–16 |
| Back | 18–22 | 14–18 |
| Shoulders | 16–20 | 12–16 |
| Biceps / Triceps | 12–16 each | 9–12 each |
| Quads | 16–20 | 12–16 |
| Hamstrings | 14–18 | 10–14 |
| Glutes | 14–18 | 10–14 |
| Calves | 10–14 | 8–12 |

Whole-physique balance is maintained throughout (this is a general, not specialized, program); the **block-driven rise and fall of weekly volume** — high in accumulation, trimmed in intensification as load rises — is the program's signature, distinct from the Intermediate program's single monotonic climb.

## Section 5 — Progression Blueprint

- **Block Periodization (Model 3):** the program's overall structure (Section 2). Each block has its own rep/volume/intensity character; the athlete progresses *through* the blocks, not just within a single arc.
- **Double Progression (Model 2):** governs load within each block — rep ranges prescribed per phase (e.g., accumulation 10–15, intensification 6–10); add load at the top of the range. Two-stage failure rule applies (Family Research Deliverable 3).
- **RPE:** permitted (PAS-D3, ADVANCED) and genuinely useful here for managing intensification-phase load against accumulated fatigue.

This is the family's **only** program where progression is multi-block rather than single-arc — the structural distinction from Muscle Building Intermediate and the conceptual payoff of the Intermediate → Advanced handoff.

---

## Section 6 — Volume Blueprint

- **Per session:** 5–8 exercises, 18–30 sets (PAS-D11); the upper end is permissible at ADVANCED (PAS §10.2) and expected in accumulation-phase sessions.
- **Per week:** Section 4 distribution; every group inside 10–20 sets/week, varying by block.
- **Session duration:** within or slightly above HYPERTROPHY's 50–80 min guideline at peak accumulation volume — expected for ADVANCED, no deviation note required (PAS §10.2).
- **Guardrail check:** inside PAS-D11 and §11.2 across all blocks. No deviation justification required.

---

## Section 7 — Recovery Blueprint

- **Recovery is built into the block structure** — the two deloads (Wks 4, 10) are scheduled discharge points between blocks, and the intensification block's reduced volume is itself a partial recovery relative to accumulation.
- **Rest-day placement:** 5 sessions/week leaves two rest days; the PPL+UL arrangement is sequenced so no muscle group is loaded hard on consecutive days, and at least one full rest day separates the heaviest sessions.
- **Intra-session rest:** ADVANCED guidance (PAS §10.3) — 120–240s required for primary compounds, optional for isolation; reference-only (EPA1-D2).
- **Fatigue management:** RPE-regulated (PAS-D3), most actively in the intensification block.

## Section 8 — Successor / Predecessor Logic

- **Predecessor — Muscle Building Intermediate (Sort 6).** Inherits an athlete with full Upper/Lower competency, a completed accumulation block (volume tolerance), one completed deload, and readiness for the model jump from single-arc (Double Progression + Volume Accumulation) to **multi-block (Double Progression + Block Periodization)** plus the step from 4 to 5 days/week. This is the family's one genuine progression-*model* jump.
- **Successor — terminal.** Per `Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.2, an athlete graduating Muscle Building Advanced sees no "What's Next" on W-3 (`successorProgramId` null). The family's general ladder ends here; further progression would require a future catalog rung above it, subject to §2.2 family-cap governance (the family is at the 5-program cap).

## Section 10 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE.**

Muscle Building Advanced is the cleanest distinction case in the family: it is the sole ADVANCED, 5-day, 12-week, Block-Periodization program, with no same-level peer and therefore no convergence risk. It is sharply differentiated from its own predecessor by level, frequency, volume, deload count, and — most importantly — a genuinely different progression model. It is the direct structural analogue of Strength Foundation III, which carried the same "only Block Periodization program in its family" justification.

**Scope guard:** this verdict clears Muscle Building Advanced and completes the family's five Stage 1 Blueprints. Whether the **family** is ready to advance to Stage 2 is the subject of the Family-Level Stage 1 Review (authored immediately after this Blueprint), not this single-program document.

**Confidence:** High.
