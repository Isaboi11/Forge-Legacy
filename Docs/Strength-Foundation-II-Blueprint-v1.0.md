## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Purpose

Strength Foundation II is the intermediate rung of the pure Strength ladder. It takes an athlete who has mastered the five primary barbell patterns under a fixed, externally-prescribed weight ladder and teaches them to self-manage load within a prescribed rep range — the first program in the family to ask the athlete to make that decision rather than follow it. It is also the first rung to carry an explicit, co-equal muscle-building alignment alongside its strength alignment, reflecting the broader accessory volume an intermediate athlete can now both recover from and requires for continued adaptation.

### Target Athlete

An athlete with roughly 1–3 years of consistent barbell training (the INTERMEDIATE definition, PCA §6) who is familiar with all five primary movement patterns and has exhausted the returns of pure linear weight progression — i.e., they can no longer add weight to the bar every session. They want continued strength gains, are ready to take on the responsibility of self-regulating load within a target rep range, and welcome (rather than merely tolerate) the additional muscular development that intermediate-level accessory volume provides.

### Included Athlete Profiles

- A graduate of Strength Foundation I who has completed the program and can no longer add load every session under linear progression.
- A graduate of Powerbuilding Foundation arriving via the Ecosystem Amendment 001 convergence — already carrying a `BUILD_MUSCLE` alignment from entry and now stepping up to the same INTERMEDIATE progression model.
- A self-directed athlete with 1–3 years of barbell experience who is technically competent on the primary lifts but has never trained under a structured double-progression model and wants one.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

- A true beginner with no barbell history or no reliable technique on the primary patterns — directed to Strength Foundation I (single-goal entry) or Powerbuilding Foundation (dual-goal entry).
- An athlete who wants strength **and** muscle from day one but has not yet trained at intermediate level — directed to Powerbuilding Foundation, the dedicated BEGINNER dual-goal door, which converges back into this program afterward.
- An advanced athlete (3+ years, understands periodization, self-regulates intensity) seeking periodized phase structure and a pure-strength single goal — directed to Strength Foundation III.

### Primary Goals

`BUILD_STRENGTH` — continued maximal-strength development on the primary compound lifts, now driven by self-managed progression within rep ranges rather than fixed session-to-session weight jumps.

### Secondary Goals

`BUILD_MUSCLE` — co-equal in the catalog metadata, expressed primarily through expanded, intermediate-appropriate accessory volume. This is not a token addition: it is one of the two axes on which Strength Foundation II is distinct from Strength Foundation I (see Section 2).

## Section 2 — Program Positioning

Strength Foundation II must prove it is a genuine, distinct rung rather than a relabeling of its neighbors. Per `Strength-Family-Research-v1.0.md`'s distinctiveness table, its case rests on **two independent axes: Progression Model and Goal Alignment.**

### Why Strength Foundation II instead of Strength Foundation I (it is not "SF I continued longer")

Strength Foundation I runs **Linear Progression** with a single `BUILD_STRENGTH` alignment over 8 weeks / 3 days. Strength Foundation II changes *what controls the load* — moving from a fixed externally-prescribed weight ladder to **Double Progression**, where the athlete works inside a rep range and advances load only after hitting the top of that range. This is a change in kind, not duration: an athlete who simply ran Strength Foundation I for ten weeks instead of eight would still be adding fixed weight under linear progression and would stall, because linear progression is precisely what the intermediate athlete has exhausted. Strength Foundation II also adds a co-equal `BUILD_MUSCLE` alignment (absent at SF I) and a fourth weekly training day (vs. SF I's three), enabling the accessory volume that intermediate adaptation requires. The two programs differ on progression model, goal alignment, and frequency — three structured axes, not elapsed weeks.

### Why Strength Foundation II instead of Strength Foundation III (it is not "SF III scaled down")

Strength Foundation III is the family's only program running **Block Periodization** (Model 3) over its overall structure, layered on top of Double Progression for accessories — the only program in the entire Strength family carrying both models, confirmed against PAS §7.1's program lists. Strength Foundation II runs Double Progression **only**, with no periodized phase structure governing the primary lifts. Scaling Strength Foundation III "down" would mean removing Block Periodization — but Block Periodization *is* Strength Foundation III's defining architecture, so what remains is not a smaller SF III, it is a different program (the one this Blueprint describes). Strength Foundation II also carries a `BUILD_MUSCLE` co-alignment that Strength Foundation III deliberately sheds (SF III returns to pure `BUILD_STRENGTH`), and runs 10 weeks vs. 12. The distinction is a presence-or-absence of a programming model, not a magnitude of weights.

### Why Strength Foundation II instead of Powerbuilding Foundation (it is not "Powerbuilding Foundation with more weeks")

Powerbuilding Foundation is a **BEGINNER** program running **Linear Progression**, serving the athlete who wants strength and muscle from their very first session. Strength Foundation II is an **INTERMEDIATE** program running **Double Progression**. Adding weeks to Powerbuilding Foundation would produce a longer linear-progression beginner program, which stalls for exactly the reason linear progression is level-bound — it would not become Strength Foundation II, which is defined by its progression model and its intermediate athlete, not its length. The two programs *do* share the `BUILD_STRENGTH, BUILD_MUSCLE` alignment, which is precisely why Powerbuilding Foundation's successor is Strength Foundation II: they are the same ladder, not competitors. The distinction between them is level and progression model, the same distinction that separates Strength Foundation I from Strength Foundation II.

### Convergence node — the two-door entry

Strength Foundation II is the convergence point of the family's two BEGINNER doors (Ecosystem Amendment 001). An athlete entering via Strength Foundation I (single-goal) and an athlete entering via Powerbuilding Foundation (dual-goal) both arrive here, and for both the model transition is identical: **Linear Progression → Double Progression**, a function of *level*, not of which BEGINNER program preceded it. The dual-goal preference shapes which BEGINNER door an athlete walks through; it does not create a separate intermediate rung. This convergence is the structural reason Strength Foundation II carries a co-equal `BUILD_MUSCLE` alignment regardless of entry point: the dual-goal athlete must not lose their muscle alignment on arrival, and the single-goal athlete is, by the intermediate stage, both able to recover from and in need of the broader accessory volume that alignment licenses.

## Section 3 — Progression Blueprint

### Progression Model: Double Progression (Model 2)

**Authority:** `Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §7.1 names Strength Foundation II in the Double Progression (Model 2) program list. This is the first appearance of Double Progression in the Strength ladder — Strength Foundation I is Linear, and Strength Foundation III adds Block Periodization on top of Double Progression. Strength Foundation II is the family's clean, single-model Double Progression program.

### What Changes From Strength Foundation I

Under Linear Progression (SF I), the athlete adds a fixed increment of load each session at a fixed rep target; the program decides the load path. Under Double Progression (SF II), the athlete works within a **prescribed rep range** at a held load, adds reps session to session until reaching the top of the range across all working sets, and only then advances the load — restarting at the bottom of the range. The decision of *when* to add weight moves from the program to the athlete (within defined rules). This is the central new competency the program teaches (Section 6) and the reason the program exists as a distinct rung rather than a longer SF I.

### Failure Handling Philosophy

Two-stage failure handling is the family default (`Strength-Family-Research-v1.0.md`), adapted to Double Progression: under this model, "failure" is failing to reach the **top** of the prescribed rep range at the current load, not failing to complete a fixed rep count. A single failed session at a given working load is repeated, not deloaded — one bad session does not indicate a true ceiling. A second consecutive failure at the same load and rep target triggers a defined load reduction and the rep-range progression restarts from there. Movement quality gates advancement: an athlete does not advance load on technically compromised reps even if the rep count was nominally met.

### Performance Expectations

By completion, the athlete should be progressing load on the primary lifts via earned rep-range completion rather than scheduled weight jumps, demonstrating reliable self-regulation of when to add weight, and carrying meaningfully higher accessory volume than they did at Strength Foundation I without recovery breakdown. Specific loads, increments, and rep ranges are Stage 2 authoring decisions and are not assigned here.

## Section 4 — Volume Blueprint

### Relative Volume Position Within the Strength Family

Per `Strength-Family-Research-v1.0.md`'s volume mapping, Strength Foundation II sits in the **middle of the PAS-D11 envelope** for its level. Recoverable capacity has increased relative to the beginner stage — the athlete is no longer purely novel-stimulus-responsive, so more total volume is required to keep producing adaptation. However, Double Progression's self-paced rep-range structure already absorbs part of that increased demand: progress comes from earning reps within a range before load advances, which adds effective volume without the program having to maximize raw set count. Strength Foundation II therefore does not run at the top of its volume envelope; it runs mid-range, with headroom deliberately preserved.

### Relationship to the Co-Equal Muscle Alignment

Because Strength Foundation II carries `BUILD_MUSCLE` as a co-equal alignment, its accessory selections have an independent justification available that Strength Foundation I's do not: an accessory may be chosen because it accumulates hypertrophy stimulus for a muscle group the primary compounds under-serve, even if it does no direct work to improve the primary lifts. (At SF I, every accessory must justify itself by supporting a primary compound.) Both still pass `Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §15 QC-1 ("state in one sentence why this exercise belongs here") — the *sentence* simply has a second valid form at this rung. This accessory-justification license is the practical expression of the `BUILD_MUSCLE` alignment and a primary site of the extra weekly training day's volume.

### Why This Volume Philosophy Exists

The mid-range positioning and preserved headroom are intentional: the family's volume escalation is concentrated later (accessory-heavy at the ADVANCED rung, governed by Block Periodization's phase structure rather than by raw set escalation). Strength Foundation II's job is to introduce self-managed progression and intermediate accessory volume without spending the athlete's recoverable capacity prematurely.

## Section 5 — Recovery Blueprint

### Deload Expectations

Strength Foundation II carries **one deload, at Week 9** (PAS §14 deload schedule: 10 weeks / 4 days, single deload, slots 33–36; the program then finishes with Week 10, slots 37–40). Deload weeks maintain training frequency while cutting primary-compound set volume 40–50% (PAS-D8) — the athlete keeps showing up, they just do meaningfully less work.

### Deload Cadence Is Duration-Driven, Not Level-Driven

Per PAS §8.1 (PAS-D7), deload frequency is set purely off `durationWeeks`, not athlete level. The family roster proves the point cleanly: Strength Foundation II (10 weeks, INTERMEDIATE) and Powerbuilding Foundation (10 weeks, BEGINNER) share an identical single-deload-at-Week-9 schedule despite differing by a full level. Recovery architecture in this family tracks program length, not athlete experience. This means Strength Foundation II's recovery scaffolding is not a "more aggressive" version of SF I's — the deload mechanism is identical in kind; what changes is the surrounding load and the athlete's self-regulation of it.

### Fatigue Management Philosophy

Minimum one full rest day between sessions at all times — `workoutsPerWeek: 4` never implies four consecutive training days. The mid-range volume position (Section 4) is itself a fatigue-management decision: by not running at the top of the volume envelope, the program preserves the athlete's capacity to self-regulate load honestly under Double Progression, which only works if the athlete is recovered enough to judge a genuine rep-range completion from a fatigued miss.

## Section 6 — Competency Blueprint

### Family-Wide Competencies (Assumed Present, Not Re-Taught)

Strength Foundation II assumes the athlete arrives with the full Strength Foundation I (or Powerbuilding Foundation) competency set: reliable technique on all five primary barbell patterns, the work capacity for a structured multi-day strength week, and the discipline to train under a prescribed structure. These are not re-taught.

### New, INTERMEDIATE-Specific Competency

The defining new competency is **self-regulated load progression within a rep range** — the judgment to recognize when all working sets have reached the top of the prescribed range and load should advance, versus when to hold and earn another session's reps. This is the single most important thing Strength Foundation II teaches and the competency Strength Foundation III's Block Periodization assumes already exists. A second, related competency is **managing co-equal goals**: balancing primary-lift strength work against expanded accessory volume without letting accessory fatigue compromise the primary lifts.

### Technical Competency Acquired

On graduation the athlete has trained under a self-paced progression model, can self-manage load within rep ranges, and has carried and recovered from intermediate-level accessory volume — the prerequisites Strength Foundation III's entry contract assumes.

## Section 7 — Successor Readiness

**Strength Foundation II's successor is Strength Foundation III**, confirmed against `Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.3 and §13's catalog table (`successorProgramName: Strength Foundation III`).

### What Graduation Demonstrates

A Strength Foundation II graduate has demonstrated the ability to self-manage load under Double Progression and to recover from intermediate accessory volume. This is exactly the readiness Strength Foundation III requires: per `Strength-Family-Research-v1.0.md`'s succession logic, the Foundation II → III transition is "readiness to absorb periodized phase structure on top of an already-familiar Double Progression model for accessories" (Double Progression → Double Progression + Block Periodization). The model jump is additive — Strength Foundation III keeps Double Progression for accessories and layers Block Periodization over the primary lifts — so the Foundation II graduate is not learning a new accessory model, only adding periodized structure on top of one they already own.

### Inflow From the Convergence

Strength Foundation II is also the *destination* of two predecessor chains: Strength Foundation I → II (Linear → Double Progression) and Powerbuilding Foundation → II (also Linear → Double Progression). The successor entry contract this program offers is identical for both inflows, because the progression-model jump is a function of level, not of which BEGINNER program preceded it. No special handling distinguishes the two arriving athletes at this rung beyond the `BUILD_MUSCLE` alignment that both now share.

## Section 8 — Stress Test

### Does this program solve a distinct athlete problem?

**Yes.** The intermediate athlete who has exhausted linear progression and must learn to self-manage load within a rep range is a real, distinct profile — not a more advanced beginner and not a less advanced periodization athlete. No other Strength-family program serves the athlete at exactly this transition.

### Is it meaningfully different from its neighbors?

**Yes.** It is the only single-model Double Progression program in the ladder (SF I is Linear; SF III adds Block Periodization). It is the first ladder rung carrying a co-equal `BUILD_MUSCLE` alignment. Both differences are structured metadata axes, verified against PAS §7.1 and §13 — not labels.

### Is the distinction larger than simply heavier weights?

**Yes.** What controls load advancement changes in kind: from a program-prescribed fixed ladder (Linear) to athlete-self-regulated rep-range completion (Double Progression). That is a different mechanism, not a heavier version of the same one. The added co-equal muscle alignment and fourth training day are further qualitative shifts.

### Does it justify its place in the catalog?

**Yes, on two independent axes — Progression Model and Goal Alignment.** This is a stronger base than Strength Foundation III's case (which rests primarily on Progression Model plus athlete maturity), because Strength Foundation II differs from its single-goal predecessor on *both* the progression model and the goal alignment. The case does not rest on topology or duration.

### Would the catalog be worse without it?

**Yes.** Without Strength Foundation II there is no rung where the linear-progression graduate learns self-managed progression before encountering Block Periodization — the athlete would face a two-model jump (Linear → Block Periodization) with no intermediate step, and the two BEGINNER doors would have nowhere to converge. Removing it would break the ladder, not trim a redundancy.

### Previously resolved governance items (cited, not re-opened)

The convergence risk once flagged between Strength Foundation II and the now-retired Powerbuilding Intermediate was resolved by Program Ecosystem Amendment 001 (Powerbuilding Intermediate retired; Powerbuilding Foundation redirected to Strength Foundation II). That decision is settled and is not revisited here. This Blueprint introduces no new convergence finding and no retirement candidate.

### Summary

Strength Foundation II is a genuine, distinct rung resting on two independent differentiation axes, occupying a structurally necessary position as both the ladder's intermediate progression-model step and the convergence node for the family's two BEGINNER entry points.
