## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Bodyweight Foundation is the catalog's **flagship zero-equipment beginner entry** — a featured program (PCA §5.3) designed for the lowest possible barrier to starting: no equipment, train anywhere. Its goal is `GENERAL_FITNESS` ("well-rounded physical capability, beginner baseline" — `Program-Catalog-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.4). The intent is to build a **general-fitness base and foundational bodyweight movement competency** — teaching the fundamental human movement patterns (squat, push, pull, hinge, brace) at bodyweight, so the athlete can later progress to bodyweight strength (Bodyweight Strength) or any other path. It is **not** strength-specialized and **not** fat-loss/conditioning-specialized; conditioning is an incidental byproduct, not a goal (Section 6 — the crux of the convergence test).

### Target Athlete

- **General athlete** (PEA §5.4), absolute beginner (PAS §3.2 BEGINNER), with **no equipment** (bodyweight; pull-up bar / bands optional).
- Wants a general-fitness starting point with zero equipment barrier — travel-friendly, train-anywhere.
- Not seeking equipment-based strength (Home Strength Foundation) or fat-loss/conditioning (Home Conditioning) as a primary goal.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Owns dumbbells/bands and wants equipment-based strength | This program uses no external load; progression is rep/variation-based | **Home Strength Foundation** (Sort 22) |
| Primary goal is fat loss / conditioning | General-fitness movement focus; conditioning is incidental | Home Conditioning (Sort 21) — the pair under test |
| Has gym access and wants barbell strength | Bodyweight-only | Strength Foundation I |
| Already competent at bodyweight basics | Beginner movement-competency dose | Bodyweight Strength (Sort 19) |

### Expected Outcomes

A general-fitness base and competent execution of fundamental bodyweight movement patterns over the 6-week block, with the rep/variation progression habit established — leaving the athlete ready for Bodyweight Strength.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Every exercise is bodyweight (± pull-up bar/bands) — no external load (verifiable against §11.7's "not available" list).
- Progression is visible (QC-2) via rising reps and harder variations across the 6 weeks — not added load.
- Whole-body movement-pattern coverage each session; conditioning demand is incidental, not the organizing principle.

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 6 weeks; **no mandatory deload** (PAS-D7, under 7 weeks — "All weeks training," PAS §14).
- **Frequency:** 3 sessions/week, full-body each session.
- **Weekly structure:** 3 full-body bodyweight sessions covering squat, push, pull, hinge, and core patterns, with rotated emphasis to avoid identical sessions. WARM_UP required; COOL_DOWN optional (PAS-D9).
- **Periodization summary:** a single short beginner block on **linear rep progression** (PAS §7.2 / §11.7) — add reps within a target range, then progress to a harder variation when the rep ceiling is reached. No load periodization (there is no external load).

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). FULL_BODY requires **WARM_UP**; COOL_DOWN optional (PAS-D9). Equipment ceiling per §11.7 (bodyweight, pull-up bar, bands; no barbell/rack/cables/machines). Each session: WARM_UP (dynamic raise) → MAIN: a bodyweight lower-body pattern (squat/hinge variations), an upper push (push-up variations), an upper pull (rows/pull-ups or band pulls), and core/brace work → (COOL_DOWN optional). MAIN sits within PAS-D11 (4–7 exercises / 12–20 sets), 40–70 min (§10.2); "HOME may use higher reps with lower weights" (§10.1) — here, higher reps at bodyweight.

Beginner emphasis: movement quality first; harder variations are earned, not rushed (§11.7's caution against advanced bodyweight technique without progressions).

---

## Section 4 — Progression Philosophy

**Linear rep progression (Model 1 variant for bodyweight; §11.7):** the progressed variable is **reps, then variation difficulty** — increase reps on the current movement across the slot sequence, and when the rep ceiling is reached, introduce a harder variation (e.g., "Master 3 × 15 before progressing to Pike Push-Ups"). There is **no external load** — this is the defining bodyweight progression mechanic, and it is what separates this program's progression from the home-equipment and gym strength programs (Section 10). No RPE at BEGINNER (PAS-D3).

---

## Section 5 — Movement-Selection Philosophy

Bodyweight movements only (+ pull-up bar/bands optional), chosen to be **foundational, learnable, and arranged in variation ladders** so progression has somewhere to go (easier → harder regressions/progressions of each pattern). Selection covers all fundamental patterns each week (full-body). Avoid bodyweight movements that require advanced technique without an on-ramp (§11.7 — e.g., no handstand push-ups at BEGINNER). The selection is **movement-development-oriented**: the goal is competent, progressable movement patterns, not metabolic output.

---

## Section 6 — Conditioning Role

**Incidental and minimal.** Full-body bodyweight work elevates heart rate as a byproduct, but conditioning is **not a goal** of this program (its goal is GENERAL_FITNESS via movement competency, not IMPROVE_CONDITIONING). Sessions are organized around movement-pattern development with adequate rest for quality reps — **not** around sustained metabolic work, work:rest density, or caloric output. **This is the baseline the Home Conditioning convergence test turns on** (Section 11): Home Conditioning must make conditioning the deliberate organizing principle; Bodyweight Foundation deliberately does not.

---

## Section 7 — Recovery Strategy

- **6-week, no-deload, 3-day full-body** structure with rest days between sessions; bodyweight loads are low, so absolute fatigue is modest and a deload is not mandated (PAS-D7).
- Beginner rest periods (PAS §10.3 — adequate rest for quality reps; the program is not rest-compressed).

---

## Section 8 — Expected Outcomes

By the end of the 6-week block: a general-fitness base, competent execution of fundamental bodyweight patterns, and an established rep/variation progression habit — readiness for Bodyweight Strength.

---

## Section 9 — Predecessor / Successor Logic

- **Predecessor — none.** The catalog's lowest-barrier BEGINNER entry (FEATURED).
- **Successor — Bodyweight Strength (Sort 19).** Hands forward movement competency and the rep/variation progression habit; Bodyweight Strength then builds bodyweight *strength* (harder progressions, BUILD_STRENGTH co-goal) en route to Bodyweight Performance (ADVANCED). Bodyweight Foundation is the **on-ramp to the only FULL_BODY ladder reaching ADVANCED** — a structural role Home Conditioning (a standalone terminal) does not have.

## Section 10 — Distinction Rationale & the Bodyweight-vs-Home-Equipment Athlete (req 5)

### What makes a bodyweight athlete different from a home-equipment athlete — without relying on environment

Both Bodyweight Foundation and Home Strength Foundation share the `HOME` environment enum, so environment **cannot** carry this distinction. It rests on four non-environmental axes:

| Axis | Bodyweight athlete (Bodyweight Foundation) | Home-equipment athlete (Home Strength Foundation) |
|---|---|---|
| **Progression mechanics** | Rep + leverage/**variation** progression — no external load to add | **Dumbbell/band load** progression (external load), layered with reps/unilateral |
| **Training style** | Calisthenics — bodyweight movement mastery | Equipment-based loaded strength training |
| **Athlete constraints** | **Zero equipment** — trains anywhere, travel-friendly | **Owns dumbbells/bands** — a different access reality |
| **Movement development** | Develops patterns → leverage/skill progressions (the path to advanced calisthenics in Bodyweight Performance) | Develops loaded strength patterns |

This makes the equipment-tier distinction concrete from the bodyweight side, completing what the Home Strength Foundation Blueprint established from the home-equipment side: **the two are different training disciplines with different overload mechanisms, not the same program in different rooms.** Environment is the same; everything that matters for training is different.

### Baseline statement (for the Home Conditioning test)

Bodyweight Foundation is **movement-competency-forward and general-fitness-oriented**: bodyweight movement-pattern development via rep/variation progression, with conditioning incidental. This is the fixed reference Home Conditioning must prove it diverges from.

## Section 11 — Convergence Test for Home Conditioning

**The question:** *Can Home Conditioning be meaningfully different from Bodyweight Foundation despite both existing in the HOME family and relying on limited-equipment (bodyweight/minimal) training?*

The pair shares level (BEGINNER), environment (HOME), duration (6wk), frequency (3/wk), total (18), and no-deload — differing only on goal (GENERAL_FITNESS vs LOSE_FAT + IMPROVE_CONDITIONING) and successor (Bodyweight Foundation → Bodyweight Strength; Home Conditioning terminal). **Critically, both can use the same bodyweight/minimal-equipment toolkit** — so the distinction cannot rest on equipment. It must rest on **training target**. Home Conditioning's Blueprint must demonstrate **all four** (evaluated with goal tags hidden):

| # | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Metabolic-forward emphasis** | Built around sustained metabolic/cardiovascular work (work:rest circuits/intervals) as the organizing principle — the inverse of Bodyweight Foundation's movement-competency-forward, adequately-rested structure. |
| 2 | **Fat-loss / caloric-output orientation (honors LOSE_FAT)** | Structure deliberately serves caloric expenditure (circuit density, elevated/sustained heart rate) — absent in Bodyweight Foundation, where conditioning is incidental. |
| 3 | **Deliberate work-capacity development (honors IMPROVE_CONDITIONING)** | Intervals/circuits at genuine conditioning intensity that progress work capacity — vs Bodyweight Foundation's movement-pattern focus where heart rate is a byproduct, not a target. |
| 4 | **Non-derivability** | A reviewer can tell them apart with goal tags hidden: metabolic circuits-for-fat-loss-and-conditioning vs a general-fitness bodyweight movement-pattern program. **If Home Conditioning is merely "Bodyweight Foundation done faster / with compressed rest," it FAILS** → re-differentiate / merge / retire (Family-Research Deliverable 7 / PEA §2.2). |

**The subtlety to flag for Home Conditioning's authoring:** because both programs draw on the same bodyweight/minimal toolkit, this is a **training-target inversion** (metabolic conditioning vs movement competency), not an equipment or emphasis-of-load inversion like the resistance pairs. A faster tempo alone does not pass — Home Conditioning must be *organized around* conditioning (work:rest as the primary variable, caloric/work-capacity intent), the way Athletic Conditioning Foundation is in the GYM family, but at home.

## Section 12 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE Bodyweight Foundation (baseline established).**

**Why it independently justifies its place in the catalog (req 9):** Bodyweight Foundation is the catalog's **lowest-barrier entry** — zero equipment, train-anywhere — and one of only two FEATURED programs (PCA §5.3), making it a deliberate front-door for the large segment of athletes who start with nothing. It is the **on-ramp to the only FULL_BODY ladder reaching ADVANCED** (Bodyweight Strength → Bodyweight Performance). It is distinct from Home Strength Foundation on the four non-environmental axes (Section 10) and from its own successor by level. Its existence does not depend on Home Conditioning's absence — it stands on its athlete (the zero-equipment beginner), its role (featured front-door + ladder on-ramp), and its training content (movement-competency-forward bodyweight progression).

**The pair is NOT resolved.** This pass establishes the baseline and the four-part falsifiable test; closing the convergence risk requires Home Conditioning's own Blueprint to pass that test. **Home Conditioning is not authored in this pass.**

**Confidence:** High for Bodyweight Foundation's own justification; the residual uncertainty is entirely on the Home Conditioning side and is handed forward as a test.
