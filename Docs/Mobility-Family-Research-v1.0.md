## Deliverable 1 — Family Identity

A Mobility program develops **flexibility, joint health, and movement quality** through near-daily, low-intensity mobility practice (`Program-Catalog-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.1: "Flexibility, injury prevention, joint health, recovery"; §3.4 IMPROVE_MOBILITY: "Flexibility, joint health, movement quality"; PAS §11.8). The family's identity is distinct from every other family's: it is the catalog's **supportive / prehab / recovery practice**, designed to serve **all athlete types** alongside their primary training — not a hypertrophy/strength/conditioning stimulus. Its structural signature (PAS §11.8) is unique: **MAIN-section only — no WARM_UP or COOL_DOWN** (PAS-D9), 5 near-daily 10–30-minute sessions, static holds (`durationSeconds`) and dynamic drills (sets × reps), sequenced global/breathwork → joint-specific → deep long holds.

Mobility's identity is **practice, not progression toward a ceiling** — which is precisely why the family is small (two rungs) and complete at INTERMEDIATE, and why its inter-rung distinction is the leanest in the catalog (Deliverable 7).

## Deliverable 2 — Target Athlete Segments

Per `Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §2.1 and §5.4, the Mobility family serves **All** athlete types — it is the one family whose audience is not a single type. Mobility is supportive practice that any athlete (Strength, Bodybuilding, Running, Hybrid, General, etc.) runs alongside their primary program for joint health, recovery, and movement quality.

The two programs segment this universal audience by **experience only**:
- **Mobility Foundation (BEGINNER):** the athlete new to structured mobility work — foundational ranges, basic holds, learning the practice (PAS §3.2 BEGINNER: no assumed technique).
- **Mobility Intermediate (INTERMEDIATE):** the athlete familiar with mobility patterns — deeper ranges, more challenging positions, longer holds (PAS §3.2 INTERMEDIATE: familiar with the primary movement patterns).

There is no goal, environment, or athlete-type segmentation (all shared) — only the experience/content-depth axis. This is the family's defining leanness and the crux of the distinction test.

## Deliverable 3 — Progression Philosophy

The family's progression model is **uniform: Time-Based Progression (Model 5)** at both levels (PAS §7.2: "MOBILITY → Time-Based Progression"; §7.1 Model 5 names both programs). Duration (in seconds) increases per slot rather than load or volume — the progression variable is endurance of a position/hold (§7.1 Model 5).

Critically, PAS §11.8 makes the **Foundation-vs-Intermediate progression explicit**, and this is the repository-grounded differentiator the whole family turns on:

> "**Progression:** Time-based (Model 5). Increase hold durations from earlier to later weeks. **Introduce more challenging variations in intermediate programs.**"

So the progression has two dimensions: (a) **longer holds** across weeks (within a program), and (b) **more challenging variations** at the Intermediate level (between programs). Mobility Intermediate is therefore *defined by the standard* as more than longer holds — it introduces harder mobility variations (deeper positions, more demanding poses/flows). This is the same kind of content-progression that validated the Bodyweight ladder via §11.7's "harder variations" rule — here grounded in §11.8.

Because the model is uniform, **progression model is not the program differentiator** — the *content depth* the model is applied to (basic vs more-challenging variations + longer holds) is.

## Deliverable 4 — Volume Philosophy

`Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §10.1 (PAS-D11) sets the MOBILITY envelope at **5–10 duration-based holds** in the MAIN section; §10.2 sets session duration at **10–30 minutes** (the catalog's shortest sessions). Volume is measured in **holds and durations**, not sets or load — the inert volume axis for this family. Mobility Intermediate's "more volume" (30 vs 20 total sessions) is a function of its longer duration (6 vs 4 weeks at the same 5/wk), plus longer/harder holds within sessions — not a higher session-count-per-week (both are 5/wk).

---

## Deliverable 5 — Recovery Philosophy

Recovery is not a *periodization* concern in this family — **the family itself is recovery/prehab.** Both programs are low-intensity, near-daily practice; neither carries a deload (PAS-D7: 4–6-week programs have no mandatory deload, and a mobility practice does not accumulate the fatigue a deload discharges). Mobility's role across the whole catalog is to *be* the recovery and joint-health support that other families' athletes layer onto their training. The 5-day near-daily cadence reflects practice consistency, not training stress.

---

## Deliverable 6 — Modality / Session Selection Philosophy

Selection follows PAS §11.8: **static holds** (prescribed in `durationSeconds`) and **dynamic mobility drills** (sets × reps) — never `sets` for static holds. Session organization is fixed: begin with global mobility or breathwork → progress to joint-specific work → end with deep, long-hold stretches; never front-load the hardest/deepest holds in Week 1; static stretching never precedes dynamic work. **MAIN section only** — no warm-up/cool-down (PAS-D9). Sessions stay ≤30 minutes (§11.8).

Selection emphasis advances by level per §11.8: Mobility Intermediate introduces **more challenging variations** (deeper positions, harder poses) on top of the same modality toolkit — the exercise-side expression of the level distinction.

## Deliverable 7 — Distinction Between Programs (the central question)

**Governing principle (carried from prior families):** a program must justify existence by a distinct athlete, progression profile, volume profile, or goal — not by topology. The Mobility pair is the **hardest application of this principle in the catalog**, because it is the leanest-differentiated pair: level + duration + total are the *only* differences (goal, environment, frequency, athlete type, and progression model are all identical).

**The question: Is Mobility Intermediate a real progression, or just Mobility Foundation extended?**

**Answer: a REAL progression — but the leanest-justified case in the catalog, grounded in PAS §11.8 and load-bearing on content.**
- **Repository-grounded differentiator:** PAS §11.8 explicitly states Intermediate programs **"introduce more challenging variations"** (beyond merely longer holds). So the standard itself defines Mobility Intermediate as harder *content*, not just more weeks.
- **Level definition:** PAS §3.2 distinguishes BEGINNER (no assumed technique → foundational ranges/basic holds) from INTERMEDIATE (familiar with patterns → deeper ranges, more demanding positions).
- **Time-Based progression:** longer hold durations + harder variations = a genuine Time-Based content progression (Model 5), the same mechanism that validated the Bodyweight ladder's leverage progression via §11.7.

**This makes it (A) a genuine training-content distinction, not (B) catalog topology** — *provided the content progression is real in authored structure.* Because the metadata distinction is so lean (level + duration only), the burden falls almost entirely on Stage 1 content.

**Convergence risk assessment & the falsifiable Stage 1 test.** The single Mobility Foundation ↔ Mobility Intermediate pair carries the catalog's leanest distinction. It **passes Stage 0A** (§11.8 grounds a genuine content progression), but is **handed to Stage 1 as a falsifiable test** — Mobility Intermediate's Blueprint must demonstrate:
1. **More challenging variations** (deeper positions / harder poses / more complex flows) than Mobility Foundation — the §11.8 differentiator, in authored detail;
2. **Meaningfully longer / deeper holds** (greater range-of-motion demand), not the same holds for two more weeks;
3. **Non-derivability** — a reviewer can tell Mobility Intermediate is not Mobility Foundation run 2 weeks longer.

If Stage 1 cannot show this, the pair should be re-examined under the family's governing principle (§2.2 / Deliverable-7 remediation). This is the same baseline → falsifiable-test pattern used for the other families' lean pairs — and fittingly, the catalog's leanest pair is the final family's test.

This is a genuine complete-by-design terminal (no journey gap), distinct from the terminal-by-unbuilt-successor cases (Running Base II, Lower Body Intermediate, Home Strength Foundation) flagged elsewhere.

## Governance Findings & Stage 0A Verdict

**Verdict: the Mobility family PASSES Stage 0A.** It is the final ungoverned family; with this pass, every family in the catalog has completed Stage 0A.

- **Both programs are justified.** The family identity (universal supportive/prehab mobility practice) is distinct; the two rungs are distinguished by experience/content-depth, grounded in PAS §11.8's explicit "more challenging variations in intermediate programs."
- **Convergence: the leanest pair in the catalog, resolved-pending-Stage-1-proof.** Mobility Foundation ↔ Mobility Intermediate differ on level + duration + total only. The distinction is genuine (content progression per §11.8) but load-bearing on Stage 1 authored content — handed forward as a three-part falsifiable test (Deliverable 7). It is the catalog's hardest "is the second rung real?" case.
- **No retirement candidates.** No program rests on topology (the §11.8 content progression is genuine), and structurally the family is at the **2-program minimum** (PEA §2.1) — retiring Mobility Intermediate would drop it below the floor (the Running logic). Mobility Intermediate is the **watch program** (leanest-justified in the catalog), not a retirement candidate.
- **No journey-gap finding.** Mobility Intermediate is complete-by-design terminal (no Mobility Advanced roadmapped) — a clean terminus.
- **Stage 1 blueprint order:** **Mobility Foundation first** (establish the beginner baseline), **then Mobility Intermediate** (authored against Deliverable 7's falsifiable test).

**Lessons applied:** the Powerbuilding-retirement anti-pattern (topology-only justification) is the central risk for the leanest pair — resolved because §11.8 grounds genuine content progression, not topology. The Bodyweight-ladder precedent (§11.7-grounded "harder variations" validating a lean ladder) is the closest analogue. The Running 2-program-minimum logic governs the no-retirement finding. The Muscle-Building/Conditioning baseline → falsifiable-test pattern governs the Stage 1 hand-off.
