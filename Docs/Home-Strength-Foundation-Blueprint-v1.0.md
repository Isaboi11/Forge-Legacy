## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Home Strength Foundation is the **beginner strength program for the athlete who trains with minimal home equipment** — adjustable or fixed dumbbells, resistance bands, and a pull-up bar — and **no barbell, rack, cables, or machines** (PAS §11.7). It occupies the **middle equipment tier**: above the bodyweight-only athlete (served by the Bodyweight ladder) and below the full-commercial-gym athlete (served by the Strength family). Its goals are `BUILD_STRENGTH` and `GENERAL_FITNESS` — it builds foundational strength *and* well-rounded fitness using the home-equipment toolkit, training the whole body each session.

The program's entire distinct existence rests on that equipment tier. This Blueprint's job is to show the tier produces **genuinely different training content**, not a relabeled version of Strength Foundation I (barbell) or Bodyweight Strength (no equipment).

### Target Athlete

- **General athlete** (PEA §5.4), new to structured strength training (PAS §3.2 BEGINNER), who owns or has access to **limited home equipment** (dumbbells, bands, pull-up bar) but not a barbell/rack.
- Wants strength + general fitness at home; cannot or does not want to train at a commercial gym; is not limited to pure bodyweight.
- Trains 3 days/week.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Has no equipment at all (bodyweight only) | This program assumes dumbbells/bands; its progression depends on external load | **Bodyweight Foundation / Bodyweight Strength** (FULL_BODY, bodyweight-only) |
| Has full commercial-gym access | Barbell linear loading (back squat, bench, deadlift) is the more direct strength path | **Strength Foundation I** (STRENGTH, GYM) |
| Primary goal is fat loss / conditioning at home | Strength-forward, not metabolic | Home Conditioning (Sort 21) |
| Wants advanced bodyweight skill | Equipment-based, not calisthenics-progression | Bodyweight Strength / Performance |

### Expected Outcomes

A measurable beginner-level strength increase (dumbbell/band load and rep progression on fundamental patterns) and improved general fitness over the 8-week block, using only home equipment — leaving the athlete ready for the roadmapped Home Strength Intermediate (when built) or a transition to a gym strength program if equipment access changes.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Every MAIN exercise is performable with **home equipment only** (dumbbells, bands, pull-up bar, bodyweight) — verifiable against PAS §11.7's "not available" list (no barbell/rack/cables/machines).
- Strength progression is visible (QC-2) via dumbbell/band **load progression where increments allow**, supplemented by **rep and unilateral progression** where load increments are too coarse.
- Whole-body coverage each session (FULL_BODY); the GENERAL_FITNESS co-goal is honored (not a pure single-lift strength specialist).

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 8 weeks (PAS-D7 → one deload).
- **Frequency:** 3 sessions/week (beginner-appropriate, full-body each session).
- **Weekly structure:** 3 full-body sessions (PAS §11.7), each covering a push, a pull, a squat/hinge lower-body pattern, and accessory/core — all with home equipment. Rotated emphasis across the week to avoid repeating the identical session.
- **Deload:** one, Week 7 (slots 19–21), peak Week 8 (slots 22–24), per PAS §14 — reduce volume/load, maintain three sessions (PAS-D8).
- **Periodization summary:** a single beginner block on **Linear Progression** (PAS §7.2 FULL_BODY), with a home-equipment twist (Section 5): where dumbbell load increments are too coarse to add weekly, progression continues via **reps, then unilateral/tempo/band-tension variation** before the next load jump.

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). FULL_BODY requires **WARM_UP**; COOL_DOWN optional (PAS-D9). Equipment ceiling per PAS §11.7 (available: bodyweight, pull-up bar, bands, limited dumbbells; not available: barbell, rack, cables, machines).

Each session: WARM_UP (dynamic raise) → MAIN: a lower-body strength pattern (e.g., dumbbell goblet/split squat, dumbbell or band Romanian deadlift), an upper push (dumbbell/band press), an upper pull (pull-up / band or dumbbell row), plus accessory/core — strength-led, full-body, home-equipment → (COOL_DOWN optional). MAIN sits within PAS-D11 (4–7 exercises / 12–20 sets), 40–70 min (§10.2); "HOME may use higher reps with lower weights" (§10.1) is the operative volume note.

Beginner instruction emphasis (PAS §11.1's "movement mastery before load" generalized): foundational patterns taught before load is pushed — the home equivalent of the Strength family's beginner technique gate.

---

## Section 4 — Resistance / Strength Emphasis

Strength is the program's spine, developed with the **home-equipment toolkit**: dumbbell compound and unilateral work, band-resisted patterns, pull-up/row pulling, loaded carries. The GENERAL_FITNESS co-goal keeps it a **full-body generalist strength program** (every session trains the whole body) rather than a specialized lift-focused program. Load is moderate and home-appropriate; intensity is managed by reps and variation as much as by absolute load.

---

## Section 5 — Progression Strategy

- **Linear Progression** (PAS §7.2 FULL_BODY) is the model, but its *mechanics differ from barbell linear progression by necessity*: home dumbbells have coarse increments (often 5 lb jumps, or fixed pairs), so the program cannot micro-load the way Strength Foundation I micro-loads a barbell. Progression therefore proceeds in a **layered order**: (1) add reps within a target range on the current load; (2) when reps are maxed, progress a **unilateral or tempo/band-tension variation** to raise difficulty without a load jump; (3) add load when the next dumbbell/band increment is reachable. This layered progression is the **home-equipment signature** — distinct from both the barbell load ladder (SF I) and pure bodyweight rep/variation (Bodyweight ladder).
- **No RPE** at BEGINNER (PAS-D3).

---

## Section 6 — Recovery Strategy

- **3-day full-body cadence** with rest days between sessions is the beginner recovery mechanism; home-equipment loads are moderate, keeping absolute fatigue manageable.
- **Deload Week 7** reduces volume/load, maintains frequency (PAS-D8).
- Beginner rest periods (PAS §10.3 — 60–90s typical, self-regulated).

---

## Section 7 — Expected Outcomes

By the end of the block: foundational full-body strength built with home equipment (load + rep + variation gains on fundamental patterns), improved general fitness, and competent technique on the home-equipment movement pool — readiness for the roadmapped Home Strength Intermediate, or a clean transition to a gym strength program if the athlete gains gym access.

## Section 9 — Pressure-Test Evaluation (the family's biggest governance question)

The Stage 0A research handed three pressure-tests to this program. Each asks whether the distinction is **(A) genuine training content** or **(B) catalog topology**.

### Test 1 — vs Strength Foundation I (cross-family; the deepest test)

**Metadata: identical** — both BEGINNER, 8wk, 3/wk, 24 total. Differ only by category (FULL_BODY vs STRENGTH), environment (HOME vs GYM), and goal (+GENERAL_FITNESS). This is the catalog's sharpest "does environment justify a program?" case.

**Verdict: (A) genuine — but the burden is real and met by equipment-driven content, not by environment alone.** Environment *alone* does not justify it; what does is that the **home-equipment ceiling changes the training content and progression mechanics**, not just the exercise names:
- SF I builds strength through **barbell linear loading** (back squat / bench / deadlift with small, frequent weight jumps) — the most efficient novice strength driver.
- Home Strength Foundation **cannot** do that (no barbell, no micro-loading). It builds strength through **dumbbell/band load + layered rep/unilateral/variation progression** (Section 5) — a materially different progression discipline, plus a GENERAL_FITNESS generalist framing.
This is **not** "SF I with dumbbells swapped for barbells": the progression *model in practice* differs (coarse-increment layered progression vs barbell micro-loading), the exercise pool differs, and the athlete differs (has-home-equipment vs has-full-gym). **It would collapse into (B) topology only if authored as SF I with 1:1 substitutions** — which the success criteria (Section 1) and Section 5 explicitly forbid. The distinction is genuine *provided Stage 2 authoring honors the home-equipment progression model.*

### Test 2 — vs Bodyweight Strength (same family, same goal)

**Same goal** (BUILD_STRENGTH + GENERAL_FITNESS), same environment (HOME). Differ by level (BEG vs INT), frequency (3 vs 4), chain position (terminal vs mid-ladder), and **equipment-style** (home-equipment vs bodyweight-only).

**Verdict: (A) genuine.** Home Strength Foundation uses **external load** (dumbbells/bands); Bodyweight Strength uses **none** — its progression is leverage/variation toward harder calisthenics. Load-based strength and bodyweight-leverage strength are different modalities, not a substitution. Level and frequency reinforce the separation. The distinction is load-bearing on the equipment-style axis (the family's under-specified axis), so the Blueprint makes the equipment assumption explicit (Section 1, 3) to prove it in content.

### Test 3 — the equipment-style axis itself (is it a real distinction or topology?)

**Verdict: a real distinction, structurally under-specified.** The bodyweight-only / home-equipment / full-gym tiers are genuine athlete distinctions that drive different progression mechanics — but the catalog `environment` enum (`HOME`) does not encode the bodyweight-vs-home-equipment split (Stage 0A Deliverable 6); it lives in program names + PAS §11.7 convention, with the structured taxonomy (Environment Tags Amendment) informational/deferred. **This Blueprint resolves the question at the content level** (it specifies the equipment tier and the distinct progression mechanics) and **flags the structural gap for the deferred V1.1 Training Environment Architecture** — it does **not** propose an architecture change.

---

## Section 10 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE.**

Home Strength Foundation is a genuine program: the beginner strength program for the **middle equipment tier** (home equipment — dumbbells/bands), a large and real athlete segment between the bodyweight-only and full-gym audiences. It passes all three pressure-tests on the strength of **equipment-driven training content** — a layered home-equipment progression model (Section 5) that is materially different from both Strength Foundation I's barbell linear loading and Bodyweight Strength's leverage progression — plus a GENERAL_FITNESS generalist framing and a distinct athlete. **It is not topology and not a substitution.**

**Why it survives despite metadata identical to Strength Foundation I:** environment alone justifies nothing, but the *equipment ceiling changes the progression mechanics and exercise pool*, which is genuine training content — demonstrated in this Blueprint, not asserted. This is the same kind of in-structure resolution used for the Body Recomposition and Conditioning convergence pairs.

**The load-bearing caveat (the family's defining watch item, now this program's):** the entire distinction rests on the equipment-style axis, which the catalog does **not** structurally enforce. Therefore:
1. **Stage 2 authoring must honor the home-equipment progression model** (Section 5) and the §11.7 equipment ceiling — if Home Strength Foundation is authored as "Strength Foundation I with dumbbells," it collapses into (B) topology and should be re-examined for retirement/merge. This is the single most important Stage 2 instruction for this program.
2. The **structural under-specification** (no equipment-tier field) is flagged for the deferred V1.1 Training Environment Architecture — **no architecture proposed here.**

**Confidence:** Moderate-to-high. The athlete and the content distinction are genuine; the residual risk is entirely an *authoring-discipline* risk (will Stage 2 preserve the equipment-driven model?), which is why the Stage 2 instruction above is explicit. A negative outcome remains possible only if authoring ignores it.

**Scope guard:** Architecture locked/unchanged; this is the **first** Full Body & Home Stage 1 Blueprint (the watch program, authored first by request). Outstanding: Bodyweight Foundation, Bodyweight Strength, Bodyweight Performance, Home Conditioning Blueprints, then a Full Body & Home Stage 1 Review. The Bodyweight Foundation ↔ Home Conditioning pressure-test (Stage 0A) is still open for those blueprints. No Stage 2 work has begun.

**Recommended next action:** author the remaining four Full Body & Home Blueprints (the Bodyweight ladder + Home Conditioning), resolving the Bodyweight Foundation ↔ Home Conditioning pair, then a Full Body & Home Stage 1 Review.
