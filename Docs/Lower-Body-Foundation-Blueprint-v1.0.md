## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity

Lower Body Foundation is the **lower-body-specialization entry point** of the Muscle Building family: a 3-day-per-week beginner program whose volume is concentrated on the quads, hamstrings, glutes, and calves, with upper-body work held to a light maintenance dose. It serves the beginner who already knows they want lower-body and glute development as their priority, and who wants — or can only commit to — a 3-day training week. It is the on-ramp to Lower Body Intermediate.

### Target Athlete

- New to structured resistance training in this style, per `Program-Authoring-Standard-v1.0.md` §3.2's BEGINNER definition.
- **Primary goal is lower-body and glute development** — goal-differentiated, not a distinct athlete type (`Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §5).
- **Trains 3 days/week** — by preference or availability.
- Accepts upper-body maintenance (not growth) in exchange for a concentrated, manageable lower-body introduction.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Wants balanced whole-body development | This program runs upper body at maintenance only | **Muscle Building Foundation** (the family's general BEGINNER door) |
| Wants/needs a 4-day training week with balanced volume | This program is deliberately 3 days and lower-concentrated | Muscle Building Foundation |
| Ready for higher lower-body volume / 4-day lower-focused training | Beginner dose, 3 days | Lower Body Intermediate (Sort 9) |
| Primary goal is maximal lower-body strength (1RM) | Volume-driven, no 1RM-style primary stimulus | Strength family |

### Expected Outcomes

A beginner-appropriate, concentrated lower-body hypertrophy stimulus across an 8-week block; technical competence on the squat/hinge/hip-thrust patterns and lower-body isolation pool; the ability to self-manage a rep range under double progression; and one completed deload — the readiness package Lower Body Intermediate assumes.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Lower-body groups sit in the 10–20 sets/week band; upper-body work sits **below** the band (maintenance).
- Total weekly working sets are lower-body-dominant (target ≥65–70%, consistent with the Lower Body ladder's identity).
- Week-over-week progression is visible (QC-2) via rising lower-body set counts and/or advancing rep-range targets.

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 8 weeks (PAS-D7 → exactly one deload).
- **Frequency:** **3 sessions/week** — the family's one exception to "Foundation tier = 4 days" (Family Research Deliverable 7), justified because a beginner's lower body cannot recover from four lower-heavy sessions a week, and because the 3-day commitment is itself part of the program's athlete fit.
- **Split:** three **lower-body-dominant** sessions with light emphasis rotation:
  1. **Lower — Quad emphasis** (squat-pattern anchor)
  2. **Lower — Posterior chain emphasis** (hinge-pattern anchor; hamstrings + glutes)
  3. **Lower — Glute / mixed** (hip-thrust-pattern anchor + balanced lower accumulation)

  A small upper-body **maintenance** block (one push pattern, one pull pattern, ~4–6 sets total per week) is folded across two of the three sessions — never a standalone upper day.
- **Deload:** one, Week 7 (slots 19–21), per PAS §14; peak Week 8 (slots 22–24). Maintains frequency, cuts primary volume 40–50% (PAS-D8); likely the athlete's first coached deload.
- **Periodization summary:** single accumulation mesocycle (Volume Accumulation, Model 4) on lower-body work across Weeks 1–6; Double Progression (Model 2) within exercises. No Block Periodization.

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9; WS-A5). WARM_UP required for HYPERTROPHY (PAS-D9); COOL_DOWN optional. Order per PAS §11.2: compounds first, isolation last; cables/machines appropriate; no core-compound minimum; beginner emphasis on learnable, stable patterns.

- **Day 1 — Quad emphasis:** WARM_UP → squat-pattern compound; quad-biased secondary; quad isolation; a hamstring counter-set; calf. *(Upper maintenance: one pull pattern, ~2–3 sets.)*
- **Day 2 — Posterior-chain emphasis:** WARM_UP → hinge-pattern compound; hamstring isolation; glute accessory; calf. *(Upper maintenance: one push pattern, ~2–3 sets.)*
- **Day 3 — Glute / mixed:** WARM_UP → hip-thrust-pattern compound; glute accessory; a quad and a hamstring accessory to balance the week; calf.

Three sessions (not four) is the deliberate beginner-recovery dose; emphasis rotation keeps the same tissue from being maximally loaded on back-to-back training days.

---

## Section 4 — Weekly Muscle-Group Coverage & Volume Distribution

Indicative beginner-dose working sets:

| Muscle group | Region | Target weekly sets | Band position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quads | Lower | 10–12 | within band |
| Hamstrings | Lower | 8–10 | within/low band |
| Glutes | Lower | 9–11 | within band |
| Calves | Lower | 6–8 | low band |
| Back / pull | Upper | 2–3 | **below band (maintenance)** |
| Chest / push | Upper | 2–3 | **below band (maintenance)** |

**Distribution result:** lower-body sets ≈ 33–41, upper-body ≈ 4–6 → lower-body share ≈ **85–88%** (even more lower-dominant than Lower Body Intermediate, because the 3-day structure leaves little room for non-lower work). Lower groups sit inside their bands at a beginner dose; upper-body sits well below — maintenance only.

## Section 5 — Progression Blueprint

- **Double Progression (Model 2):** rep ranges (compounds ~6–10, isolation ~10–15); add load at the top of the range on all sets; two-stage failure rule (Family Research Deliverable 3).
- **Volume Accumulation (Model 4):** lower-body working sets rise across Weeks 1–6 (e.g., 2 → 3 → 4), reset Week 7, peak Week 8. Upper-maintenance sets hold flat and do not accumulate.
- **RPE:** NOT permitted at BEGINNER (PAS-D3); effort guided by rep-range target and technique.

Starting position: **low end** of PAS-D11 (Family Research Deliverable 4 BEGINNER curve), concentrated on the lower body.

---

## Section 6 — Volume Blueprint

- **Per session:** 5–8 exercises, 18–30 sets (PAS-D11), opening near the lower bound. The 3-day structure means each session carries a meaningful share of the week's lower-body volume while staying inside the per-session range.
- **Per week:** the Section 4 distribution; lower-body in-band at a beginner dose, upper-body below band.
- **Session duration:** within HYPERTROPHY's 50–80 min guideline (PAS §10.2).
- **Guardrail check:** inside PAS-D11 throughout; §11.2 honored (lower in-band, upper intentionally below as maintenance). No deviation justification required.

---

## Section 7 — Recovery Blueprint

- **3-day frequency IS the primary recovery mechanism.** A beginner's lower body cannot recover from four lower-heavy sessions weekly; three sessions with a full rest day between most of them is the deliberate dose (Family Research Deliverable 7).
- **Rest-day placement:** ≥1 full rest day; the squat-pattern (D1) and hinge-pattern (D2) days are sequenced so the two highest-axial-load sessions are not consecutive.
- **Intra-session rest:** BEGINNER guidance (PAS §10.3) — 60–90s typical on compounds, accessory rest omitted; reference-only.
- **Deload:** Week 7 maintains frequency, cuts primary lower volume 40–50% (PAS-D8).

## Section 8 — Successor / Predecessor Logic

- **Predecessor — none** (BEGINNER entry into the Lower Body ladder).
- **Successor — Lower Body Intermediate (Sort 9).** Hands forward movement competency on the lower-body pool, lower-body volume tolerance at a beginner dose, one completed deload, and readiness for the Intermediate jump (higher lower-body volume, RPE introduced, 3 → 4 days/week). The two BEGINNER doors of the family do not converge (Family Research Deliverable 7): this program feeds the Lower Body ladder, Muscle Building Foundation feeds the general ladder.

## Section 9 — Foundation-Pair Pressure Test

### The governing question

*If Lower Body Foundation did not exist, would Muscle Building Foundation fully satisfy the intended athlete?* The intended athlete is the **beginner whose primary goal is lower-body / glute development, training 3 days a week.** If the answer is "yes," Lower Body Foundation is redundant and the pair needs remediation.

### Honest two-sided analysis

**The case that the answer is "yes" (→ remediation).** At true-beginner level, specialization is arguably premature: novices grow on relatively low volume (Family Research Deliverable 4), so even the ~2 lower sessions/week inside Muscle Building Foundation's balanced split would drive solid lower-body development, *and* build a balanced base. One could argue the lower-body-focused beginner should run the general program first and specialize later at Lower Body Intermediate (which already exists). On the body-region axis alone, the distinction is genuinely weaker at BEGINNER than at INTERMEDIATE. This is the real tension, and it is acknowledged, not waved away.

**The case that the answer is "no" (→ Lower Body Foundation justified).** Two structural facts that the Intermediate-pair test could not rely on:

1. **Frequency / availability.** Muscle Building Foundation is a **4-day** program. The intended athlete trains **3 days/week**. Muscle Building Foundation structurally cannot serve a 3-day athlete *at all* — there is no "do 3 of the 4 days" path that preserves its balanced Upper/Lower logic. Lower Body Foundation is the family's **only** 3-day program. This is an access gap independent of body region, and it alone means Muscle Building Foundation does not *fully* satisfy the intended athlete.
2. **Volume concentration.** Even setting frequency aside, Muscle Building Foundation allots the lower body ~50% of its volume (Section 4 of that Blueprint). Lower Body Foundation allots ~85%. For an athlete whose stated goal *is* the lower body, ~50% is a materially weaker fit — partial satisfaction, not full.

### Verdict on the test

**Answer: NO — Muscle Building Foundation would not *fully* satisfy the intended athlete.** It fails on frequency (cannot serve a 3-day athlete) and only partially satisfies on volume concentration (~50% vs the lower-focused athlete's goal). Therefore **the Foundation pair does not trigger remediation.**

**Important nuance for governance:** the Foundation pair's distinction is carried **more by frequency/total (4d/32 vs 3d/24) than by body-region focus** — the inverse weighting of the Intermediate pair, where body-region was the entire differentiator. This is actually a *stronger* distinction posture than the Intermediate pair had (two independent separators instead of one), even though the body-region argument by itself is weaker at beginner level. The pair is safe, but the reason it is safe is the frequency axis, and that should be recorded so a future catalog edit does not "harmonize" Lower Body Foundation to 4 days without re-running this test.

---

## Section 10 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE.**

Lower Body Foundation is justified: it serves a beginner — lower-body goal, 3-day week — that Muscle Building Foundation structurally cannot fully serve, on both a frequency axis and a volume-concentration axis. The pressure test's answer is **"no,"** so **no remediation (revise / reposition / merge / retire) is triggered.**

**Foundation-pair convergence: RESOLVED**, on a different and in some ways firmer basis than the Intermediate pair — frequency + total + body-region, versus the Intermediate pair's body-region-only. Recorded caveat: the body-region justification alone is weaker at BEGINNER level, so the 3-day frequency is load-bearing in this verdict and must be preserved.

**Scope guard — the Muscle Building family is NOT yet declared complete.** Both Foundations and both Intermediates are now validated (4 of 5 programs). **One Stage 1 Blueprint remains: Muscle Building Advanced (Sort 7).** Architecture is locked; Content Production (Stage 1) is nearly complete; no Stage 2 work has begun for any program in the family.

**Confidence:** High on the verdict; the beginner-specialization tension is real but does not change the outcome given the decisive frequency separator.

**Recommended next action:** author **Muscle Building Advanced** (Sort 7) — the family's last outstanding Stage 1 Blueprint and its only Block Periodization / 5-day program — to complete the Muscle Building family's Stage 1 sequence.
