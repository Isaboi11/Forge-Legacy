## The short version

Forge Legacy keeps a record of your training. That record is yours.

- We do not sell your data. We do not share it with advertisers. There is no ad network in this app.
- There are **no third-party analytics or tracking tools** of any kind. Nothing follows you.
- Your workouts, photos and reflections are **private by default**. Things become visible to other people
  only when you put them somewhere shared — a squad, a friends feed, a competition.
- Your location is used **only while you are recording a run, walk or ride**, and your route never leaves
  your phone.
- You can delete your account, and everything in it, from inside the app.

The rest of this document is the detail behind those statements.

## 2. What we collect

We collect only what the app needs to work. There is no data collected for advertising, profiling, or
resale, because none of those things exist in this product.

### Account
- Email address and a password. Passwords are handled by our authentication provider and stored hashed —
  we never see or store your password.

### Profile
- Your name or handle, and optionally a profile photo.
- Training details you provide during setup: your goals, experience level, available equipment, training
  schedule, and sex where you choose to give it (used to select appropriate exercise demonstrations and
  strength standards).

### Training records
- Workouts you log: exercises, sets, weights, repetitions, duration, and rest.
- Programs you follow or build, and templates you save.
- Personal records, goals, honors earned, rank, and the chapters of your training history.
- Cardio sessions: distance, pace, duration, and activity type.
- Body metrics you choose to log, such as body weight and measurements.

### Photos and video
- Progress photos, workout or accomplishment photos, squad posts, form-check videos, check-in videos, and
  profile pictures — all only when you add them.
- Photos are resized on your device before upload. We do not read or retain the original full-resolution
  file, and we do not extract or store camera metadata such as the location a photo was taken.

### Social activity
- Squads you belong to, friend connections, posts, comments, reactions, competition entries and results,
  and notifications generated by that activity.

### Technical
- Basic information needed to deliver the service and diagnose failures, such as app version and the type
  of device. We do not build advertising profiles and we do not use device identifiers for tracking.

## 3. What we do not collect

Stated plainly, because these are the things people reasonably assume an app is doing:

- **No third-party analytics.** No Google Analytics, no Firebase, no Amplitude, Mixpanel, Segment, or
  similar. None are present in the application.
- **No advertising SDKs**, no ad identifiers, no cross-app or cross-site tracking.
- **No sale of personal information**, under any definition, to anyone.
- **No contacts access.** We never read your address book.
- **No background location.** See below.
- **No health-app integration.** We do not read from Apple Health.

---

## 4. Location, specifically

Location deserves its own section because it is the permission people worry about most.

- Location is requested **only when you start recording a run, walk or ride**, and only as
  “while using the app”. We never request or use background location.
- Coordinates are read on your device to calculate **distance and pace**. Those coordinates are
  **not transmitted to our servers and not stored in our database.** Only the resulting distance, pace and
  duration are saved with your workout.
- Your route is never shared with other users, because it is never collected in the first place.
- You can decline location entirely and still log every cardio session by entering the distance yourself.

## 5. Who can see your information

- **Private by default.** Your workouts, progress photos, body metrics, goals and reflections are visible
  only to you unless you deliberately share them.
- **Squads.** Content you post to a squad is visible to members of that squad.
- **Friends.** Content you share to your friends feed is visible to people you have mutually accepted as
  friends. Friendship requires acceptance by both people.
- **Competitions.** If you enter a competition, your results within it are visible to the other
  participants.
- **Your profile.** Your name, handle and profile photo may be visible to people who can find you in the
  app. You can adjust what is shown in the app’s privacy settings.

Progress photos and body metrics are never posted anywhere automatically. Nothing you log is shared
without an action you take.

## 6. How your information is stored

- Your data is stored using **Supabase**, our hosting and database provider, acting as a processor on our
  behalf. Their handling of data is governed by their own terms and privacy commitments.
- Data is encrypted in transit.
- Access is restricted at the database level so that your records are readable only by your account and by
  the specific people you have shared them with.
- Photos and video are held in access-controlled storage, not on a public URL that anyone can guess.

## 8. Keeping and deleting your information

- We keep your data for as long as your account exists, because the point of the product is a record that
  lasts.
- **You can delete your account from inside the app**, in Account Settings. Deleting your account removes
  your personal data, your training records, and your uploaded photos and video.
- Deletion is permanent. We cannot restore a deleted account.
- Content you posted into a shared space may remain visible to that group in limited form (for example,
  that a member left) — but your personal records, photos and profile are removed.

## Must be checked before you rely on it

1. **Verify the deletion claim end to end.** § 8 promises that deleting your account removes your uploaded
   photos and video. Database rows and *storage objects* are deleted by different mechanisms, and a
   cascade that clears the rows does not necessarily clear the files. Test it on a real account with real
   photos before this sentence is published, and fix the code if it is not true — a privacy policy is a
   promise, and this is the one most likely to be quietly false.
2. **Decide on EU/UK.** If you ever distribute there, this needs GDPR-specific sections: legal basis for
   processing, data-transfer mechanism, and a controller contact. Related to the DSA trader question you
   already answered.
3. **Decide on California.** CCPA/CPRA adds required disclosures and a “Do Not Sell or Share” statement —
   simpler than usual here, since the honest answer is that you do neither.
4. **Match it to Apple’s privacy questionnaire.** App Store Connect asks you to declare data collection
   category by category. Those answers and this document must agree; a mismatch is a review rejection and,
   worse, a public inconsistency.

## Where it needs to live

Apple requires a publicly reachable URL. The cleanest option is your own domain —
`forgelegacy.expo.app/privacy` — which you already control and which costs nothing to add.
