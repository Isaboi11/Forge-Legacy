## Section 1 — Quad Stretch

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Quad Stretch |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | STRETCH_STATIC |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | QUADS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |

**ABOUT**
> The Quad Stretch is a static stretch performed standing, pulling one heel toward the glutes to lengthen the front of the thigh. It stretches the quads while balancing on the opposite leg. It is held for a sustained period rather than moved through repetitions.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Quad Stretch builds front-of-thigh flexibility that supports squat and lunge depth. It is well suited as part of a cool-down after lower-body training or a general flexibility routine. It also helps offset quad tightness that builds from running or cycling. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand on one leg, holding onto something stable if needed.
2. Bend your other knee and reach back to grasp your ankle.
3. Pull your heel gently toward your glutes.
4. Keep your knees close together throughout the stretch.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.
6. Lower your leg and switch sides.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your standing knee soft rather than locked
> — Pull your heel toward your glutes rather than your knee outward
> — Breathe steadily throughout the hold
> — Keep your hips level rather than tilting to one side

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back arches to compensate for tight hips
> — The knees drift apart instead of staying close together
> — The standing leg locks out fully, reducing balance

**Progressions:** Couch Stretch
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch

## Section 2 — Couch Stretch

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Couch Stretch |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | STRETCH_STATIC |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | QUADS |

**ABOUT**
> The Couch Stretch is a static stretch performed kneeling with the back foot propped against a wall or surface behind the body. It stretches the quads and hip flexors more deeply than a standing quad stretch. It is held for a sustained period on each side.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Couch Stretch builds deep hip-flexor and quad flexibility beyond what a standing stretch reaches. It is well suited for lifters with tight hips from prolonged sitting or heavy squatting. It also supports a more upright torso position in squats by lengthening the structures that limit hip extension. It is one of the more demanding static stretches in the catalog.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Kneel in front of a wall or couch with one foot propped up behind you.
2. Position your back knee close to the wall, shin resting against it.
3. Step your front foot forward and bend your front knee.
4. Keep your torso upright as you sink into the stretch.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.
6. Switch sides and repeat.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your torso upright rather than leaning forward
> — Squeeze your glute on the stretching side to deepen the hip-flexor stretch
> — Breathe steadily rather than holding your breath
> — Ease into the stretch gradually rather than forcing the position

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back arches to compensate for tight hips
> — The torso leans forward instead of staying upright
> — The position is forced deeper than is comfortable

**Progressions:** None — no genuinely harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Quad Stretch
**Alternatives:** Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch

## Section 3 — Seated Forward Fold

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Seated Forward Fold |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | STRETCH_STATIC |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | HAMSTRINGS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | LOWER_BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Seated Forward Fold is a static stretch performed sitting with legs extended, folding forward at the hips to reach toward the feet. It stretches the hamstrings and lower back. It is held for a sustained period rather than moved through repetitions.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Seated Forward Fold builds hamstring and lower-back flexibility using a stable seated position. It is well suited as a gentler entry point before progressing to the standing version of the stretch. It also helps offset tightness that builds from long periods of sitting. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit on the floor with your legs extended in front of you.
2. Keep your back as flat as possible.
3. Hinge forward from your hips, reaching toward your feet.
4. Reach only as far as feels like a stretch, not pain.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Hinge from your hips rather than rounding your lower back
> — Breathe steadily throughout the hold
> — Keep a slight bend in your knees if your hamstrings are very tight
> — Ease into the stretch gradually rather than bouncing

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back rounds aggressively instead of hinging from the hips
> — The stretch is bounced into instead of held steadily
> — The knees lock out fully, adding unnecessary strain

**Progressions:** Standing Hamstring Stretch
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Butterfly Stretch

## Section 5 — Figure-Four Stretch

| Movement Pattern | STRETCH_STATIC |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | GLUTES |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |

**ABOUT**
> The Figure-Four Stretch is a static stretch performed lying on the back, crossing one ankle over the opposite knee and pulling the legs toward the chest. It stretches the glutes and hip external rotators. It is held for a sustained period on each side.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Figure-Four Stretch builds glute and hip flexibility from a supported, lying position. It is well suited as a gentler regression for athletes who find Pigeon Pose too demanding on the knees or balance. It also helps offset hip tightness that builds from sitting or heavy lower-body training. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your back with both knees bent.
2. Cross one ankle over the opposite knee.
3. Reach through and grasp the back of the uncrossed leg's thigh.
4. Pull both legs gently toward your chest.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.
6. Switch sides and repeat.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your lower back pressed into the floor throughout
> — Pull your legs in gently rather than yanking them
> — Breathe steadily rather than holding your breath
> — Ease into the stretch gradually rather than forcing the position

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back arches off the floor during the pull
> — The position is forced deeper than is comfortable
> — The hips twist to one side instead of staying level

**Progressions:** Pigeon Pose
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Butterfly Stretch

**ABOUT**
> The Doorway Chest Stretch is a static stretch performed standing in a doorway, pressing the forearm against the frame and rotating away. It stretches the chest and front of the shoulder. It is held for a sustained period on each side.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Doorway Chest Stretch builds chest and shoulder flexibility that offsets the tightness built up from heavy pressing work and prolonged sitting. It is well suited as a cool-down after upper-body pressing sessions. It also helps maintain healthy shoulder positioning for overhead movements. It requires only a doorway or vertical surface.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand in a doorway and place one forearm against the frame at shoulder height.
2. Step forward with the opposite foot.
3. Rotate your torso away from the raised arm.
4. Keep your forearm pressed against the frame throughout.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.
6. Switch sides and repeat.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Rotate from your torso rather than just stepping forward
> — Keep your raised elbow roughly level with your shoulder
> — Breathe steadily throughout the hold
> — Ease into the stretch gradually rather than forcing the rotation

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The shoulder shrugs up toward the ear during the stretch
> — The position is forced deeper than is comfortable
> — The lower back arches to add range instead of the shoulder opening

## Section 7 — Child's Pose

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Child's Pose |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | STRETCH_STATIC |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | LOWER_BACK |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | BACK |

**ABOUT**
> Child's Pose is a static stretch performed kneeling, sitting the hips back toward the heels while reaching the arms forward on the floor. It stretches the lower back, hips, and shoulders. It is one of the gentlest full-body stretches in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> Child's Pose builds gentle flexibility across the back, hips, and shoulders in a single relaxed position. It is well suited as a calming cool-down stretch or a brief reset between sets of demanding training. It also offers a low-intensity option for athletes easing back into mobility work. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Kneel on the floor with your big toes touching and knees spread wide.
2. Sit your hips back toward your heels.
3. Reach your arms forward on the floor in front of you.
4. Rest your forehead on the floor or a cushion.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Sit your hips back toward your heels rather than staying lifted
> — Let your arms relax forward rather than reaching forcefully
> — Breathe steadily and let your back relax with each exhale
> — Keep your knees as wide as is comfortable

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips stay lifted instead of sinking back toward the heels
> — The breath is held instead of staying steady through the hold
> — The shoulders tense instead of relaxing toward the floor

**Progressions:** None — no genuinely harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Pigeon Pose, Lat Stretch

## Section 11 — Diaphragmatic Breathing

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Diaphragmatic Breathing |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | BREATHWORK |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> Diaphragmatic Breathing is a breathwork exercise performed by breathing deeply into the belly rather than the chest. It trains full, relaxed breathing using the diaphragm. It is performed lying down or seated in a quiet position.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> Diaphragmatic Breathing builds awareness and control of full, relaxed breathing, which supports recovery and core bracing during heavier lifts. It is well suited as a foundational breathing practice before progressing to more structured patterns like Box Breathing. It also supports recovery by shifting the body out of a high-stress breathing pattern. It requires no equipment and can be practiced almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie down or sit in a comfortable, quiet position.
2. Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly.
3. Inhale slowly through your nose, letting your belly rise.
4. Keep your chest relatively still as your belly expands.
5. Exhale slowly through your mouth, letting your belly fall.
6. Continue breathing this way for the duration of the practice.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Let your belly rise and fall rather than your chest
> — Breathe slowly through your nose on the inhale
> — Relax your shoulders throughout the practice
> — Keep each breath smooth rather than forced

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The chest rises more than the belly during the inhale
> — The breath becomes shallow instead of staying slow and full
> — The shoulders tense up rather than staying relaxed

**Progressions:** None — breathwork has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.
**Regressions:** None — breathwork has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.

## Section 12 — 4-7-8 Breathing

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | 4-7-8 Breathing |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | BREATHWORK |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> 4-7-8 Breathing is a breathwork exercise performed by inhaling for four counts, holding for seven, and exhaling for eight. It trains a longer, controlled exhale relative to the inhale. It is performed seated or lying down in a quiet position.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> 4-7-8 Breathing builds a strong calming response through its extended exhale, which is associated with reducing stress and easing the body toward sleep. It is well suited for use before bed or after a stressful event. It also supports recovery by shifting the body out of a high-stress state more strongly than an even-count pattern. It requires no equipment and can be practiced almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit or lie in a comfortable, quiet position.
2. Exhale fully to begin with empty lungs.
3. Inhale slowly through your nose for a count of four.
4. Hold your breath for a count of seven.
5. Exhale slowly through your mouth for a count of eight.
6. Repeat the cycle for the duration of the practice.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the exhale longer than the inhale throughout
> — Breathe through your nose on the inhale for more control
> — Relax your shoulders throughout the practice
> — Use a shorter count if the full pattern feels difficult to sustain

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The breath becomes shallow instead of staying slow and full
> — The hold feels strained instead of comfortable
> — The shoulders and chest tense up rather than staying relaxed

**Progressions:** None — breathwork has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.
**Regressions:** None — breathwork has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.
**Alternatives:** Box Breathing, Diaphragmatic Breathing

## Section 13 — Resonant Breathing

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Resonant Breathing |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | BREATHWORK |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> Resonant Breathing is a breathwork exercise performed by breathing at a steady rate of roughly five to six breaths per minute, with equal inhale and exhale lengths. It trains a slow, even breathing rhythm. It is performed seated or lying down in a quiet position.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> Resonant Breathing builds a steady, even breathing rhythm associated with balancing the nervous system over a sustained practice. It is well suited for longer relaxation sessions or as a daily calming practice. It also supports recovery by encouraging a consistent, unhurried breathing pattern rather than short counted holds. It requires no equipment and can be practiced almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit or lie in a comfortable, quiet position.
2. Inhale slowly through your nose for roughly five to six seconds.
3. Exhale slowly through your nose or mouth for the same length.
4. Keep the inhale and exhale equal in length throughout.
5. Continue the steady rhythm for the duration of the practice.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the inhale and exhale equal in length
> — Breathe slowly and smoothly rather than counting rigidly
> — Relax your shoulders throughout the practice
> — Let the breath settle into a steady, repeatable rhythm

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The inhale and exhale become uneven in length
> — The breath becomes shallow instead of staying slow and full
> — The rhythm rushes instead of staying steady

**Progressions:** None — breathwork has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.
**Regressions:** None — breathwork has no genuine difficulty ladder within the launch catalog.

## Section 14 — Breath-Led Cooldown

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Breath-Led Cooldown |
| Category | MOBILITY |
| Movement Pattern | BREATHWORK |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Breath-Led Cooldown is a breathwork exercise performed lying down at the end of a session, breathing slowly while allowing the body to settle. It trains a gradual transition from elevated training heart rate to a relaxed state. It is performed without any counted pattern.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Breath-Led Cooldown builds a deliberate transition out of training intensity rather than ending a session abruptly. It is well suited as the final few minutes of any workout, regardless of training style. It also supports recovery by giving the body dedicated time to downshift before returning to daily activity. It requires no equipment and can be practiced almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie down in a comfortable position at the end of your session.
2. Close your eyes or soften your gaze.
3. Breathe slowly and let each exhale relax your body further.
4. Allow your breathing rate to settle naturally rather than counting it.
5. Continue for several minutes until your heart rate feels settled.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Let your breathing rate settle naturally rather than forcing a count
> — Relax each part of your body progressively with each exhale
> — Keep your attention on the sensation of breathing
> — Stay in the position until your heart rate feels calm

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The transition is skipped, ending the session abruptly
> — The breath stays shallow and quick instead of settling
> — The mind stays occupied with the workout instead of settling into the cooldown
