## Q1 — Did all 5 programs survive governance?

**Yes. All five received a Stage 1 APPROVE.** No program was revised or retired.

This is a different outcome from the Strength family, where the equivalent sequence retired Powerbuilding Intermediate. That difference is meaningful and was earned, not assumed: the two genuine convergence threats in this family were each subjected to an explicit, pre-registered test (Q3), and the family's uniform goal alignment (`BUILD_MUSCLE` across all five) was treated as an *inert* differentiator throughout, forcing every justification onto athlete / progression / volume / frequency / body-region instead. The family survived intact because those axes carried real weight, not because the bar was lowered.

**Honest note:** "all five survived" should not be read as "all five are equally strong." The justification strength varies (Q4).

## Q2 — Are the successor chains coherent?

**Yes.** The family is two parallel ladders with no cross-links (`Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.3):

```
Muscle Building Foundation → Muscle Building Intermediate → Muscle Building Advanced   (terminal)
Lower Body Foundation       → Lower Body Intermediate       (terminal)
```

Coherence checks, all pass:
- **One successor per program** (PEA §3.1) — respected; no program points to two.
- **Directional, no back-links** — respected.
- **Two BEGINNER entry doors that do NOT converge** (Family Research Deliverable 7) — each feeds its own ladder. This is deliberate and the opposite of the Strength family's converging doors; it is correct because the body-region preference is durable, not a starting condition.
- **Terminals correct** — Muscle Building Advanced and Lower Body Intermediate both terminal. Lower Body Intermediate's roadmapped continuation (Lower Body Advanced, PEA §5) is not in the catalog, so its link is correctly null (no mis-pointing at a whole-physique program), and W-3 shows no "What's Next" (PEA §3.2).
- **No orphans** — every non-terminal program has a published-in-catalog successor.

## Q3 — Are any pairs convergent?

**No unresolved convergence.** Two pairs carried genuine risk; both were tested and resolved. The third possible peer (Advanced) has no same-level partner.

| Pair | Risk | Test | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| MB Intermediate ↔ Lower Body Intermediate | **Highest** — identical on *every* locked metadata axis | 3-part falsifiable test (≥65–70% lower-body sets; upper at maintenance; non-derivable from deleting MB Int's upper days) | **PASS all 3.** Distinguished by ~70/30 body-region distribution + emphasis-rotation structure. Resolved. |
| MB Foundation ↔ Lower Body Foundation | Medium | Inverted test ("would MB Foundation fully satisfy the lower-body-goal beginner?") | **Answer: NO.** Distinguished by frequency (4 vs 3 days) + total (32 vs 24) + body-region. Resolved. |
| MB Advanced ↔ (none) | None | n/a — only ADVANCED / 5-day / Block-Periodization program | No peer; no test needed. |

**Cross-pair observation:** the two ladders are distinguished on a *sliding* basis — body-region is the entire differentiator at INTERMEDIATE, but at FOUNDATION the frequency/total separation carries more weight than body-region (which is weaker at beginner level). This is coherent, but it means the distinction mechanism is not uniform up the ladders. See Q4.

## Q4 — Are any programs weak enough to retire before Stage 2?

**No. No program is weak enough to retire.** But the justification strengths are not uniform, and one program is the clear "watch" item:

- **Strongest:** Muscle Building Advanced (unique model/frequency/duration, no peer) and Muscle Building Intermediate (the whole-physique baseline, self-justifying regardless of the Lower Body ladder).
- **Strong:** Muscle Building Foundation (clean entry; only weak link would be its successor distinction, which is decisive) and Lower Body Intermediate (passed the hardest test in authored structure).
- **Weakest-justified — the watch item: Lower Body Foundation.** Its body-region justification is genuinely weaker at BEGINNER level (specialization is arguably premature for novices), so its survival rests heavily on a *single* load-bearing axis: it is the family's only **3-day** program, serving an athlete the 4-day Muscle Building Foundation structurally cannot. That justification is real and sufficient — but it is narrow. **Governance flag:** if a future catalog edit ever "harmonizes" Lower Body Foundation to 4 days/week, its distinction would collapse toward the weak body-region-only case and it would have to be re-tested (and would likely fail). This fragility is recorded, not actioned — it does not warrant retirement now, because the 3-day separator is currently real and the program serves a genuine athlete.

The family therefore loses **zero** programs at Stage 1 — a stronger result than the Strength family — with one documented fragility to monitor rather than remediate.

## Q5 — Is the family ready to move from Stage 1 Blueprinting to Stage 2 Authoring?

**Governance: YES — Stage 1 is complete for the Muscle Building family.** All five Blueprints are APPROVE, both convergence risks are resolved, the succession chains are coherent, and no remediation is outstanding. From a governance standpoint there is nothing blocking Stage 2.

**Three cross-dependencies must be acknowledged before authoring actually begins** (these are not Stage 1 governance gaps — they are Stage 2 preconditions, and most are cross-family, not family-specific):

1. **Exercise Library availability (the one practical gate).** Stage 2 (Google Sheets authoring, PAS §12) references real `ExerciseDefinition` records. The library's 45 anchors are authored but pending review/media, and the broader ~200-exercise catalog is not yet populated (`Exercise-Library-Production-Plan.md`). These Blueprints deliberately named *patterns* (squat-pattern, hinge-pattern, press compound), not specific exercises — so authoring cannot complete until the exercises they'll reference exist. This is a genuine sequencing dependency between the two content workstreams.
2. **Cross-family production order (Production Standard §7 recommendation).** The standard recommends authoring the entry-tier program of *every* family before any family's second-tier — "produce by rung, not family-by-family" — to surface cross-category calibration issues early. Proceeding to author all five Muscle Building programs in isolation would run against that recommendation; it is a sequencing consideration for the product owner, not a defect in this family.
3. **PAS-R1 Difficulty Calibration Audit (PAS §18.4) is a pre-*publish* gate, not a Stage-1→Stage-2 gate.** It runs once, after all 25 programs exist as drafts, before any publish. It does not block this family entering Stage 2, but it is the checkpoint where this family's difficulty curve will be compared against the rest of the catalog.

**Not a blocker:** the broader catalog naming/positioning conflict (Production Standard §1) is **resolved for this family** by `Muscle-Building-Rename-Amendment-001.md`; it remains open only for other families and does not gate Muscle Building's Stage 2.
