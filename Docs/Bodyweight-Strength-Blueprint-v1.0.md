## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Bodyweight Strength is the **intermediate bodyweight-strength** program: it builds genuine strength using **bodyweight only**, by progressing the athlete along **leverage/mechanical-difficulty ladders** toward harder strength movements. Its goals are `BUILD_STRENGTH` ("1RM, compound lift focus" — here expressed as maximal-effort bodyweight strength) and `GENERAL_FITNESS`. It takes the athlete who has competent fundamental movement (from Bodyweight Foundation) and develops strength on that base — the strength rung between general-fitness movement competency (Foundation) and advanced calisthenics performance (Performance).

### Target Athlete

- **General athlete** (PEA §5.4), **intermediate** (PAS §3.2), who has graduated **Bodyweight Foundation** or has equivalent bodyweight movement competency.
- Trains bodyweight-only and wants to get genuinely **strong** without equipment — not equipment-based strength (Home Strength Foundation), not advanced skill/performance work yet (Bodyweight Performance).
- Can train 4 days/week.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| New to bodyweight training | Assumes movement competency and intermediate strength tolerance | **Bodyweight Foundation** (Sort 18) |
| Has dumbbells/bands and wants equipment strength | This program uses no external load — leverage/variation progression | **Home Strength Foundation** (Sort 22) |
| Ready for advanced calisthenics skill/performance | Intermediate strength, not advanced skill | Bodyweight Performance (Sort 20) |
| Has gym access and wants barbell strength | Bodyweight-only | Strength family |

### Expected Outcomes

Measurable bodyweight-strength gains over the 8-week block — competence on harder strength variations than the athlete could perform at the start (e.g., progress along push/pull/squat difficulty ladders) — leaving the athlete ready for Bodyweight Performance's advanced skill/performance work.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Strength progression is visible (QC-2) via **advancement along leverage/variation difficulty ladders** (and rep gains within a variation), not added external load (none available) and not merely more reps of Foundation-level basics.
- Rep ranges skew **lower/strength-focused** on the primary strength movements vs Bodyweight Foundation's higher-rep general-fitness work.
- Whole-body coverage each session; bodyweight-only (verifiable against §11.7's "not available" list).

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 8 weeks (PAS-D7 → one deload).
- **Frequency:** 4 sessions/week (up from Foundation's 3 — more volume to drive strength).
- **Weekly structure:** 4 full-body bodyweight-strength sessions, each working a push, pull, and lower-body strength pattern at the athlete's current variation difficulty, plus accessory/core. WARM_UP required; COOL_DOWN optional (PAS-D9).
- **Deload:** one, Week 7 (slots 25–28), peak Week 8 (slots 29–32), per PAS §14 — reduces volume/difficulty, maintains four sessions (PAS-D8). (Foundation, at 6 weeks, had no deload; the longer, more intense Bodyweight Strength warrants one.)
- **Periodization summary:** a single block on **linear rep-based progression** (PAS §7.1 Model 1 variant), applied to strength-movement difficulty ladders (Section 4).

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9). FULL_BODY requires **WARM_UP**; COOL_DOWN optional (PAS-D9). Equipment ceiling per §11.7 (bodyweight, pull-up bar, bands; no barbell/rack/cables/machines). Each session: WARM_UP (dynamic raise + strength-movement prep) → MAIN: a push-pattern strength movement, a pull-pattern strength movement, a lower-body strength movement (each at the athlete's current difficulty variation), plus accessory/core → (COOL_DOWN optional). MAIN sits within PAS-D11 (4–7 exercises / 12–20 sets), 40–70 min (§10.2), with rep ranges skewed lower on the primary strength movements (strength-focused, not the higher-rep general-fitness ranges of Foundation).

## Section 4 — How Strength Progresses Without External Load (the crux)

Bodyweight Strength cannot add external load (no barbell/dumbbell as the primary driver — §11.7). It builds strength by manipulating **leverage and mechanical disadvantage** — progressing the athlete to **harder variations** of each movement pattern, where each harder variation demands more strength from the target muscles at equal or fewer reps. Leverage replaces load:

- **Push:** incline push-up → flat → decline → archer push-up → (toward) one-arm push-up; pike push-up → toward handstand push-up progressions.
- **Pull:** assisted/band row → inverted row → full pull-up → archer / weighted-pack / toward one-arm progressions.
- **Lower body:** squat → split squat → Bulgarian split squat → toward pistol-squat progressions.

The **rep-based linear progression** (PAS §7.1 Model 1 variant) operates **in service of advancing the variation ladder**: build reps to a target at the current variation, then progress to the harder variation (the strength-overload event), where reps reset lower. This is genuine strength development — the calisthenics analogue of adding weight — and it is a fundamentally different mechanic from both Bodyweight Foundation (which progresses reps/variations for *general movement competency*, not maximal strength) and Home Strength Foundation (which progresses *external dumbbell/band load*).

---

## Section 5 — Progression from Bodyweight Foundation

Bodyweight Foundation builds **movement competency** on fundamental patterns at higher reps (general fitness). Bodyweight Strength **takes that competency and develops strength on it**: it shifts the target to maximal-effort bodyweight strength via harder variations (lower reps), adds the BUILD_STRENGTH goal, raises frequency (3 → 4), extends duration (6 → 8 weeks), and introduces a deload. The athlete arrives able to perform the basic patterns competently; they leave able to perform meaningfully harder strength variations. This is a genuine level transition, not continuation — see Section 9.

---

## Section 6 — Recovery Strategy

- **8 weeks / 4 days with a Week 7 deload** — the higher frequency and strength-focused (harder-variation, lower-rep, higher-effort) work warrant the deload that the 6-week Foundation did not.
- Rest days between sessions; intermediate rest periods adequate for strength-focused efforts (PAS §10.3); harder strength variations are sequenced with recovery in mind.

---

## Section 7 — Expected Outcomes

By the end of the block: demonstrable bodyweight-strength gains (advancement along push/pull/lower-body difficulty ladders) and a strength base sufficient for the advanced skill/performance work of Bodyweight Performance.

## Section 8 — Predecessor / Successor Logic

- **Predecessor — Bodyweight Foundation (Sort 18):** inherits movement competency and the rep/variation progression habit; Bodyweight Strength develops strength on that base.
- **Successor — Bodyweight Performance (Sort 20):** hands forward a genuine bodyweight-strength base, the prerequisite for advanced calisthenics skill/performance work. The forward test that Bodyweight Performance must pass is defined in Section 10.

---

## Section 9 — Distinction Rationale

**Not "Bodyweight Foundation continued longer."** Foundation is BEGINNER, GENERAL_FITNESS only, movement-competency-forward, higher-rep basic patterns, 6 weeks / 3 days / no deload. Bodyweight Strength adds a **BUILD_STRENGTH goal**, shifts the training target to **strength via harder leverage variations** (lower-rep, maximal-effort), runs **8 weeks / 4 days with a deload**, and progresses along strength-movement difficulty ladders rather than accumulating reps of basics. It is a different training target on a higher base — a genuine intermediate rung, not more weeks of the same program.

**Not Home Strength Foundation.** The two share goal alignment (BUILD_STRENGTH + GENERAL_FITNESS) but differ on **equipment tier, progression mechanic, and level**: Home Strength Foundation builds strength via **external load** (dumbbells/bands) at BEGINNER; Bodyweight Strength via **leverage/variation** (no external load) at INTERMEDIATE. Loaded strength and leverage-based bodyweight strength are different disciplines (the equipment-tier distinction established from the home-equipment side in the Home Strength Foundation Blueprint and the bodyweight side in the Bodyweight Foundation Blueprint — confirmed here from the Bodyweight-Strength side).

## Section 10 — Forward Test for Bodyweight Performance

Bodyweight Performance (ADVANCED) shares Bodyweight Strength's goal alignment (`BUILD_STRENGTH, GENERAL_FITNESS`), so its Blueprint must prove it is a genuine ADVANCED rung — **not "Bodyweight Strength continued longer."** It must demonstrate all of:

| # | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Advanced-level content** | Advanced calisthenics skill/performance movements (e.g., explosive/plyometric calisthenics, lever/planche/front-lever progressions, muscle-ups, handstand work) that require the strength base Bodyweight Strength builds and that an intermediate could not perform. |
| 2 | **A "Performance" dimension beyond strength** | Skill, explosiveness, and/or peaking — distinguishing "Performance" from "Strength." The name implies a qualitative shift, not just harder strength reps. |
| 3 | **Genuine level jump (ADV vs INT)** | Assumes the Bodyweight Strength base and introduces content/structure an intermediate could not handle. It must also **define its ADVANCED progression model** — §7.1's Model 1 program list names only Bodyweight Foundation and Bodyweight Strength as rep-based variants, so Bodyweight Performance must specify how it progresses at ADVANCED (skill progressions, possible block/phase structure for peaking). |
| 4 | **Non-derivability** | Not "Bodyweight Strength with more weeks / harder variations" — a qualitatively advanced training target a reviewer can distinguish from the strength rung. |

If Bodyweight Performance cannot demonstrate these, the ADVANCED rung should be re-examined under the family's governing principle (re-differentiate / merge / — though retirement of an ADVANCED capstone is unlikely — at minimum re-justify).

## Section 11 — Governance Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE — Bodyweight Strength is a legitimate intermediate rung.**

It is distinct from its predecessor (Bodyweight Foundation) by goal, training target, frequency, duration, and deload — a genuine level transition, not continuation. It is distinct from its same-goal sibling (Home Strength Foundation) by equipment tier, progression mechanic (leverage vs load), and level. And it has a real, repository-consistent bodyweight-strength progression mechanic — leverage/variation difficulty ladders driven by rep-based linear progression — that answers "how strength progresses without external load." There is **no convergence risk, no revision, and no retirement** warranted.
