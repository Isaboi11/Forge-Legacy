## Section 1 — Program Overview

### Program Identity / Training Intent

Conditioning Intermediate is a **work-capacity** program. Its single goal is `IMPROVE_CONDITIONING` — per `Program-Catalog-Architecture-v1.0.md` §3.4, "work capacity, anaerobic threshold, general fitness." Everything in the program serves that end: it raises how much quality work the athlete can perform per unit time and rest, and lifts the anaerobic threshold at which performance degrades. **Resistance work is present but in service of conditioning** — loaded circuits, carries, and moderate compound work performed at conditioning densities — **not** as hypertrophy training. The program makes no claim to muscle growth or body-composition change; those are other programs' goals.

This work-capacity-forward identity is the baseline that Body Recomposition Intermediate must later prove distinct from (Section 9).

### Target Athlete

- **General athlete** (`Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §5.4) who has graduated **Athletic Conditioning Foundation** or has equivalent intermediate conditioning experience.
- Goal is improved work capacity / general conditioning — **not** fat loss as a primary aim, **not** muscle growth, **not** maximal strength.
- Has gym access and is willing to train at prescribed work-to-rest ratios (PAS §11.3).
- Can sustain 4 conditioning sessions per week at intermediate density.

### Excluded Athlete Profiles

| Profile | Why excluded | Better served by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary goal is fat loss + muscle retention | This program does not prescribe hypertrophy-supportive resistance or deficit-oriented structure | **Body Recomposition Intermediate** (same level/metadata; the goal-distinct sibling under test) |
| Primary goal is concurrent strength + conditioning | No dedicated strength-development block; GYM not MIXED | Hybrid Intermediate (Sort 17) |
| New to structured conditioning | Intermediate density assumes a base | Athletic Conditioning Foundation (Sort 12) |
| Primary goal is maximal strength or pure hypertrophy | Volume-/work-capacity-driven, not load- or hypertrophy-driven | Strength / Muscle Building families |

### Expected Outcomes

Measurably greater work capacity over the 10-week block: more total quality work per unit time/rest, a higher sustainable conditioning density, and an improved anaerobic threshold — with **no** claim to hypertrophy or composition change.

### Success Criteria (for the eventual authored program)

- Conditioning (energy-system) work is the clear majority of the session's training emphasis; resistance is present but conditioning-serving.
- Work-to-rest density progresses across the block (QC-2 visible as compressed rest / added rounds / added reps), not merely heavier load.
- Every session has WARM_UP and COOL_DOWN (PAS-D9); `restSeconds` is populated on every prescription (PAS §11.3); circuits are named in slots.

## Section 2 — Program Architecture

- **Duration:** 10 weeks (PAS-D7 → one deload).
- **Frequency:** 4 sessions/week (a step up from Athletic Conditioning Foundation's 3).
- **Weekly structure:** four combined strength + cardio sessions (PAS §11.3) on a rotation that varies the energy-system stimulus, e.g.:
  1. **Interval / threshold** (anaerobic-threshold focus)
  2. **Loaded circuit / metcon** (work density with light-to-moderate resistance)
  3. **Steady-state + light resistance** (aerobic base + circuit accessory)
  4. **Mixed conditioning** (rotating modality to broaden work capacity)
  (Exact slot pattern fixed at Stage 2; the Blueprint commits to a 4-day, conditioning-forward, work:rest-governed structure.)
- **Deload:** one, Week 9 (slots 33–36), peak Week 10 (slots 37–40), per PAS §14 — reduces work density / volume while maintaining all four sessions (PAS-D8 generalized).
- **Periodization summary:** a single block in which **work-to-rest density progressively compresses** (PAS §11.3 — work-to-rest ratios are the primary prescription variable), with Double Progression governing the resistance elements within that arc.

---

## Section 3 — Session Structure

Section-first model (PAS §2.2, §9; WS-A5). **CONDITIONING requires both WARM_UP and COOL_DOWN** (PAS-D9). Exercise/element order and rest are governed by PAS §11.3: sessions combine strength and cardio; work-to-rest ratios are primary; `restSeconds` always populated; circuit intent named in the slot (e.g., "Week 3 — AMRAP 20 Min Circuit") and noted in `notes`; complex barbell lifts requiring unavailable coaching are avoided (conditioning athletes may lack a strength background).

Each session: WARM_UP (dynamic raise) → MAIN (the day's conditioning stimulus — interval block, circuit, or metcon — with conditioning-serving resistance interleaved at the prescribed work:rest) → COOL_DOWN. MAIN sits within PAS-D11 (4–8 exercises / 12–24 sets) and 30–60 min (PAS §10.2). The resistance present is **light-to-moderate load at conditioning densities**, never hypertrophy-style sets to growth-driving fatigue.

---

## Section 4 — Volume Distribution & Conditioning/Resistance Emphasis

The defining volume variable is **work-to-rest density**, not set count (PAS §11.3). Indicative emphasis:

| Element | Emphasis | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Conditioning / energy-system work (intervals, circuits, metcons, steady-state) | **Majority** of session emphasis | The program — drives work capacity and anaerobic threshold |
| Resistance (loaded carries, circuit resistance, moderate compounds) | **Minority**, conditioning-serving | Adds load/variety to conditioning; NOT hypertrophy volume |

Conditioning-forward = compressed rest, higher density, energy-system emphasis. The resistance share stays inside PAS-D11 but is deliberately sub-hypertrophic: moderate loads, conditioning rep/round structures, short rest. **This conditioning-forward majority is the baseline Body Recomposition Intermediate must invert** (Section 9).

---

## Section 5 — Progression Strategy

- **Primary progressed variable: work-to-rest density** (PAS §11.3) — across the 10-week block the program compresses rest, adds rounds, or adds reps within fixed work:rest, raising sustainable work capacity.
- **Double Progression (PAS §7.2, Model 2)** governs the resistance elements: a rep range is prescribed; load advances when the top of the range is hit on all sets. In a conditioning context "failure" is failure to complete the prescribed work at the prescribed density; the family two-stage failure response applies (repeat, then reduce).
- **RPE** permitted (PAS-D3, INTERMEDIATE) in `notes` to manage effort as density rises.

The progression *headline* is density, not load — which is itself a distinction marker from a hypertrophy/recomp program (where load and resistance volume lead).

---

## Section 6 — Recovery Strategy

- **Work-to-rest is the recovery lever.** `restSeconds` is always populated (PAS §11.3); recovery within and across sessions is managed by the prescribed work:rest ratio.
- **Deload Week 9** reduces work density / volume while maintaining 4-day frequency (PAS-D8 generalized to conditioning).
- **Rest-day placement:** ≥1 full rest day across four sessions; the highest-density days (intervals, metcon) are sequenced so they are not back-to-back.
- Intermediate RPE self-regulation (PAS-D3) supplements the fixed work:rest structure.

---

## Section 7 — Expected Outcome

By the end of the block: a measurable increase in work capacity (more quality work per unit time/rest), a higher anaerobic threshold, and broader conditioning across modalities — achieved through progressive density, not through hypertrophy or fat-loss-specific programming.

## Section 8 — Distinction Rationale & Baseline Statement

**From its own side, Conditioning Intermediate is self-justifying.** It is the INTERMEDIATE continuation of the Athletic Conditioning ladder for the General athlete, distinct from its predecessor (Athletic Conditioning Foundation) by level, frequency (3→4 days), and work-capacity density. Its existence does not depend on Body Recomposition Intermediate's absence.

**Baseline statement (for the convergence test):** Conditioning Intermediate is **work-capacity-forward** — energy-system conditioning is the majority of session emphasis; resistance is a conditioning-serving minority at sub-hypertrophic structure; the progressed variable is work-to-rest density; there is no muscle-growth or fat-loss goal. This is the fixed reference Body Recomposition Intermediate must prove it diverges from.

## Section 9 — Convergence Test for Body Recomposition Intermediate

**The question this test answers:** *Can Body Recomposition Intermediate be meaningfully different from Conditioning Intermediate despite identical metadata (both INT, 10wk, 4/wk, 40, GYM, Double Progression, identical Wk9 deload, same PAS §11.3 template)?*

The two programs' entire distinction rests on goal alignment — Conditioning Intermediate `IMPROVE_CONDITIONING` vs Body Recomposition Intermediate `LOSE_FAT, BUILD_MUSCLE`. That difference is real on paper; the Stage 1 burden is to prove it survives in **authored structure**. Body Recomposition Intermediate's Blueprint must demonstrate **all four** of the following, evaluated with the goal tag hidden:

| # | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Emphasis inversion** | The **majority** of training emphasis/volume is **resistance**, with conditioning serving caloric output — the inverse of Conditioning Intermediate's conditioning-forward majority (Section 4). |
| 2 | **Genuine hypertrophy-style resistance (honors BUILD_MUSCLE)** | Resistance prescribed for muscle retention/growth — moderate loads, hypertrophy rep ranges (~8–15), adequate inter-set rest on the resistance portion — **not** merely loaded conditioning circuits. Conditioning Intermediate carries no such requirement. |
| 3 | **Deficit-oriented conditioning (honors LOSE_FAT)** | Conditioning that serves caloric expenditure / body composition (volume sustainable in a deficit), **distinct from** the anaerobic-threshold, work-capacity-ceiling-pushing that defines Conditioning Intermediate. |
| 4 | **Non-derivability** | A reviewer shown both programs' authored sessions, goal tags hidden, can tell them apart. If Body Recomposition Intermediate reads as "Conditioning Intermediate with a different goal tag," it fails. |

**If Body Recomposition Intermediate fails any of the four** (especially #1 and #4), the pair has **not** survived authored structure, and it triggers the remediation paths named in `Conditioning-Family-Research-v1.0.md` Deliverable 7 / `Program-Ecosystem-Architecture-v1.0.md` §2.2: re-differentiate, merge, or retire-and-redirect (the Powerbuilding Intermediate precedent). **If it passes all four**, the goal-alignment distinction is demonstrated in content, not asserted, and the pair survives — the same bar Lower Body Intermediate cleared against Muscle Building Intermediate.

## Section 10 — Verdict

**Verdict: APPROVE Conditioning Intermediate (baseline established).**

Conditioning Intermediate is a clear, self-standing work-capacity program — the INTERMEDIATE continuation of the Athletic Conditioning ladder, distinct from its predecessor and not dependent on any sibling's absence. Its architecture (4-day conditioning rotation), progression (work-density compression + Double Progression on resistance), and recovery (work:rest lever, Wk9 deload) all sit inside the locked CONDITIONING standards (PAS-D7/D8/D9/D11, §10.2, §11.3).

**The pair is NOT declared resolved.** This pass establishes the baseline and the four-part falsifiable test; closing the convergence risk requires Body Recomposition Intermediate's own Blueprint to pass that test. **Body Recomposition Intermediate is not authored in this pass.**

**Confidence:** High for Conditioning Intermediate's own justification; the residual uncertainty is entirely on the Body Recomposition Intermediate side and is handed forward explicitly as a test rather than left implicit.

**Recommended next action:** author the **Body Recomposition Intermediate Blueprint** against Section 9's four-part test before declaring the Conditioning family's Intermediate pair resolved or the family's Stage 1 sequence complete.
