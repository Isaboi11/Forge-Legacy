## Section 1 — Half-Kneeling Cable Chop

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Half-Kneeling Cable Chop |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Half-Kneeling Cable Chop is a core exercise performed kneeling on one knee, pulling a cable handle diagonally across the body from high to low. It trains the core's ability to resist rotation while in a half-kneeling stance. The kneeling position removes the legs' ability to assist the movement.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Half-Kneeling Cable Chop builds anti-rotation core strength while the half-kneeling stance removes the legs' ability to generate or resist force, isolating the core's contribution more than a standing version. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the standing Pallof Press and want a more demanding anti-rotation challenge. It also reinforces the same core bracing used to keep the torso square during single-arm or single-leg lifts. It is one of the more demanding anti-rotation exercises in the catalog.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach a handle to a high cable pulley and kneel on one knee, sideways to the machine.
2. Grip the handle with both hands at shoulder height.
3. Brace your core and keep your hips square.
4. Pull the handle diagonally down and across your body.
5. Keep your hips from rotating as the handle travels down.
6. Return the handle back up under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core before you start pulling the handle down
> — Keep your hips square to the front throughout
> — Move slowly rather than rushing the chop
> — Pull with your core rather than just your arms

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips rotate toward the cable machine during the chop
> — The torso leans to one side to resist the pull
> — The movement turns into a yanking motion instead of a controlled chop

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Pallof Press
**Alternatives:** Resistance Band Anti-Rotation Hold

## Section 2 — Side Plank

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Side Plank |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, GLUTES |

**ABOUT**
> The Side Plank is a bodyweight core exercise performed holding a straight-line position on one forearm and the side of one foot. It trains the obliques and the core's ability to resist side bending. It is held statically rather than performed for repetitions.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Side Plank builds core strength specifically for resisting lateral bending, which a standard Plank does not train as directly. It is well suited for beginners building core stability in all directions, not just front to back. It also exposes and helps correct side-to-side core strength imbalances. It requires no equipment and can be performed anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your side with your forearm on the floor, elbow under your shoulder.
2. Stack your feet on top of each other.
3. Brace your core and lift your hips off the floor.
4. Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.
6. Lower down and switch sides.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips lifted in line with your shoulders
> — Stack your shoulders directly on top of each other
> — Brace your core throughout the entire hold
> — Breathe steadily rather than holding your breath

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips sag toward the floor as the hold continues
> — The top shoulder rotates forward instead of stacking over the bottom one
> — The hips drift forward or back instead of staying in a straight line

**Progressions:** Plank Reach-Through
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Bird Dog, Dead Bug

## Section 3 — Plank Reach-Through

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Plank Reach-Through |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Plank Reach-Through is a core exercise performed in a hands-and-knees or push-up plank position, reaching one arm underneath the body and across to the opposite side. It trains the core's ability to resist rotation as one arm moves. It is one of the more demanding anti-rotation bodyweight exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Plank Reach-Through builds anti-rotation core strength while also challenging shoulder stability on the supporting arm. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the Side Plank or Bird Dog and want a more dynamic anti-rotation challenge. It also reinforces the same core bracing used to keep the torso square during single-arm pressing or pulling movements. It requires no equipment beyond a flat surface.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Start in a push-up plank position with your body in a straight line.
2. Brace your core and keep your hips level.
3. Reach one arm underneath your body and across to the opposite side.
4. Rotate only as far as your hips stay level.
5. Return your arm back to the starting position.
6. Repeat on the other side.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your hips level throughout the entire reach
> — Move slowly rather than rushing the reach-through
> — Keep your supporting arm stable as your other arm reaches
> — Brace your core before each reach

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips rotate or dip as the arm reaches across
> — The supporting shoulder collapses to allow more rotation
> — The reach is rushed instead of controlled

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Bird Dog
**Alternatives:** Side Plank

## Section 4 — Resistance Band Anti-Rotation Hold

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Resistance Band Anti-Rotation Hold |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ANTI_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | RESISTANCE_BAND |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Resistance Band Anti-Rotation Hold is a core exercise performed standing, holding a resistance band handle out from the chest while resisting the band's pull to one side. It trains the core's ability to resist rotation. It requires only a resistance band anchored at chest height.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Resistance Band Anti-Rotation Hold builds anti-rotation core strength using the lightest equipment of any anti-rotation exercise in the library. It is well suited as an accessible entry point before progressing to the cable-based Pallof Press. It also allows easy resistance adjustment by changing the band or standing distance. It is a useful option for athletes without access to a cable machine.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Anchor a resistance band at chest height and stand sideways to it.
2. Hold the handle with both hands at your chest.
3. Step away from the anchor to create tension in the band.
4. Brace your core to resist the band pulling you sideways.
5. Hold the position while breathing steadily.
6. Turn around and repeat facing the other direction.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core before you settle into the hold
> — Keep your hips and shoulders square to the front
> — Resist the pull with your core rather than your arms
> — Breathe steadily throughout the hold

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso rotates toward the anchor point
> — The hips shift sideways to resist the pull instead of the core bracing
> — The arms tense up rather than the core doing the work

**Progressions:** Pallof Press
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Half-Kneeling Cable Chop

## Section 5 — Crunch

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Crunch |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_FLEXION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Crunch is a core flexion exercise performed lying on the back, curling the shoulders up off the floor while the lower back stays in contact with it. It trains the core through a short range of spinal flexion. It is performed with the feet flat on the floor or knees bent.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Crunch builds basic core-flexion strength through a shorter, more controlled range than a full Sit-Up. It is well suited as the most basic core-flexion exercise for beginners or anyone managing a lower-back limitation. It also serves as a foundation before progressing to the larger range of motion in a Sit-Up. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat on the floor.
2. Place your hands lightly behind your head or crossed over your chest.
3. Brace your core and curl your shoulders up off the floor.
4. Keep your lower back in contact with the floor throughout.
5. Lower your shoulders back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Lead with your chest rather than pulling with your neck
> — Keep your lower back pressed into the floor throughout
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than using momentum
> — Exhale as you curl your shoulders up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hands pull on the neck to help lift the shoulders
> — The lower back lifts off the floor during the curl
> — The movement uses a jerking motion instead of a controlled curl

**Progressions:** Sit-Up
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Reverse Crunch, Cable Crunch

## Section 6 — Cable Crunch

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Cable Crunch |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_FLEXION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | CABLE_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Cable Crunch is a core flexion exercise performed kneeling at a high cable pulley, curling the torso down toward the floor against cable resistance. It trains the core under constant load. It is performed kneeling, facing the cable machine.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Cable Crunch builds core-flexion strength under constant resistance, unlike bodyweight crunches and sit-ups. It is well suited for lifters who have built a base of bodyweight core strength and want to add external load. It also allows precise load adjustment, making it accessible across a wide range of training levels. It is a common accessory exercise in core-focused hypertrophy programs.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach a rope to a high cable pulley and kneel facing the machine.
2. Hold the rope ends at the sides of your head.
3. Brace your core and curl your torso down toward your knees.
4. Keep your hips still as your torso curls.
5. Squeeze your core at the bottom of the curl.
6. Return your torso back up under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Curl from your core rather than pulling with your arms
> — Keep your hips still throughout the movement
> — Round your spine as you curl down rather than just bending forward
> — Control the return rather than letting the cable pull you back up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The arms pull the rope down instead of the core curling
> — The hips shift backward to help the torso curl
> — The range of motion shortens, stopping short of a full curl

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern cable variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Sit-Up, Crunch

## Section 7 — Reverse Crunch

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Reverse Crunch |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_FLEXION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |

**ABOUT**
> The Reverse Crunch is a core flexion exercise performed lying on the back, curling the hips and knees up toward the chest. It trains the lower portion of the core through a reversed flexion pattern. It is performed with the upper body staying relatively still.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Reverse Crunch builds lower-core strength by curling the hips toward the chest rather than the chest toward the hips, training the core from the opposite direction of a standard Crunch. It is well suited for lifters wanting to balance upper- and lower-core training. It also serves as a useful regression for athletes building toward the Hanging Leg Raise. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet off the floor.
2. Rest your arms at your sides or lightly under your lower back.
3. Brace your core and curl your knees up toward your chest.
4. Lift your hips slightly off the floor at the top of the curl.
5. Lower your hips and knees back down under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Curl your hips up rather than just bending your knees
> — Keep your upper body relaxed and still
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than using momentum
> — Exhale as you curl your hips up

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The legs swing using momentum instead of a controlled curl
> — The lower back arches as the hips lower back down
> — The upper body rocks to help generate momentum

**Progressions:** Hanging Leg Raise
**Regressions:** Crunch
**Alternatives:** Sit-Up

## Section 8 — Standing Trunk Rotation

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Standing Trunk Rotation |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | RESISTANCE_BAND |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | None |

**ABOUT**
> The Standing Trunk Rotation is a core rotation exercise performed standing, rotating the torso against the resistance of a band anchored to one side. It trains the obliques and deep core rotators. It is performed with the hips facing forward as the torso rotates.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Standing Trunk Rotation builds rotational core strength using the lightest equipment of any rotation exercise in the library, making it practical for home training or travel. It is well suited as an accessible entry point before progressing to the cable-based Cable Woodchop. It also allows easy resistance adjustment by changing the band or standing distance. It carries over to throwing, swinging, and other rotational movements in sport.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Anchor a resistance band at chest height and stand sideways to it.
2. Hold the handle with both hands at your chest.
3. Step away from the anchor to create tension in the band.
4. Brace your core and rotate your torso away from the anchor point.
5. Rotate back through center under control.
6. Repeat for the set, then face the other direction.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Rotate from your torso rather than just swinging your arms
> — Keep your hips facing forward throughout the rotation
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than rushing the twist
> — Brace your core before starting each rotation

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hips rotate along with the torso instead of staying forward
> — The movement turns into a yanking motion instead of a controlled rotation
> — The arms swing instead of the torso driving the movement

**Progressions:** Cable Woodchop
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Russian Twist, Bicycle Crunch

## Section 9 — Bicycle Crunch

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Bicycle Crunch |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BODYWEIGHT |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HIP_FLEXORS |

**ABOUT**
> The Bicycle Crunch is a core rotation exercise performed lying on the back, bringing one elbow toward the opposite knee while extending the other leg. It trains the obliques and deep core rotators. It is performed with a continuous pedaling-style alternation between sides.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Bicycle Crunch builds rotational core strength using only body weight, making it the most accessible rotation exercise in the library. It is well suited for beginners building basic oblique strength before adding external resistance. It also serves as a useful regression for athletes building toward the Russian Twist. It requires no equipment and can be performed almost anywhere.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Lie on your back with your hands lightly behind your head.
2. Lift your shoulders and legs slightly off the floor.
3. Bring one knee toward your chest while rotating the opposite elbow to meet it.
4. Extend your other leg out straight as you rotate.
5. Switch sides in a continuous pedaling motion.
6. Keep your lower back pressed into the floor throughout.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Rotate your torso rather than just pulling with your elbow
> — Keep your lower back pressed into the floor throughout
> — Move at a controlled pace rather than rushing the pedaling motion
> — Exhale as your elbow and knee meet

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The hands pull on the neck to help rotate the torso
> — The lower back arches off the floor during the movement
> — The pace rushes instead of staying controlled

**Progressions:** Russian Twist
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Standing Trunk Rotation

## Section 10 — Medicine Ball Rotational Throw

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Medicine Ball Rotational Throw |
| Category | CORE |
| Movement Pattern | CORE_ROTATION |
| Equipment Tag(s) | WEIGHT_PLATE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS |

**ABOUT**
> The Medicine Ball Rotational Throw is a core rotation exercise performed standing sideways to a wall, rotating the torso and throwing a weighted ball against it. It trains the obliques and deep core rotators through an explosive, powerful rotation. It is one of the most advanced core-rotation exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Medicine Ball Rotational Throw builds explosive rotational power that carries over directly to throwing, swinging, and striking movements in sport. It trains the core to produce force quickly rather than only resist it. It is well suited for athletes who have already built a base of rotational core strength on the Russian Twist or Cable Woodchop. It is a strong benchmark exercise for advanced, power-focused core training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand sideways to a sturdy wall, a few feet away, holding a weighted ball.
2. Rotate your torso away from the wall to load the throw.
3. Brace your core and rotate explosively back toward the wall.
4. Release the ball forcefully into the wall at the end of the rotation.
5. Catch the ball as it bounces back.
6. Reset and repeat for the set, then face the other direction.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Rotate through your hips and torso together
> — Load the rotation fully before throwing
> — Release the ball with full force rather than holding back
> — Keep your feet stable throughout the throw

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The throw uses only the arms instead of the full rotation
> — The feet shift or step instead of staying stable
> — The ball is released without fully loading the rotation first

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Russian Twist
**Alternatives:** Cable Woodchop
