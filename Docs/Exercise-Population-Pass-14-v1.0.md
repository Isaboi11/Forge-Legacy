## Section 1 — Front-Loaded Carry

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Front-Loaded Carry |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | CARRY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | KETTLEBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE, SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | FOREARMS |

**ABOUT**
> The Front-Loaded Carry is a loaded carry exercise performed by holding one or two kettlebells at the chest in a front-rack position and walking a fixed distance. It trains the core, upper back, and total-body stability under an anteriorly loaded position. It uses one or two kettlebells racked against the forearms.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Front-Loaded Carry builds core and upper-back strength specifically for resisting forward-leaning posture under load. It carries over to real-world tasks where weight is held close to the chest, such as carrying a heavy box. It is well suited for athletes and lifters who want to add an anti-flexion core challenge to their carry training. It also reinforces the same front-rack position used in front squats and cleans.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Clean or lift a kettlebell into a front-rack position against your forearm.
2. Stand tall with your elbow tucked close to your body.
3. Brace your core and pull your shoulders back.
4. Walk forward in a straight line with controlled steps.
5. Keep the kettlebell stable against your forearm throughout.
6. Set the kettlebell down with control at the end of the distance.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your elbow tucked close to your body throughout
> — Brace your core with every step
> — Keep your chest tall rather than leaning forward
> — Take controlled, even steps rather than rushing

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans forward under the front-loaded weight
> — The elbow drifts away from the body, destabilizing the kettlebell
> — The pace speeds up and steps become uneven near the end

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Suitcase Carry
**Alternatives:** Farmer Carry, Trap Bar Carry

## Section 2 — Overhead Carry

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Overhead Carry |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | CARRY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | TRICEPS |

**ABOUT**
> The Overhead Carry is a loaded carry exercise performed by holding a dumbbell locked out overhead and walking a fixed distance. It trains shoulder stability and total-body bracing under an overhead load. It can be performed with one or two dumbbells.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Overhead Carry builds shoulder stability and core bracing under a uniquely demanding overhead position. It carries over to any activity requiring stable overhead control, such as lifting objects onto shelves. It is well suited for lifters who have mastered the Suitcase Carry and want a more advanced shoulder-stability challenge. It also reinforces the same overhead lockout position used in pressing movements.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Press a dumbbell overhead until your arm is fully locked out.
2. Brace your core and pull your shoulder down away from your ear.
3. Walk forward in a straight line with controlled steps.
4. Keep the dumbbell directly above your shoulder throughout.
5. Set the dumbbell down with control at the end of the distance.
6. Switch arms and repeat if performing this one side at a time.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the dumbbell stacked directly over your shoulder
> — Brace your core with every step
> — Keep your arm fully locked out throughout the walk
> — Take controlled, even steps rather than rushing

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The dumbbell drifts forward or behind the shoulder
> — The shoulder shrugs up toward the ear instead of staying packed down
> — The torso leans away from the loaded side to compensate

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Suitcase Carry
**Alternatives:** Farmer Carry

## Section 3 — Sled Push

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Sled Push |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | CARRY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | SLED |
| Primary Muscle(s) | QUADS, GLUTES |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | CALVES, CORE |

**ABOUT**
> The Sled Push is a loaded conditioning exercise performed by driving a weighted sled forward across the floor. It trains the legs, glutes, and core through sustained forward force production. It is performed by pushing the sled's handles while leaning into the drive.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Sled Push builds lower-body strength and conditioning together, since the sustained driving effort combines strength output with a significant cardiovascular demand. It carries over to any sport requiring forward driving force, such as blocking or accelerating. It is well suited for athletes and lifters wanting a low-impact way to build both strength and conditioning. It also places minimal load on the spine compared to many other loaded exercises.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Grip the sled handles and position your body in a forward lean.
2. Brace your core and set your feet under your hips.
3. Drive through your legs to push the sled forward.
4. Take quick, driving steps while maintaining the forward lean.
5. Continue pushing for the prescribed distance.
6. Stand up and reset before the next set.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your lean angle consistent throughout the push
> — Drive through the balls of your feet with each step
> — Keep your core braced to maintain the lean
> — Take quick, choppy steps rather than long strides

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso stands up too tall, losing the driving lean
> — The steps become long and uneven instead of quick and choppy
> — The lower back rounds to compensate for fatigue

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern sled variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Sled Drag
**Alternatives:** Farmer Carry

## Section 4 — Sled Drag

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Sled Drag |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | CARRY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | SLED |
| Primary Muscle(s) | QUADS, GLUTES |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HAMSTRINGS, CORE |

**ABOUT**
> The Sled Drag is a loaded conditioning exercise performed by pulling a weighted sled across the floor using a strap or rope. It trains the legs, glutes, and back through sustained pulling force production. It can be performed walking forward or backward.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Sled Drag builds lower-body and back strength alongside conditioning, since the sustained pulling effort combines strength output with a significant cardiovascular demand. It carries over to any sport requiring pulling or backward movement, such as wrestling or tackling. It is well suited for athletes and lifters wanting a joint-friendly way to build both strength and conditioning. It also places minimal load on the spine compared to many other loaded exercises.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Attach a strap or rope from the sled to your body or hands.
2. Brace your core and set your feet under your hips.
3. Drive through your legs to begin moving the sled.
4. Take controlled, even steps while maintaining tension on the strap.
5. Continue dragging for the prescribed distance.
6. Stand up tall and reset before the next set.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep steady tension on the strap throughout
> — Drive through your whole foot with each step
> — Keep your core braced as you move
> — Take even steps rather than rushing or stalling

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The tension on the strap goes slack between steps
> — The torso hunches forward instead of staying tall
> — The steps become uneven or rushed near the end

**Progressions:** Sled Push
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern sled variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Farmer Carry

## Section 5 — Yoke Carry

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Yoke Carry |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | CARRY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | WEIGHT_PLATE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE, GLUTES |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | QUADS |

**ABOUT**
> The Yoke Carry is a loaded carry exercise performed by walking forward under a weighted yoke frame resting across the shoulders. It trains the legs, core, and upper back under the heaviest load of any carry exercise in the catalog. It requires a dedicated yoke frame loaded with weight plates.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Yoke Carry builds total-body strength under the heaviest relative load of any carry exercise in the library, since the frame allows much more weight than handheld implements. It carries over to strongman-style events and any sport requiring maximal force production while moving. It is well suited only for athletes who have already built a strength base with the Farmer Carry. It is one of the most demanding exercises in the entire catalog.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Position yourself under the loaded yoke frame.
2. Stand up to lift the frame off its supports, keeping your back flat.
3. Brace your core hard before taking your first step.
4. Walk forward in short, controlled steps.
5. Keep your torso upright throughout the walk.
6. Lower the frame back onto its supports at the end of the distance.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Brace your core hard before every step
> — Take short, controlled steps rather than long strides
> — Keep your torso upright under the load
> — Set the frame down with control rather than dropping it

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The torso leans forward under the heavy load
> — The steps become long and unstable instead of short and controlled
> — The frame is dropped instead of set down under control

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Farmer Carry
**Alternatives:** Trap Bar Carry

## Section 6 — Trap Bar Carry

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Trap Bar Carry |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | CARRY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | DUMBBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | FOREARMS, CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | GLUTES, QUADS |

**ABOUT**
> The Trap Bar Carry is a loaded carry exercise performed by lifting a hexagonal trap bar and walking a fixed distance. It trains the grip, core, and total-body stability under a centered, heavier load than dumbbells typically allow. It positions the load directly at the sides of the body rather than out in front.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Trap Bar Carry builds grip and total-body strength using a centered load position that some lifters find more comfortable than dumbbells or kettlebells. It carries over to everyday lifting and carrying tasks, much like the Farmer Carry. It is well suited for lifters with access to a trap bar who want a heavier-loading carry option. It also serves as an efficient conditioning tool since it challenges the whole body at once.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand inside the trap bar with your shins close to it.
2. Bend at your hips and knees to grip the handles.
3. Drive through your feet to stand up, keeping your back flat.
4. Stand tall with the bar at your sides.
5. Walk forward with controlled, even steps.
6. Set the bar down with control at the end of the distance.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep your back flat as you stand up with the bar
> — Keep your shoulders pulled back and chest tall
> — Take controlled, even steps rather than rushing
> — Brace your core with every step

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The lower back rounds as the bar leaves the floor
> — The shoulders round forward as the walk continues
> — The pace speeds up and steps become uneven near the end

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Farmer Carry, Suitcase Carry

## Section 7 — Medicine Ball Slam

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Medicine Ball Slam |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | EXPLOSIVE_FULLBODY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | WEIGHT_PLATE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | CORE, SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | LOWER_BACK |

**ABOUT**
> The Medicine Ball Slam is an explosive full-body exercise performed by lifting a weighted ball overhead and slamming it forcefully into the floor. It trains the core, shoulders, and legs through a fast, powerful extension-to-flexion movement. It combines strength and conditioning in a single explosive action.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Medicine Ball Slam builds explosive power through the core and shoulders while also providing a significant conditioning demand. It is a useful outlet for training power output without any eccentric loading, since the ball is simply released. It is well suited for athletes and lifters wanting a high-intensity, low-skill explosive exercise. It also serves as a satisfying way to train hard effort safely.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, holding a weighted ball.
2. Lift the ball overhead, extending your whole body.
3. Brace your core and slam the ball down into the floor with full force.
4. Bend your knees and hips slightly as you follow through.
5. Pick the ball back up and reset your stance.
6. Repeat for the set.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Extend fully overhead before initiating the slam
> — Slam with your whole body, not just your arms
> — Bend your knees slightly to absorb the follow-through
> — Reset your stance fully between reps

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The slam comes from the arms alone instead of the whole body
> — The lower back rounds aggressively to pick the ball up
> — The pace rushes instead of resetting fully between reps

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Burpee
**Alternatives:** Battle Ropes, Wall Ball

## Section 8 — Battle Ropes

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Battle Ropes |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | EXPLOSIVE_FULLBODY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BATTLE_ROPES |
| Primary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, CORE |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | FOREARMS |

**ABOUT**
> Battle Ropes is an explosive conditioning exercise performed by whipping the ends of heavy ropes in alternating or simultaneous waves. It trains the shoulders, arms, and core through sustained, fast upper-body movement. It is the only rope-based exercise in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> Battle Ropes builds upper-body conditioning and shoulder endurance through sustained, fast movement rather than a single explosive effort. It is well suited as a high-intensity finisher or conditioning station in a circuit. It also offers a joint-friendly way to elevate heart rate without any impact on the lower body. It requires only a set of battle ropes anchored at one end.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, holding one end of each rope.
2. Bend your knees slightly and brace your core.
3. Whip your arms up and down to create waves in the ropes.
4. Keep the waves traveling continuously down the length of the ropes.
5. Maintain a steady, sustainable pace for the duration of the set.
6. Slow down and stop under control at the end of the set.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep a slight bend in your knees throughout the set
> — Drive the waves from your shoulders rather than just your wrists
> — Keep your core braced to stay stable
> — Maintain a steady rhythm rather than starting too fast

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The waves slow down and lose rhythm as fatigue builds
> — The lower back rounds to compensate for tired shoulders
> — The knees lock out, removing the slight athletic bend

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Medicine Ball Slam, Rowing Machine Intervals

## Section 9 — Rowing Machine Intervals

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Rowing Machine Intervals |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | EXPLOSIVE_FULLBODY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | ROWING_MACHINE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | BACK, QUADS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | HAMSTRINGS, CORE |

**ABOUT**
> Rowing Machine Intervals is a conditioning exercise performed on a rowing machine, alternating between hard efforts and easier recovery periods. It trains the legs, back, and arms through a repeated full-body pulling motion. It is the only rowing-machine-based exercise in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> Rowing Machine Intervals builds full-body conditioning while training the same pulling pattern used in the Barbell Row and Dumbbell Row, but at speed and under sustained effort. It is well suited as a low-impact conditioning option that still trains pulling musculature. It also offers a structured way to build cardiovascular capacity without running. It requires only a rowing machine.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Sit on the rowing machine with your feet secured in the straps.
2. Grip the handle and extend your legs to begin the row.
3. Pull the handle toward your torso as your legs finish extending.
4. Extend your arms and bend your knees to return to the starting position.
5. Row hard for the prescribed interval, then row easily during recovery.
6. Repeat the hard and easy intervals for the set.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Drive with your legs first, then pull with your arms
> — Keep your back flat throughout each stroke
> — Match your effort to the prescribed interval, hard or easy
> — Keep a consistent rhythm rather than rushing the stroke

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The arms pull before the legs finish driving
> — The lower back rounds during the pulling phase
> — The hard intervals are paced the same as the easy ones

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** None — no easier same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Alternatives:** Battle Ropes

## Section 10 — Clean and Press

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Clean and Press |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | EXPLOSIVE_FULLBODY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | BARBELL |
| Primary Muscle(s) | FULL_BODY |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | SHOULDERS, GLUTES |

**ABOUT**
> The Clean and Press is an explosive full-body exercise performed by pulling a barbell from the floor to the shoulders in one motion, then pressing it overhead. It trains the legs, back, shoulders, and core together in a single demanding sequence. It is one of the most technically advanced exercises in the catalog.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Clean and Press builds total-body explosive power and coordination by combining a hip-hinge pull with an overhead press in one continuous movement. It carries over to any sport requiring rapid force production from the ground up through the whole body. It is well suited only for athletes who have already built strength and technique across squatting, hinging, and pressing patterns. It is one of the most demanding and rewarding benchmark lifts in the entire catalog.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, the bar over the middle of your feet.
2. Hinge down and grip the bar just outside your shins.
3. Drive through your feet to pull the bar explosively off the floor.
4. Pull the bar up to your shoulders, rotating your elbows underneath it.
5. Stand fully upright, then press the bar overhead to full arm extension.
6. Lower the bar back to the floor under control.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Keep the bar close to your body throughout the pull
> — Drive through your feet before pulling with your arms
> — Catch the bar on your shoulders with your elbows up and forward
> — Brace your core hard before the overhead press

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The bar drifts away from the body during the pull
> — The lower back rounds as the bar leaves the floor
> — The overhead press begins before the legs and hips are fully locked out

**Progressions:** None — no harder same-pattern variant exists in the launch catalog for this exercise.
**Regressions:** Turkish Get-Up
**Alternatives:** Wall Ball

## Section 11 — Wall Ball

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Canonical Name | Wall Ball |
| Category | FULL_BODY |
| Movement Pattern | EXPLOSIVE_FULLBODY |
| Equipment Tag(s) | WEIGHT_PLATE |
| Primary Muscle(s) | QUADS, SHOULDERS |
| Secondary Muscle(s) | GLUTES, CORE |

**ABOUT**
> The Wall Ball is an explosive full-body exercise performed by squatting down with a weighted ball and standing up to throw it against a wall target. It trains the legs, shoulders, and core through a combined squat-and-throw movement. It combines strength and conditioning in a single explosive action.

**WHY IT MATTERS**
> The Wall Ball builds explosive lower-body power and shoulder coordination in one continuous movement, training the legs to drive the throw rather than the arms alone. It is a common conditioning benchmark exercise that also reinforces squat depth and timing. It is well suited for athletes who have mastered the Burpee and want a more loaded explosive full-body challenge. It pairs strength and conditioning efficiently in circuit-style training.

**HOW TO DO IT**
1. Stand holding a weighted ball at chest height, feet shoulder-width apart.
2. Lower into a squat, keeping the ball at your chest.
3. Drive through your feet to stand up explosively.
4. Extend your arms to throw the ball at a target on the wall.
5. Catch the ball as it returns and absorb it back into the squat.
6. Repeat the squat-and-throw sequence for the set.

**COACHING CUES**
> — Drive the throw with your legs rather than just your arms
> — Catch the ball softly and flow directly back into the squat
> — Keep your chest tall throughout the squat portion
> — Aim for a consistent target height on the wall

**WATCH OUT FOR**
> — The throw comes from the arms alone instead of the legs driving it
> — The squat depth shortens as fatigue builds
> — The ball is caught stiffly instead of absorbed into the next squat

**Progressions:** Clean and Press
**Regressions:** Burpee
**Alternatives:** Medicine Ball Slam
